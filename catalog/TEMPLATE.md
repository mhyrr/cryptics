# Full Title

## What it is
Two to five sentences. Author, date, form, physical description, where it is now.

## What is unsolved
State the open question in one sentence, then expand. Be exact: "the meaning
of the 87 emblems" is a different problem from "the identity of the author."

## What survives
Witnesses, editions, digitization, transcriptions. Name the best transcription
and whether it is machine-readable.

## Prior attempts and current consensus
What capable people have tried, what the field currently believes, and where
the dispute sits. Name the two or three treatments a newcomer must read.

## What a solution would have to do
The falsifiability clause. What would convince the field? What would a
proposed solution need to predict or explain? This is the section that keeps
us from becoming one more crank.

## Why the scores
One line per axis. Say what drove the number, especially when it could have
gone the other way.

## Sources
Tier each: PRIMARY (the object, a facsimile, a critical edition), SCHOLARLY
(peer-reviewed or academic press), SECONDARY (encyclopedic), CLAIMANT (a
proposed solution written by its proposer), POPULAR (journalism, blogs).

- PRIMARY — Title. URL
- SCHOLARLY — Author, Title (Year). URL
- SECONDARY — ...

## Unverified claims
Anything above that you could not source. Never delete a claim to make this
section empty; move it here instead.
