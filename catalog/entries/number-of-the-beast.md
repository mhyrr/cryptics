# The number of the beast (Revelation 13:18): 666 / 616

## What it is
A one-line puzzle set by its author. Revelation 13:18 tells the reader that wisdom is required, that he should "calculate the number of the beast," and that the number is that of a man. The number is 666 in the majority of witnesses and 616 in others. It is included here as a **calibration case**: a text that is popularly believed to be unsolved, and for which a scholarly solution exists and is broadly held. The rubric keeps such cases on purpose, and this one is useful because the mechanism by which the field converged is unusually clean.

## What is unsolved
Almost nothing, on the main question, and the entry records that rather than manufacturing a mystery. There is a broad consensus in contemporary scholarship that the number is linked by gematria to the Roman emperor Nero: the Hebrew/Aramaic letters of *Nron Qsr* (נרון קסר), the Greek form of the name transliterated into Hebrew characters, sum to 666 — nun 50 + resh 200 + vav 6 + nun 50 + qoph 100 + samekh 60 + resh 200 — while the Latin form *Nro Qsr* (נרו קסר), dropping one nun, sums to 616. The explanation dates from the 1830s, with Fritzsche and Benary. What remains genuinely open is narrower and worth stating exactly: (1) why a Greek-writing author expected a Greek-reading audience in Asia to compute a Hebrew-letter gematria; (2) whether the number carries additional designed structure alongside the gematria, such as its being the 36th triangular number, or the sixfold repetition signifying incompleteness against seven's perfection; (3) whether the reference is to Nero himself or to a Nero typology applied to a later emperor, which is how the Domitian proposals survive; (4) which reading is original, 666 or 616 — the textual question, which is separate from the identification.

## What survives
The verse in a large and well-studied manuscript tradition, all of it digitized by the major New Testament imaging projects. For 616: Papyrus 115, described as the oldest manuscript of Revelation 13 known as of 2017; Codex Ephraemi Rescriptus (C); the Latin version of Tyconius; the ancient Armenian version. For 666: Codex Alexandrinus, the Byzantine tradition, and most later Greek manuscripts. The key evidential point is that the variant is not noise: 616 is what you get if you transliterate the Latin form of the name rather than the Greek one, so the two readings are two spellings of the same solution. That is the strongest single argument for the Nero identification, and it is an argument from the textual apparatus rather than from historical plausibility.

## Prior attempts and current consensus
Irenaeus, in the second century, already did not know the answer: he canvassed Euanthas, Teitan and Lateinos, and concluded that it was "more certain, and less hazardous, to await the fulfilment of the prophecy, than to be making surmises." From the 1830s the Nero gematria became the standard critical explanation and it now dominates contemporary scholarship. Competing or supplementary proposals still in print: the triangular-number structure; the symbolic reading of six as incompleteness; Domitian via a Nero typology; the 666 talents of gold in 1 Kings 10:14 as a literary source. The residual scholarly caution is real — one 2020 treatment concludes that "if Irenaeus was unclear about 666 already in the second century, the twenty-first century can be no more certain" — but it is caution about certainty, not a competing identification with comparable support.

## What a solution would have to do
Stated here for calibration, as the bar the accepted solution already clears and that rivals do not. A proposal must (1) yield the number from a name by a gematria system attested in the first century, not invented for the purpose; (2) explain *both* attested numbers, 666 and 616, from the same referent — this is the discriminating requirement, and it is the one the Nero solution meets and the Domitian, Lateinos and modern candidates do not; (3) identify a figure who fits Revelation's description of the beast and its historical setting in Roman Asia; (4) not depend on a spelling or a transliteration chosen only because it produces the target number, which is the standard failure mode of the popular candidates and can be checked by enumerating how many plausible names and titles sum to 666 in Hebrew, Greek and Latin — a finite computation whose largeness is precisely why arbitrary gematria "solutions" are worthless; (5) be consistent with the author's own instruction that the calculation is possible for his readers. Any future challenge to the Nero solution has to beat requirement (2), which is a high bar.

## Why the scores
- mystery 2: a working consensus exists with real minority dissent about typology and about which number is original. Not 1, because the mechanism by which a Greek audience was to do a Hebrew calculation is a genuine unsettled question, and not 3, because no rival identification has comparable support.
- material 4: a secure text apart from the one variant, and the variant is itself the decisive evidence; all witnesses digitized and collated. Not 5 because the puzzle is one sentence long and its cultural context has to be supplied from outside.
- solvable 5: the author states that the number encodes a man's name and instructs the reader to compute it. Designed meaning is not in doubt.
- compute 2: the gematria search space is trivially enumerable and enumerating it is useful mainly as a crank filter, which is a small job. There is nothing left here for computation to decide. Worth noting for calibration: on this axis the entry would have scored no higher *before* the solution, which is a limitation of the axis, not of the case.
- verifiable 4: the 616 variant is an independent anomaly that the solution explains without adjustment, which is the rubric's "explains multiple independent anomalies" criterion. Not 5 because there is no mechanical decryption, only a convergence.
- crowding 1: thousands of attempts, an enormous popular and apocalyptic literature, and every naive gematria exhausted.
