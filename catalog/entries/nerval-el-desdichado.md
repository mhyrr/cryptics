# Nerval, El Desdichado (from Les Chimères)

## What it is
A fourteen-line sonnet by Gérard de Nerval, first printed in Alexandre Dumas's paper *Le Mousquetaire* in December 1853 and then as the opening poem of *Les Chimères*, the group of twelve sonnets appended to *Les Filles du feu* (1854). Its title is Spanish for "the disinherited" and is taken from the device on the shield of the unknown knight in Walter Scott's *Ivanhoe*. In fourteen lines it names or alludes to a "ténébreux," a "veuf," an "inconsolé," the "Prince d'Aquitaine à la tour abolie," a dead star and a black sun, Posilipo and Italy, Lusignan and Biron, Amour and Phébus, the queen, the siren's grotto, Orpheus's lyre, and the "soupirs de la sainte" against the "cris de la fée." It is among the most heavily commented short poems in French.

## What is unsolved
Which determinate persons, places and events the proper names and epithets designate, and whether they designate anything outside the poem. Nerval wrote the sonnets during and after the psychotic episodes documented in *Aurélia*, and in the dedicatory letter to Dumas prefacing *Les Filles du feu* he said of them that they "would lose their charm by being explained" — a statement that concedes there is something to explain while refusing to do it. The specific cruxes are the identity of the "Prince d'Aquitaine," the referent of "la tour abolie," why Lusignan (a lineage tied to the fairy Mélusine) and Biron are paired, who "la Reine" is, and how the "deux fois vainqueur" crossing of Acheron relates to Orpheus.

## What survives
The 1853 newspaper printing, the 1854 volume, and autograph material including the copy Nerval annotated for a recipient, on which some marginal identifications are reported. The standard critical text is the Pléiade *Œuvres complètes* edited by Jean Guillaume and Claude Pichois, which prints the variants; the text is also in Wikisource. Nerval's own prose — *Aurélia*, *Voyage en Orient*, *Les Faux Saulniers*, the letters, the Valois genealogical material he collected — constitutes a large, digitized, machine-readable corpus in which almost every name in the sonnet recurs, and is the main untapped evidence base.

## Prior attempts and current consensus
Three traditions. The esoteric-biographical, above all Jean Richer's work on Nerval's astrological, tarot and initiatic sources, reads the sonnets as an encoded personal cosmology; it is respected for its erudition and criticised for its unfalsifiability. The phenomenological and thematic tradition — Georges Poulet, Jean-Pierre Richard, Michel Jeanneret — reads the poem as the record of a subject's dissolution and does not look for a key. The structural tradition, above all Jacques Geninasca's analyses of *Les Chimères*, treats the poem as a self-contained system of oppositions, which is the reading that makes the fewest external commitments. The consensus is that some referents are securely identified (Nerval's own claimed Valois descent, the Lusignan–Mélusine link, the Orpheus figure, the Naples material shared with "Myrtho" and "Delfica"), that others are not, and that no global decoding is accepted. The French Wikipedia account of *Les Chimères* records Nerval's refusal to explain and notes that "El Desdichado" has generated hundreds of distinct readings.

## What a solution would have to do
Because the poem is short and allusive, a solution must be judged on breadth and on independence of evidence. It would have to: (a) assign a referent to every proper name and to each of "la tour abolie," "la Reine," "la sainte" and "la fée," rather than to the two or three that are easy; (b) ground each assignment in Nerval's own documented reading or writing — a passage in *Aurélia*, a letter, a genealogical note, a source he is known to have used — not in general Renaissance or occult lore; (c) explain the sonnet's place in the sequence of twelve, since *Les Chimères* was assembled as a set and shares vocabulary across poems; (d) be consistent with the autograph annotations, which are the only near-authorial glosses available; and (e) account for Nerval's refusal in the Dumas letter, either by showing the refusal was rhetorical or by explaining why a decodable poem would be said to lose its charm on decoding. A concrete, checkable move that appears not to have been done exhaustively: a full concordance and collocation study of every lexical item of the twelve sonnets against the whole Nerval corpus, which would show which of the competing referents Nerval himself associates with each term.

## Why the scores
- mystery 3: partly resolved — several referents are agreed — with a large and famous residue; a demonstrated global reading would matter to nineteenth-century French studies but not beyond.
- material 4: the printings, variants and an annotated autograph survive, with a critical edition and a fully digitized authorial corpus for comparison; short of 5 because the annotations' status is not settled.
- solvable 3: Nerval's own "would lose their charm by being explained" implies determinate content, but the *Aurélia* context makes irreducible private reference a serious possibility; both positions are held by serious scholars.
- compute 3: a corpus concordance across Nerval's complete works is a genuine and apparently incomplete computational task that would constrain the referents; it cannot decide the poem.
- verifiable 2: competing readings would survive any such study; only the autograph glosses could raise this.
- crowding 1: an immense critical literature, including famous structuralist set-pieces; every obvious approach has been tried.

## Unverified claims
- The December 1853 *Le Mousquetaire* printing date was not verified against the newspaper.
- The existence, contents and location of the autograph bearing Nerval's marginal identifications (commonly called the "manuscrit Éluard," after the collection of Paul Éluard) could not be verified in this pass; the French Wikipedia article on *Les Chimères* does not mention it. Everything said above about "the autograph annotations" rests on that unverified report.
- The derivation of the title from *Ivanhoe* is standard but was not verified against Scott's text in this pass.
- Michel Pastoureau's heraldic reading is mentioned in the secondary source but was not examined.
- Whether a full corpus concordance of *Les Chimères* against Nerval's complete works has been published is unknown to this entry.
