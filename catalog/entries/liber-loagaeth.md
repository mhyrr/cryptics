# Liber Loagaeth (Dee & Kelley's Enochian letter-tables, Sloane MS 3189)

## What it is
The "Book of Speech from God": a manuscript of very large letter-tables received by John Dee and Edward Kelley in scrying sessions beginning in March 1583, in which Kelley reported letters dictated one at a time by angels and Dee wrote them into 49-row grids. The surviving fair copy is British Library Sloane MS 3189, with related material in Sloane MS 3188 (the *Five Books of Mystery* diary, 22 Dec 1581 – 23 May 1583) and Cotton MS Appendix XLVI. The associated but textually distinct *Claves Angelicae* — the 48 (or 19, counting the collapsed Aethyr key) Angelic Keys, which *were* given with English translations — are in Sloane MS 3191. Loagaeth itself was never translated: Dee records that the angels declined to render it.

## What is unsolved
Whether the Loagaeth tables encode anything at all. Three positions are live: (1) a real constructed or revealed language whose lexicon we lack; (2) a cipher or table-generation system in the manner of the Book of Soyga, which Dee knew and was actively asking the angels about in the same period; (3) Kelley's improvised glossolalia, patterned enough to look linguistic and empty of content. Note that positions (1) and (3) are decidable in part by statistics, and position (2) is decidable outright if a generator is found. Also open: why the tables are dimensioned as they are, and the relation between Loagaeth and the separately translated Keys.

## What survives
Sloane MS 3189 in Dee's hand, described as preserving 54 of the book's 73 folios; the structure is given in one encyclopedic account as 98 tables of 49×49 letters on the recto and verso of 49 leaves, and in another as 96 grids of which 94 are 49×49, one is 49 rows of running text and one is 40 rows of text plus 9 rows of 49 letters. These two descriptions do not agree and the discrepancy matters for any statistical work (see Unverified claims). Joseph Peterson's *John Dee's Five Books of Mystery* (2003) and Clay Holden's Dee transcription project have put substantial Dee diary material into print and online; Geoffrey James / Donald Laycock editions print Enochian lexical material. No verified complete machine-readable transcription of the Loagaeth tables themselves was located.

## Prior attempts and current consensus
Donald Laycock's *The Complete Enochian Dictionary* (1978, repr. 1994) is the pivotal treatment: he analysed the Angelical corpus phonologically and concluded the phonology and grammar resemble English, that most words have no apparent etymology, and that the Loagaeth material specifically shows the patterning found in glossolalia rather than in a language with morphology. Deborah Harkness's *John Dee's Conversations with Angels* (1999) reframed the whole corpus as an apocalyptic natural-philosophical project rather than a cipher, and is the standard historical account. Egil Asprem's *Arguing with Angels* (SUNY, 2012) traces the reception into modern occultism. Nobody in the scholarly literature claims a decipherment; the modern occult literature treats the tables as operative rather than semantic. The field has essentially not applied the Soyga method — hunt for a generating rule — to Loagaeth in published form, which is why `crowding` is 4 and `compute` is 4 despite `solvable` being 2.

## What a solution would have to do
A generator claim would have to regenerate at least one full 49×49 table from a stated seed and rule, then predict the contents of a second table not used in fitting, and account for the scribal-error structure the way Reeds's Soyga rule does. A language claim would have to produce a lexicon and morphology derived from the Keys (the only bilingual we have) and then read continuous stretches of Loagaeth with it — that is the crucial test, because the Keys are the corpus's only parallel text and every existing "Enochian dictionary" is built from them. A glossolalia verdict would have to show that Loagaeth's letter statistics match a corpus of attested glossolalic and improvised-text baselines better than they match any natural or constructed language, and would have to explain the 49-based numerology as framing rather than content. In all three cases the first deliverable is the same and does not exist: a verified, complete, machine-readable transcription of Sloane MS 3189 with folio-level provenance.

## Why the scores
- mystery 3: genuinely unresolved, and it bears on Dee's project and on the history of constructed languages, but a resolution would be a chapter, not an earthquake.
- material 4: a substantial autograph witness survives with related diaries; not 5 because the transcription is incomplete and two published descriptions of the book's structure conflict.
- solvable 2: the best expert assessment (Laycock) is that the Loagaeth material is patterned meaninglessness, and the angels' own refusal to translate is, read plainly, Kelley declining to be tested.
- compute 4: exactly the two computable sub-questions — is there a generating rule, and do the statistics match glossolalia baselines — have not been run at scale on a clean transcription.
- verifiable 2: a generator would be mechanically checkable, but the likeliest outcome (glossolalia) is a probabilistic verdict competing readings would survive.
- crowding 4: a handful of serious treatments, a large non-scholarly literature that does not attempt decipherment, and no dedicated computational study.

## Unverified claims
- The structure of Loagaeth. Two encyclopedic descriptions conflict: "98 tables, each 49×49, on recto and verso of 49 leaves" versus "96 grids, 94 of them 49×49, one of 49 rows of text, one of 40 rows of text plus 9 rows of 49 letters," together with "Sloane MS 3189 preserves 54 of its 73 folios." Neither was checked against the manuscript or a facsimile. Any statistical work must settle this first.
- The count of the Angelic Keys: 48 Claves Angelicae versus "nineteen Angelical Keys" (the 19th opening the Thirty Aethyrs) appear in different sources; the relation between the two counts was not verified.
- Laycock's exact wording and the pages for his glossolalia argument were not read; the characterization is from encyclopedic paraphrase of his conclusion.
- Whether Sloane MS 3189 is digitized with public page images. Not established; the `digitized` field points at the BL portal only.
- The Cotton MS shelfmark holding related Loagaeth material is given variously as "Cotton MS Appendix I" and "Cotton Appendix XLVI" in different sources; not resolved here.
