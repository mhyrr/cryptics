# Rimbaud, Voyelles

## What it is
A fourteen-line sonnet written by Arthur Rimbaud in 1871, before his seventeenth birthday. It opens by assigning a colour to each vowel — "A noir, E blanc, I rouge, U vert, O bleu, voyelles" — and then, for each, gives a compressed run of images (flies on stinking corpses for A, glacier and spears for E, spat blood and drunken laughter for I, and so on), closing on the O of the "Clairon suprême" and the violet ray of "Ses Yeux". Rimbaud gave one autograph to his friend Émile Blémont; Verlaine made a copy; Verlaine first published the poem in the review *Lutèce*, 5–12 October 1883.

## What is unsolved
Whether the colour assignments and the image sequence encode a determinate system, and if so which. The sonnet reads like a key — it has the form of a table — and a century of readers have tried to supply the table's rule. Candidate rules include a childhood coloured alphabet primer, a physiological or genuinely synaesthetic correspondence, an alchemical or hermetic scheme (Rimbaud's own retrospective "Alchimie du verbe" in *Une saison en enfer* is the standard warrant), a Kabbalistic scheme, an optical-scientific scheme, and an erotic-anatomical scheme. A second, prior question is whether the poem is a key at all rather than a demonstration of the poet's power to *impose* correspondences, which is how Rimbaud describes his own project in the 1871 "lettres du voyant."

## What survives
Two early witnesses: Rimbaud's autograph, given to Blémont, and Verlaine's transcription; they differ mainly in punctuation. The French Wikipedia account places an autograph in the Musée Rimbaud at Charleville-Mézières. The *Lutèce* printing of October 1883 survives, as does its reprint in Verlaine's *Les Poètes maudits*. The text is short, stable and available in the Pléiade and Garnier critical editions and in Wikisource transcription; there is nothing to transcribe and nothing damaged. The material problem is the opposite of scarcity: the text is complete and still under-determines every proposed rule.

## Prior attempts and current consensus
Ernest Gaubert, Henri Héraut and Rimbaud's schoolmaster Georges Izambard advanced the coloured-primer hypothesis, with Ernest Delahaye's recollections sometimes adduced in support; the objection is that no primer with these colours has been produced. Robert Faurisson, then a lycée teacher, argued in 1961–62 that the poem describes the female body; the claim provoked a national controversy in *Le Monde* and drew academic replies including Étiemble's, and is now generally regarded as a case study in over-reading rather than a reading. Claude Lévi-Strauss proposed that the poem does not assert vowel-to-colour correspondences at all but a system of *oppositions* — saturated A against silent E mapped onto black against white, then chromatic oppositions across I, U, O — which explains the counter-intuitive assignments (the /a/ normally associated with red is made black) without needing an external key. Jean-Jacques Lefrère noted the constraint that no colour-adjective in the poem contains the vowel it is assigned. The current position is that no determinate key has been established, that the poem is better read as poetics than as cipher, and that the esoteric readings are unfalsifiable. Hermetic and Kabbalistic interpretations continue to be published and continue not to be taken up.

## What a solution would have to do
A determinate-key claim would have to: (a) produce the source — an actual primer, an actual occult table, an actual physiological chart — as an object with a publication or provenance, not as a reconstruction; (b) show that the source's assignments match all five of Rimbaud's, since any four can be matched by chance across the space of candidate colour schemes; (c) explain the image clusters, not only the colours, because the bulk of the poem is the images and every proposed key has left them as decoration; (d) survive Lefrère's orthographic constraint and Lévi-Strauss's structural account, i.e. show that the correspondences are not adequately explained by internal opposition; and (e) be consistent with Rimbaud's documented practice elsewhere in 1871–73, where he claims to *invent* correspondences rather than transcribe received ones. Absent (a), the entry treats any proposal as an interpretation rather than a solution.

## Why the scores
- mystery 3: the field has effectively settled that there is no recoverable key, but the question keeps live scholarly attention and the primer hypothesis has never been definitively closed.
- material 4: two early witnesses plus the first printing, all published and digitized; not 5 because the autograph's location and status were not confirmed from a museum catalogue here.
- solvable 2: the best evidence — Lévi-Strauss's structural account, Lefrère's constraint, Rimbaud's own aesthetic statements — favours deliberate construction without an external referent.
- compute 2: one could sweep 19th-century primers, occult colour tables and synaesthesia literature for a five-vowel match, which is a real if narrow corpus task; the interpretive residue is not computational.
- verifiable 2: only the physical discovery of a matching source would convert a reading into a result; otherwise competing readings persist indefinitely.
- crowding 1: one of the most commented poems in French, with a large claimant literature and a famous scandal attached.
