# Cantong qi (Zhouyi cantong qi, 周易參同契)

## What it is
The earliest and most influential Chinese alchemical text: a work in rhymed four- and five-character verse, traditionally attributed to Wei Boyang and dated to the mid-second century CE, which welds three subjects together — cosmology built from the *Book of Changes* (the trigrams Qian, Kun, Kan, Li and the sixty-four hexagram sequence), Daoist non-action, and alchemy described in terms of lead, mercury, cinnabar and "the gold elixir." The title has been rendered *The Seal of the Unity of the Three* and *Kinship of the Three*. It carried over three dozen traditional commentaries and became canonical for later internal alchemy.

## What is unsolved
What it is about. The text's language is emblematic by design — lead and mercury name cosmological principles as much as substances, and "black lead" and cinnabar are said to contain hidden essences requiring extraction — and it has sustained three incompatible readings for over a thousand years: external alchemy on minerals and metals (*waidan*), the reading of the only surviving pre-Tang commentary, of about 700; internal alchemy on the practitioner's own body (*neidan*), the reading of the traditions from the eighth century onward that made it foundational; and cosmology or philosophy, the reading of Zhu Xi and later literati. The text itself criticises breathing, meditation and sexual practices, but who the criticism targets is ambiguous, and no surviving Han or Six Dynasties *waidan* text exhibits its doctrinal principles. Alongside this: authorship (Wei Boyang, or Xu Congshi and Chunyu Shutong, whom the earliest sources credit), the dating and mechanism of the text's layers, and the authenticity of the sixteenth-century "Ancient Text."

## What survives
The received text with an exceptionally rich commentarial apparatus, all transmitted in the Daoist canon and digitized in the Chinese classical corpora (CText, Kanripo, the *Zhengtong daozang*). Zhu Xi's commentary of 1197 compared textual variants and is the first critical treatment; Yu Yan's of 1284 assembled over a hundred source quotations and variants; Peng Xiao's of 947 popularised the Wei Boyang attribution. The *Guwen cantong qi* ("Ancient Text"), published by Yang Shen (1488–1559) and claimed to have been found in a stone casket, rearranges the whole work into separate four- and five-character sections and was rejected as a forgery by many Qing scholars, though it preserves nearly the entire text. Fabrizio Pregadio's *The Seal of the Unity of the Three* (Golden Elixir Press, 2011) is the annotated English translation of the standard text, and his *Bibliographic Studies on Taoism: The Cantong qi* (2012) surveys the commentaries. Earlier translations: Lu-Ch'iang Wu and Tenney Davis (1932), Zhou Shiyi (1988), Richard Bertschinger (1994, of the Ancient Text).

## Prior attempts and current consensus
Pregadio's two volumes are the necessary reading and the current state of the art: he argues the work reached a form close to the present one before about 450 and possibly one or two centuries later, and that it is a layered accretion rather than a single composition. The strata arguments he and others rely on are specific and testable: the cosmological portions may draw on Yu Fan's *Yijing* commentary (Yu Fan, 164–233), which would place them after the Han; the alchemical content shows no influence from earlier Han material; and the Daoist sections use terminology from the Shangqing revelations of 364–370, implying fourth-century or later revision. Meng Naichang (1993) investigated the relation to Han cosmological apocrypha (*weishu*). Needham, Ho Peng Yoke and Sivin translated parts within larger histories. The consensus: composite, later than tradition claims, and genuinely ambiguous as to subject — with the ambiguity increasingly read as constitutive rather than accidental.

## What a solution would have to do
The stratification question is the tractable one and it has real criteria. A solution must date the layers by internal linguistic evidence: rhyme analysis against the reconstructed phonology of Eastern Han, Six Dynasties and Tang Chinese, which for verse of this regularity is a genuine dating instrument; lexical and grammatical profiling of the four-character against the five-character passages; and n-gram alignment against the digitized Han *weishu*, *Yijing* commentaries including Yu Fan, and the Shangqing corpus, to establish which passages quote what. Each of those returns a checkable result independent of any reading of the alchemy, and together they would test Pregadio's stratification directly. The Ancient Text question is decidable by the same means: if Yang Shen's rearrangement introduces language later than the standard text, it is a reconstruction; if not, the forgery verdict needs a different argument. For the subject question, a reading must explain why no Han or Six Dynasties *waidan* text matches the doctrine, must account for the text's own criticism of other practices, and must cover the whole work — the commentarial tradition's characteristic move is to read the passages that suit and allegorise the rest, and a modern reading may not do that.

## Why the scores
- mystery 4: the subject of the founding text of Chinese alchemy is genuinely unresolved after a millennium of commentary, and a resolution would matter to sinology and to the comparative history of alchemy.
- material 4: the text is fully transmitted with three dozen commentaries, digitized in the classical corpora, with a modern annotated scholarly translation and a bibliographic survey; not 5 because there is no critical edition that stratifies the text and the commentarial variants are not collated in machine-readable form.
- solvable 3: a layered composite may not have a single intended meaning to recover, and serious scholars hold both that it does and that the ambiguity is original.
- compute 4: rhyme-based dating, lexical stratification and corpus-wide n-gram alignment are exactly the right instruments, the Chinese classical corpus is heavily digitized, and none of this has been done systematically; not 5 because the commentaries' variant readings still need transcription and collation.
- verifiable 4: a stratification argument from rhyme and lexicon is checkable by independent linguistic criteria and would be accepted by sinologists; the subject question would remain interpretive.
- crowding 3: Pregadio's two volumes, Meng Naichang, Needham and Sivin, plus an immense traditional commentarial literature; the obvious philological approaches have been described but the computational ones are untried.
