# The unidentified 1452/53 eruption (UE 1453)

## What it is
A sulfate spike that starts in 1453 CE in Greenland ice cores (NEEM-2011-S1,
TUNU2013, NGRIP), with a much smaller Antarctic signal. It is followed by the
coldest Northern Hemisphere summer in tree-ring density records for about 800
years. The eruption that produced it is not identified. It is distinct from the
larger 1458 event, which was long conflated with it under the name "Kuwae".
SCHOLARLY — Abbott et al. 2021; Esper et al. 2017.

## What is unsolved
Which volcano erupted in 1452 or early 1453. Sub-questions: its latitude band,
the season of eruption, and its size. No tephra has been reported from the
1453 ice layer, so the standard geochemical match has never been possible.

## What survives
- Ice-core sulfate, open on PANGAEA. eVolv2k v4 lists 1453 as 24.8 kg km⁻²
  in Greenland, 5.1 in Antarctica, asymmetry 0.829, 9.97 ± 3.07 Tg S, tephra
  not detected, location N/A. PRIMARY — PANGAEA.971968.
- Sulfur isotopes from TUNU2013 (Burke et al. 2023, dataset S01 and code on
  GitHub). None for 1453 in Antarctica.
- Tree-ring density networks (Esper et al. 2017; N-TREND) and documentary
  records (Ming, Joseon, European), partly digitized.
- Smithsonian GVP eruption table, bulk CSV via its WFS endpoint, without login.
- The ice itself (TUNU2013 archive at DRI and Queen's University Belfast) is
  not open data.

## Prior attempts and current consensus
The 1452/53 dates were tied to Kuwae (Vanuatu) through Antarctic chronologies
anchored on the 1453 tree-ring minimum. Better-dated cores put the large
bipolar spike at 1458, which leaves 1453 as a separate, Greenland-heavy event
(Sigl et al. 2013, 2015; Abbott et al. 2021). Kuwae is out for 1453. Latitude
is mildly contested: older work says low-latitude NH, and Burke et al. 2023
(PNAS) classify it as NH extratropical from sulfur isotopes. No paper names a
candidate volcano. Read Abbott et al. 2021, Burke et al. 2023 and Esper et al. 2017.

## What a solution would have to do
1. Explain the deposition pattern: about 25 kg km⁻² Greenland and 5 kg km⁻²
   Antarctica, with onset in the 1453 layer.
2. Fit the eruption date (1452 or early 1453) independently of the ice, from
   proximal stratigraphy dated by radiocarbon, dendrochronology or tephra.
3. Fit the size: about 10 Tg S injected, with partial stratospheric reach
   (54 ± 6 % stratospheric, Burke 2023).
4. Fit the latitude and the sulfur-isotope signature.
5. Account for the lack of contemporary eyewitness accounts.
6. Decisively: match glass shards from the 1453 ice layer to the proximal
   deposit by major and trace elements.

Criteria 1 to 5 can only shortlist candidates. Only 6 identifies the volcano.

## Why the scores
- mystery 4: a top-five cooling event of the last 1,500 years with no known source.
- material 4: excellent open sulfate and climate data; the decisive physical
  sample (ice shards) is held, not published.
- solvable 4: a physical cause certainly exists; the analogue cases (1831
  Zavaritskii, 1477 Veiðivötn) were solved. The risk is that the proximal
  deposit is unmapped or eroded.
- compute 4: open data can narrow latitude, season and candidates, but it cannot decide.
- verifiable 5: a glass geochemical match is mechanical.
- crowding 4: named "future work" in Abbott 2021; no dedicated study found.

## Unverified claims
- The Ming and Constantinople documentary effects reach the literature
  through Pang 1993 only. No primary chronicle entries checked yet.
- The eVolv2k v4 header gives deposition as kg/m²; Abbott 2021 gives the same numbers as kg km⁻².
- The season of onset in the ice is not yet extracted.
- Burke 2023 ref. 50 (an earlier NH-extratropical proposal) not identified.
