# The identity of Elena Ferrante

## What it is
"Elena Ferrante" is the pseudonym under which a body of Italian fiction has appeared since 1992, published by Edizioni E/O in Rome: *L'amore molesto*, *I giorni dell'abbandono*, *La figlia oscura*, the four Neapolitan Novels (2011–2015), and later work, together with the collected letters, interviews and essays published as *La frantumaglia*. The author has never appeared in public, conducts all exchanges in writing through the publisher, and has argued from the beginning that "books, once they are written, have no need of their authors." The Neapolitan Novels made the pseudonym internationally famous; *Time* named Ferrante one of its hundred most influential people in 2016.

## What is unsolved
Who wrote the books. The case belongs in this catalog because it is an authorial concealment with a determinate answer — a real person or persons wrote them — and because it is the clearest modern instance where the standard method of attack is computational. It also raises a question the catalog should not dodge: the author has asked not to be identified, and the field's reaction to the most aggressive attempt at identification was largely that it was an intrusion rather than a contribution. Both the epistemic and the ethical status of a "solution" are therefore contested.

## What survives
Everything a stylometric attribution needs. The entire Ferrante corpus is published, digitized and machine-readable; so are the candidate authors' corpora and a large comparison corpus of contemporary Italian fiction. *La frantumaglia* provides the author's own statements, including an early letter setting out the reasons for anonymity. The non-textual evidence is financial and archival: royalty flows, property records, publisher records. This is an unusually favourable evidence situation for an attribution problem — a fixed target corpus of substantial length, named candidates with substantial comparison corpora, and an independent documentary channel.

## Prior attempts and current consensus
Two lines of attack, pointing at a married couple. In October 2016 the investigative journalist Claudio Gatti published an analysis of real-estate transactions and royalty payments and concluded that the Rome-based translator Anita Raja was Ferrante; Raja and the publisher denied it, and the reaction in the literary press was predominantly that the investigation was a violation of the author's stated wishes. Separately, a stylometric programme at the University of Padua led by Arjuna Tuzzi and Michele Cortelazzo compared the Ferrante corpus against a corpus of around 150 contemporary Italian novels and found the closest authorial profile to be that of Domenico Starnone, Raja's husband, a novelist and journalist; Starnone has repeatedly and emphatically denied it. Earlier candidates, including the historian Marcella Marmo and the novelist Anna Maria Ortese, have been dismissed. The current position is contested: the stylometric result is robust across several independent studies presented at the 2017 Padua workshop, the financial result is circumstantial but concrete, and the two point at the same household, which is either strong convergence or a confound — the couple's shared language, shared editing and shared milieu would produce exactly this signal.

## What a solution would have to do
Because two plausible answers are now on the table and both come from the same household, the discriminating work is specific. (a) A stylometric claim must distinguish *Starnone wrote them* from *Raja wrote them and Starnone edited them* (or vice versa, or genuine collaboration). That requires a controlled design: validation on held-out texts by both candidates, a test on known collaborative and known edited texts to establish how the method behaves under editing, and a demonstration that the Ferrante–Starnone similarity is not attributable to a shared editor, a shared translator's register, or Edizioni E/O house style. Reporting a nearest neighbour in a 150-author space is not sufficient, because the true author may be outside the space and the nearest neighbour is then meaningless. (b) A claim must also handle the multi-author hypothesis explicitly, by rollup analysis across the corpus over time — if the profile shifts between books, a single author is in doubt. (c) Documentary confirmation would settle it: a contract, a manuscript, a tax record, an acknowledgement. (d) Any solution must confront the ethical question directly rather than by omission: the subject is living, has stated a reason for anonymity, and the field's response to Gatti indicates that an identification advanced without consent will be judged partly on whether it was worth making. A catalog entry can record the state of the evidence without endorsing the project of outing.

## Why the scores
- mystery 3: contested with two well-supported candidates converging on one household; a settled answer would matter to Italian literary studies and to the wider argument about authorship and anonymity, but the books do not change.
- material 5: the target corpus and all comparison corpora are complete, digitized and machine-readable, with an independent documentary channel.
- solvable 5: a determinate answer certainly exists — someone wrote the books.
- compute 5: this is the catalog's clearest case of a core question that is computational with data already prepared, and the published methodology exists; the outstanding work is experimental design, not access.
- verifiable 3: a consensus could form within years from a well-controlled study plus documentary evidence; it is held down by the denials, by the shared-household confound, and by a real possibility that the field will decline to ratify any answer.
- crowding 3: a substantial stylometric literature and a high-profile journalistic investigation; the discriminating experiments described above appear not to have been run.

## Unverified claims
- The exact publisher, title and date of the Padua proceedings volume, and the number of novels in its comparison corpus (given as about 150 in the secondary source), were not verified against the volume.
- The date and venues of Gatti's publication were not verified.
- The characterisation of the stylometric result as "robust across several independent studies" rests on the secondary source's description of the workshop and was not verified study by study.
- Whether any published study has tested the Raja-versus-Starnone discrimination, the edited-text confound, or the multi-author hypothesis in the controlled way described above is unknown to this entry; the statement of absence is a finding from this pass only, and it is what the `crowding` score of 3 rests on.
- Ferrante's 1991 or 1992 letter to Edizioni E/O setting out the reasons for anonymity is dated inconsistently across accounts; not settled here.
