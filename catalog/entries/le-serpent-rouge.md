# Le Serpent Rouge (1967) and the Priory of Sion dossiers

## What it is
A short typescript in prose-poetry, dated 15 February 1967 and mailed to the Bibliothèque nationale de France on 20 March 1967, consisting of thirteen stanzas named for the signs of the zodiac — the twelve conventional signs plus Ophiuchus inserted between Scorpio and Sagittarius — together with plans and diagrams of the seminary of Saint-Sulpice and the church of Saint-Germain-des-Prés in Paris, and allusions to Rennes-le-Château, the Blanchefort–Hautpoul genealogy and a Merovingian bloodline. It is credited to three authors, Pierre Feugère, Louis Saint-Maxent and Gaston de Koker. It is the fifth of a series of documents deposited at the BnF between 1964 and 1967 under a succession of pseudonyms — Henri Lobineau (January 1964), Madeleine Blancasall (August 1965), Antoine l'Ermite (May 1966), S. Roux (November 1966), then *Le Serpent Rouge* (March 1967), then Philippe Toscan du Plantier (April 1967) — collectively the *Dossiers Secrets*.

## What is unsolved
Nothing about its origin: the document belongs to a fabricated corpus, and the corpus's fabricators are known. The catalog keeps it as a calibration case, because *Le Serpent Rouge* is the purest specimen of the genre the rubric exists to filter — a text engineered to look like an unsolved cipher, complete with a manufactured atmosphere of danger. All three named authors are recorded as having died between the document's stated composition date and its mailing date, and the French Wikipedia account of the *Dossiers* judges this to be a device: the real author appears to have trawled obituaries for the names of people who had recently killed themselves, so that the document would arrive trailing three deaths. The genuinely open residue is minor and documentary: which of Pierre Plantard and Philippe de Chérisey wrote which parts, and what, if anything, the Saint-Sulpice diagrams were meant to point at within their own invented scheme.

## What survives
The deposited typescript at the BnF, the other *Dossiers Secrets* items with their deposit dates, the forged "Rennes-le-Château parchments," Plantard's journal *Vaincre*, and the record of the 1993 judicial investigation in which Plantard admitted under oath that he had fabricated everything, including the documents proclaiming him the rightful king of France. In 1989 Plantard had already declared in *Vaincre* that the *Dossiers Secrets* were "false documents fabricated by Philippe de Chérisey." Facsimiles of *Le Serpent Rouge* circulate widely on enthusiast sites; no scholarly facsimile or critical edition exists, and no verified digitization URL is pinned here.

## Prior attempts and current consensus
The consensus, established by French and British investigative work in the 1980s–90s and confirmed by Plantard's own admissions, is that the Priory of Sion was Plantard's invention and the dossiers were manufactured by Plantard and Chérisey and planted in the BnF so that later researchers would "discover" them. Chérisey admitted composing the Rennes-le-Château parchments, whose Latin contains a cipher he designed. The documents became the evidentiary basis of *The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail* (1982) and, through it, of *The Da Vinci Code*; the entire popular edifice rests on a corpus its own authors disowned. Paul Smith's documentary archive and Bill Putnam and John Edwin Wood's *The Treasure of Rennes-le-Château: A Mystery Solved* (2003) are the standard debunkings.

## What a solution would have to do
There is no meaning to recover, so the useful questions are attributional and archival, and they have real bars. A claim about authorship of *Le Serpent Rouge* specifically would need: (a) the BnF deposit record with its date and accession details; (b) stylometric comparison against the known writings of Plantard and Chérisey and against the other dossiers, which is feasible — the corpus is small but the comparison texts exist; (c) documentary evidence for the obituary-trawling hypothesis, i.e. the death records of Feugère, Saint-Maxent and de Koker with dates, causes and any published notices, which would confirm or kill it outright; and (d) an account of why Ophiuchus is inserted, since a thirteenth sign is the one structural choice in the document that looks deliberate rather than decorative. Conversely, any claim that the document encodes a real pre-1956 secret must first explain how a text written by two identified hoaxers in 1967 could do so, and must produce a pre-1956 witness to the alleged secret. None has been produced in sixty years.

## Unverified claims
- No BnF call number or catalogue record for *Le Serpent Rouge* was located; the deposit is attested only through the secondary accounts.
- The deaths of Feugère, Saint-Maxent and de Koker are reported in the secondary source, which also reports the obituary-trawling explanation as a judgement rather than a documented finding. Neither the deaths nor the explanation is verified here.
- The English and French Wikipedia articles give different dates for the *Dossiers Secrets* deposit (27 April 1967 for the 27-page compilation; 20 March 1967 for *Le Serpent Rouge*). These are probably different objects but the relationship was not confirmed.
- The content description of *Le Serpent Rouge* beyond the zodiac structure and the two Paris church plans is from the secondary account, not from the document.
- Paul Smith's archive at priory-of-sion.com is the usual source for document facsimiles; no URL was verified in this pass.
