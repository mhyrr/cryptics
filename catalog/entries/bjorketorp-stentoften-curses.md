# Björketorp and Stentoften curse inscriptions (the Blekinge group)

## What it is
Four related Proto-Norse inscriptions from Blekinge in south-eastern Sweden, cut roughly 500–700 CE in Elder Futhark with some transitional rune-forms, which share formulae, personal names and a curse. The **Björketorp** stone stands 4.2 m high at Björketorp, among the tallest runestones anywhere, and carries text on two faces: side A, transliterated *hAidz runo ronu, fAlAhAk hAiderA ginArunAz, ArAgeu hAerAmAlAusz, … welAdAude, sAz þAt bArutz*; side B, *uþArAbA sbA*. The **Stentoften** stone (DR 357) was found in 1823 at Stentoften and moved to the church at Sölvesborg in 1864; it names *Haþuwulfz* and *Hariwulfz* and uses an ideographic *j*-rune for *jēra*, "year/harvest." The **Gummarp** and **Istaby** stones complete the group; the names suggest one chieftainly kindred.

## What is unsolved
The curse is understood in outline and the residue is lexical and syntactic. Björketorp side A is rendered along the lines of "I, master of the runes(?), conceal here runes of power. Incessantly plagued by maleficence, doomed to insidious death, is he who breaks this monument," and side B as "I prophesy destruction" or "prophecy of destruction" — but *hAidz runo ronu* has been construed as "runes of brightness," "a row of bright runes," "I hid here runes of power," and as a genitive chain of uncertain government; *fAlh*/*fAlAhAk* as "I concealed" or "I entrusted"; *ArAgeu* as a dative of "cowardice/perversion" or "unmanliness"; and *uþArAbA sbA* as "prophecy of ruin," "evil prophecy," or a compound of a different sense entirely. On Stentoften, the sequence conventionally read as a parallel to Björketorp's *hAideRrunoronu* has been reinterpreted by Santesson (1989) as *hAborumR hAideRrunoronu* meaning "nine bucks, nine stallions," i.e. a record of animal sacrifice for good harvest rather than a rune-formula — a reading that, if right, changes the sense of the parallel passage on Björketorp too. Whether Björketorp marks a grave, a cenotaph, a cult site or a boundary is also unresolved; excavation found nothing to decide it.

## What survives
All four stones survive; Björketorp is in situ within a stone setting, Stentoften at Sölvesborg church. The Gummarp stone's condition is the weak point of the group (see Unverified claims). Texts are published in *Danmarks runeindskrifter* (Jacobsen and Moltke, 1941–42; Stentoften is DR 357), in Krause and Jankuhn's *Die Runeninschriften im älteren Futhark* (1966), and in Rundata. Readings and translations differ by edition. No open digital facsimile of museum standard was located.

## Prior attempts and current consensus
The Blekinge group has been read as a set since Wimmer and Jacobsen; the agreed core is that Björketorp and Stentoften carry substantially the same curse against whoever destroys the monument, and that the personal names tie the stones to one family. Krause and Jankuhn's readings are the German standard; Jacobsen and Moltke's the Danish; Grønvik has published on the group; Lillemor Santesson's 1989 reinterpretation of the Stentoften sacrifice lines is the most consequential recent intervention and is treated in the handbooks as a live proposal rather than as settled. The field's position is therefore: the curse is translated with confidence at the level of force and function, and with low confidence at the level of individual words; the Santesson reading is reported and debated.

## What a solution would have to do
Treat the group as a single philological problem, not four. Concretely: (1) give one construal of *hAidz runo ronu* / *hAideRrunoronu* that works on both Björketorp and Stentoften, since the two occurrences are the strongest available control and most readings handle them separately; (2) decide *fAlh* / *fAlAhAk* as a verb form with a stated morphology and apply that morphology consistently to the rest of the inscription; (3) construe *uþArAbA sbA* with a segmentation that yields attested Proto-Norse lexemes, and explain why it stands alone on face B; (4) take a position on the Santesson reading and show what it predicts elsewhere in the corpus — if *hAborumR* is "nine bucks," the reading should illuminate other sacrificial inscriptions or the *jēra* ideograph, not only this line; (5) accommodate the transitional rune-forms and the syncope question, since the group straddles the same boundary as the Eggja stone. The available verification is internal and comparative: a reading that resolves the shared formula on both stones with the same morphology, and needs fewer *hapax* senses than Krause's, would be adopted.

## Why the scores
- mystery 3: the inscriptions are read in outline and the curse's force is agreed; the residue of dispute is real but lexical, and a resolution would matter to runology alone.
- material 3: four related stones, substantially preserved, which is an unusually good evidential situation for Elder Futhark — but the texts are short, access to high-quality imaging is limited, and no open critical digitization was found.
- solvable 4: formulaic curse language repeated across monuments is about as clear a case of determinate design as early runic epigraphy offers.
- compute 2: segmentation enumeration scored against a Proto-Norse lexicon is tractable and would be useful, but the deciding arguments are morphological and comparative.
- verifiable 2: no external control; competing readings would remain, and the best one can do is relative economy.
- crowding 3: a substantial but not saturated runological literature; Santesson's 1989 reading shows the obvious approaches were not all tried.

## Unverified claims
- **The state of the Gummarp stone.** It is widely reported to have been destroyed in the Copenhagen fire of 1728 and to survive only through earlier drawings. This was not confirmed in the sources consulted and is recorded here as unverified; if true it materially weakens the group's evidential base.
- The attribution of specific construals of *hAidz runo ronu*, *fAlh* and *ArAgeu* to particular editors is generalised from the existence of competing editions; the individual readings were not checked against Krause, Jacobsen–Moltke or Grønvik.
- Santesson's 1989 publication details (journal, volume) are from the standard bibliography, not from a fetched source; the sources fetched attribute the "nine bucks, nine stallions" reading without full citation.
- Whether the Santesson reading has been accepted, rejected or left open by the runological community was not established.
- No open digital facsimile was located for any of the four stones; absence of a link means "not found."
