# Royalist and Commonwealth cipher fragments of the English Civil War and Interregnum, 1645–1678

## What it is
This entry groups a dozen short unread passages in numerical nomenclators, all from Satoshi Tomokiyo's Cryptiana list under "English Civil War" and "Intercepts by Commonwealth." Most are a few lines of figures inside a letter that is otherwise in clear English. The figures are partly a letter alphabet and partly code numbers for words and names. The writers include Charles I, Charles II, Prince Maurice, Lord Craven, Edward Hyde and the Duke of Ormond, as well as Dutch and royalist correspondents whose letters John Thurloe's office intercepted. The ciphertexts survive in the British Library (Add MS 72438, 18982, 4166), in Thurloe's papers, and in nineteenth-century printed editions.

## What is unsolved
The plaintext of each short ciphered passage. The question is not the system, which is well understood: these are English and Dutch state nomenclators of the 1640s–1670s, and Tomokiyo, Lasry and Biermann have reconstructed dozens of them. The question is which key each passage used, and what the few lines say.

## What survives
Every item survives as ciphertext. About half are printed in old editions now online:
- Warburton (Google Books).
- *Thurloe State Papers* (British History Online).
- HMC Ormonde (Internet Archive).
- *Preservation of King Charles II*.
- Albin's *History of the Isle of Wight*.

The British Library originals (Add MS 72438, 18982, 4166) are catalogued in DECODE with images. As of September 2026 Bourdeau reports that the BL's own image server has been offline since the 2023 cyber-attack, and that the DECODE images need a login. Tomokiyo has transcribed the printed items (Cryptiana articles linked below). There is no single machine-readable corpus.

## Prior attempts and current consensus
Tomokiyo has catalogued these items and reconstructed the neighbouring keys (Cryptiana, "King Charles I's Ciphers," "King Charles II's Ciphers during Exile," "Ciphers of Thurloe," "Dutch ciphers"). Two of the four 1648 letters fell to Biermann and Brown in 2021. They anchored on alphabetically arranged common words (615 = YOU, 563 = THE) and used context from the clear text.

In September 2026 Daniel Bourdeau's AI-assisted project (dbourdeau.github.io/cyphersolver) attacked several of the items. It reports:
- **Maurice–Rupert, Charles II–Hamilton and the remaining Isle of Wight letters:** "not solvable online." Ciphertext-only methods on 20–100-group passages fail matched controls, so each needs its key.
- **Thurloe intercepts:** "stuck."
- **BL items (Add MS 72438 f.9–10, R8447, R4886):** "reopenable" once DECODE images are fetched.

Consensus: unsolved, and probably solvable by finding keys rather than by cryptanalysis.

Newcomers should read Tomokiyo's articles first, then Biermann/Brown on Cipherbrain (2021), then Bourdeau's `TARGETS.md` for the most current state.

## What a solution would have to do
- Read each passage with a single key: either a key found in an archive or one reconstructed from other letters in the same correspondence. The same key must give grammatical seventeenth-century English (or Dutch/French) wherever that correspondent used it.
- Fit the clear text on either side of the passage. Several have strong constraints. One sits after "a conditional advice concerning." Another is followed by the remark "This is a trial whether you are skilful in deciphering." The Hyde 1659 list must yield names matching its cover names.
- Not be fitted to a 20-group fragment alone. A reading of a passage under about 100 groups that no other letter confirms is a guess, however fluent.

## Why the scores
- **mystery 2:** short operational passages in well-documented correspondences. Specialists would welcome the Isle of Wight and Hamilton letters (the King's escape plans, Charles II in Scotland), but none is expected to overturn a known question.
- **material 3:** the ciphertexts are complete, but they are short. The BL originals are not openly viewable as of 2026, and the printed transcriptions carry typesetting risk. Could be 4 for the printed items alone.
- **solvable 5:** state nomenclators, determinate, used consistently. Several siblings in the same hands have been solved.
- **compute 3:** this could have gone either way. The key-matching sweep is computational and cheap: test each passage against every reconstructed royalist and Thurloe-era key, including the 49 captured Digby keys in Add MS 72438. But ciphertext-only attack on fragments this short fails controls (Bourdeau), so the decisive step is archival.
- **verifiable 4:** with an archive key the check is mechanical. Without one, a 20–40-group English reading stays ambiguous.
- **crowding 3:** below the genre default. Tomokiyo, Biermann, Brown, Lasry, Pitt, Aymeloglu and Bourdeau have all worked this exact list, most of them within the last month.

## Unverified claims
- The BL shelfmarks and DECODE record numbers are as given by Tomokiyo and Bourdeau. The DECODE records were not opened here.
- That NRS GD406/1/2197 contains the key actually used in the 1650 Hamilton letters. Bourdeau identifies it from the catalogue description only.
- The count "about 49 captured Digby keys" in Add MS 72438, and that they would read ff.9–10. This is Bourdeau's expectation, not a result.
- Whether any of these passages was deciphered at the time or in print (for example, in the Calendar of State Papers Domestic or the Clarendon State Papers). Tomokiyo's notice warns that listed items are "not necessarily ones that have never been deciphered," and no full prior-art check was made here.
- The group counts for the Thurloe pieces (136/22/16/50) come from Bourdeau. They were not counted here.
