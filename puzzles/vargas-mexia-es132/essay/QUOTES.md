# Quotation ledger: Into My Hands

2026-10-06. Audited against the fuller `DRAFT-fable.md`, then the reordered and expanded `DRAFT.md`.
This ledger concerns quotation provenance, not a new decipherment.

## What the typography means

In `DRAFT.md`, bold inside a manuscript quotation marks a span written in cipher in BnF Espagnol 132.
Plain type marks ordinary handwriting. English translations follow the same boundaries as closely as English word
order permits. Brackets still mark editorial supplies or uncertainty: bold locates the cipher span and does not
certify a bracketed reconstruction. The worked example from Tomokiyo uses the same bold convention.

Quotations taken from printed books are identified in the prose and stay plain. Some printed texts themselves descend
from historical decipherments. Thus plain type in a quotation attributed to Teulet does **not** mean that Vargas's
original dispatch was unenciphered. We have not inspected those originals.

All English renderings are this essay's editorial translations, including translations of Spanish already in print.
They are not independent evidence. Word division, accents, expanded abbreviations and punctuation in the manuscript
readings are also editorial. Spanish spellings are retained from the cited joined readings, rather than silently
harmonized across different letters or editions.

## Reading history

“From cipher” and “first deciphered here” are different claims. No quotation in this essay is advertised as a first
decipherment by this project. Tomokiyo published the keys and specimen and read isolated words of f. 198 in 2020.
Cabinet-noir published overlapping readings before this dive opened on 3 October 2026. Pangoleen supplies other
overlapping readings dated 4 October, consulted after our relevant decodes were fixed; relative priority within that
day is not established. Clear handwriting required transcription and translation, but no cryptographic decoding.

The letter-by-letter record below distinguishes what we read ourselves from what we quote from print. It does not
claim an exhaustive search for every earlier edition or unpublished decipherment. Neither another model's agreement
nor its claim of novelty establishes priority or historical accuracy.

## Mixed passages and spelling decisions

- **Expansion, 2026-10-06:** Q11 now includes the request for papers/letters. Its ellipsis omits the intervening
  clause containing the uncertain `[prendas?]`; it does not join different letters. The reconciled f. 123 reading
  gives *teatino*, while reader B has *se a ti no*. The identity is unresolved and the narrative says so.
  Q12 now includes the reported union and money, from `readings-v2.md` and `f154_v2.txt`, numbered lines 66–71.
  `[Duke]` is an English expansion of *el de Lorrena*, not a recovered Spanish title. The joined reading has *mil*
  here and *mill* in Q13, both retained. These are reports under inquiry, not established payments or a proved pact.
  Q32 restores the longer question already joined in `OVERVIEW.md`, “After the arrest,” and Q35 replaces a paraphrase
  with Pérez's ciphered claim; all remain provisional readings with known overlaps, not new decipherments.
- **Q05:** In `f11_B_decoded.txt`, numbered transcript lines 95–97 carry the cipher from “y de todo…” through
  “Alanson.” Lines 98–99 put “porque cierto son cosas que tienen mucha consideración” inside `[[clear text]]`.
  Bold stops before the ellipsis. The English stops at “Alençon.” `[que]` is retained as a supply.
- **Q16:** `readings-v2.md` marks “solo” and “[carta] particular” as cipher, “venga en la” and the final reassurance
  as clear. Bracketed `[carta]` is a proposed expansion, not an established key value. The English moves “only” to its
  natural position without changing its cipher status.
- **Q22:** The reconciled raw output `f198_v2.txt`, numbered line 56, has cipher before `[[sus ministros]]`.
  It contains an unresolved `<y7>` sign inside the word interpreted as *diablo*. The essay now uses `[diablo]` and
  `[devil]`; the conjunction *y* is also outside the clear-text delimiters. The earlier v1 joining leaves that
  conjunction plain; the current marking follows the reconciled v2 transcript.
- **Q19/Q20/Q23:** V2 supersedes v1's *me acusó*, *honrarme/honra* and *desea* with the joined readings *me puso*,
  *honrrarme/honrra* and *dessea*. The essay follows v2, without silently changing the old research files.
- **Q23/Q24:** `V.Magd` follows the joined reading's representation of the cipher's royal title. “His Majesty” is
  the English contextual rendering, not a claim that the Spanish abbreviation has been diplomatically expanded.
- **Q25:** Checked against the printed page image, not only OCR: *escussar*, *no asido*, *escusse*, *sosegaeis*.
  The earlier fuller draft had *escusar*, *no a sido*, *escuse*, *sosegueis*. The excerpt now follows the print,
  including *será*. The Hague original remains uninspected. The English rendering follows the sense Mignet gives
  in the adjoining French: intervention to stop proceedings would admit the King's part in the murder.
- **Names:** The narrative uses *Escobedo* and *Pérez*. Spanish excerpts retain *Escovedo*, *Perez*, *Guysas*,
  *Alanson* and *Çayas* where the recorded reading has them. English uses the familiar Alençon and Zayas.
