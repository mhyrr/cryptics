**Computed on the v1 one-reader decodes (`f157_v1.txt`, `f179_v1.txt`, decoder `decode_v1.py`). To be redone on v2.**

Source for the clear text: Rubino (2012) transcriptions, `sources/cache/rubino-ff105-148-157-179.txt`. Our decoded runs are
given as syllables exactly as output; the italic word division is mine and changes no syllable. ⟨…⟩ = undecoded sign
(a gap); UPPER = extension value; [clear] = clear text as our reader saw it, inside a run. A syllable that looks wrong is
left as output.

Judging rule: "join before" = the run's first words continue Rubino's clear text grammatically; "join after" = the clear
text resumes grammatically from the run's last words; "yes" = both the edge and the run are readable Spanish; "partly" =
the edge fits but the run is partly unreadable, or only one side of the edge works; "no" = no grammatical join or no
decoded run at that spot. Judged by one reader (the drafter), not blind to Rubino, so the table is a check, not a test.

Counting the string "[CIFRA]" in her text gives **19 in f. 157 and 16 in f. 179**. The tables use the counted gaps. (The 13 and 8
were probably counted by cipher passage as she summarises them.)

## Summary
The decodes join Rubino's clear text far better than chance on both folios: in f. 179 only 5 of 16 left joins and 4 of
16 right joins are not "yes", and in f. 157 the clean joins ("al Príncipe de Parma", "el manejo del dinero", "conseguir… como se
quería", "con tanto recato que ni por imaginación") come at the places where the run is itself readable. Most "partly"
rows are runs that are partly unreadable (undecoded signs, *cauti-*, "tr m ui ra to"), not runs that contradict the
clear text; no row shows a run that is readable Spanish and breaks the grammar of its edge. The weakest evidence is
f. 157's second half and the passages where our reader and Rubino disagree about what is clear text (rows 14, 15, 17,
19), which is a reading problem to settle on the folio before v2.
