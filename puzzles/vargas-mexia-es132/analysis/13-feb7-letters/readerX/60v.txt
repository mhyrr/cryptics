## Summary
At the assembly (the Portuguese Estates), when the King's harangue was given the prelates protested; some began to cry out loudly against their king, and some even laid hands on (?) — then things calmed and the King returned to his palace.
The King had the second session told of his displeasure that they had been so stirred against his goodwill, and after a long harangue urged them to consider that the succession belongs to the Catholic King (Philip II), and that their good and welfare lay there.
At the second session opinions were divided; several inclined to the King's council.
At the third session they were pressed to accept his will, that one treat with the Catholic King for honest and bearable conditions, saying they would be very happy to live under so great a king.
The page ends turning to the claim of the Duchess of Braganza.
