## New or doubtful signs
- ʃʃʃ (triple long stroke): no letter value fits in L09 (credict), L22 (debvoir), L23 (Italliens), L27 (volontiers). Proposed null. On 41r L16 I read it t; that may be wrong.
- Ꝣ / ʐ (z-shape with tail): ff here (L06 Affricque, L09/L12 traffic). On 40v L08 and 41r L01 I read ʐ as rr ("arrive"); the two shapes may be distinct, or one of the readings is wrong.
- ꝗ (q with crossed stem): q in L28 (querelle), y in L13/L27 (françoys, il y a). It may be two signs.
- 8 alone = cc in L18 (occasion).
- ⁖ (three dots) = d in L08 and L23 (Mousset's third d).
- L29 "a r q a n s t h o i n e": a name? Not fixed; "Anthoine" is possible inside it, but I have not forced it.

## Summary
The letter says that someone would have had the heads cut off of all the Frenchmen who trade in Barbary and "Maroecos" (Morocco), and in Africa.
Those French have lost all credit and can no longer trade safely in any of those parts.
The writer prays God that "ces humeurs" (these humours) may be settled, and that each man may keep to his duty.
The Italians under the command of Don Pedro de Medicis (Pietro de' Medici) are starting to "se pourrir" (rot, i.e. fall apart), and those who can desert do so willingly.
There is a very great quarrel between Fabrice Colonne (Fabrizio Colonna) and another person, whose name I could not read (L29).
