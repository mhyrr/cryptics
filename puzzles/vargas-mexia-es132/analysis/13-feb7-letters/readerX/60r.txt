## New or doubtful signs
- ꝺσ (flat ∞-loop with left hook) = u/v: fixed by couronne (L14), toutes (L03), envoya (L07), duchesse (L17, L21), excluant (L18), vouloir (L19). Treated as null on 59v by me at first; corrected there.
- ∂ = l on this page: celles (L16), seule (L15), seulement (L19), conseil (L30), fille (L23), failoit (L32). This conflicts with the brief's ∂ = z (confirmed on fr. 16107 elsewhere). Either two signs that look alike to me at this resolution, or ∂ is l/z homophone. Flag for the main thread.
- ‖ (two verticals with crossbar) = f: force (L33), failoit (L32), fille (L23). Not in Mousset's f row.
- ∂S (∂ joined to large S/3) = Prince (Mousset's word sign): "si grand et puissant prince" (L32); also L31 and 59v L08 before "successeur".
- ꝗ: at least two signs. With short tail: y (luy L21, L33; roy L10; envoya L07). With long crossed stem: p (pouvoient L13, pretenseurs L13, peust L16, plus L20, portoit L27). Also q in "quelle" (L22), "quel" (L08). I could not separate them reliably.
- "8": before successeur twice (L31; 59v L08) and in "pour/par estre" (L26). Value unresolved (par?); cc in succession (L14), successeur (L31).
- "~": h in duchesse (L17, L21, L28), catholicque; a or null elsewhere (L20 "de ~raison").
- L07 dotted sign after "quant de": read as Espagne (·ħ·) by shape and sense; doubtful.
- L23 first sign of "fille" may be # (g) with dots, not ‖.

## Summary
The ambassadors (of the Catholic King, Philip II) and the "lesdicts embassadeurs" write; the writer has done "toutes les diligences" (every effort) to learn what is really going on.
Spain sent someone on its behalf to the assembly of the Estates (Portuguese Cortes); the King's council is to consider right and justice and what all the pretenders could claim to the succession of the crown of Portugal.
It is reported that the claims of the Catholic King and of the Duchess of Braganza exclude all the others, so the only question is which of the two has more right and reason.
The Duchess's advantage is that she is the daughter of a man who would have been king; the Catholic King's is that his mother was the sister, and that he is a man and older.
The conclusion reported is that the Catholic King is so great and powerful a prince that one cannot think of coming to force against him.
