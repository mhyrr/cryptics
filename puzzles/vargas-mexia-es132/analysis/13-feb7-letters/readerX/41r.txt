## New or doubtful signs
- ∂ (d with flourish): ll in L11 (Andallouzie), L16, L23 (conseiller), L25 (intelligences); z only in L13 "assez". Brief's value z is doubtful for this letter.
- ꝗ (q with crossed stem): y in L04 (envoye), L28 (François); n in L23 (nouvelle). Doubtful.
- ⴖ ("11" with a cap): f in L09 (fort), L14 (feit), L19 (faisoit), L28 (François). Not in either key as drawn; close to Mousset's U-box f.
- Ⱨ / # (H with two bars): g in L06, L10, L12, L21, L23. Proposed g.
- ʃʃʃ (triple long stroke): t in L16 (n'est). Proposed t.
- ρ (long curl, open loop): ss in L08, L10, L14, L15. Proposed ss.
- ⴗ-like sign in L25 between n and ll: guess "te" (intelligences). Guessed.
- "\" stroke in L30 after o: null.
- L22 opening "[que] ? r a n c i s": the first sign after que may be f, giving a name ("Francis…"); not fixed.

## Summary
The writer reports that an envoy was sent to ask leave to carry out an enterprise "de tres grande importance"; great armies are said to be assembling in Andalusia with a very great guard on the coasts.
He recounts the caresses shown at first to the ambassador of the Roy Catholicque (Philip II), and that, as is said, he is now held prisoner.
Someone is said to be sending to Constantinople to have advice of the Grand Seigneur (the Ottoman sultan).
A member of the council of war has had news; "Lanssac" (Lansac) is named twice, with intelligences and a claim that if he had been believed something would have been taken.
François (the French) are mentioned near the foot; the page ends "par ce que ceulx qui…".
