## New or doubtful signs
- α (open "fish", Mousset's o) reads e on this page: L02 "a i r [α] s" = affaires, L04 "[qui] α s … tout" = qui est. Proposed e here; o elsewhere still possible.
- ȝ (3 with long tail) = i in L02, L05, L06 (affaires, -is); in L08 the same-looking sign after "a" reads rr (arrive). Doubtful; two signs may be confused.
- ∂ (d with flourish): reads ll in L09 "nouvelle", not z as the brief has it. Doubtful.
- Large 8 with top flourish and dot (L02, L06): Portugal word sign? context does not fix it. Left as [8-flourish].
- ʌ small caret after "a" in L08 (apporte): null or a-variant.
- "ɤ8"/"18" read as null throughout.

## Summary
The King's letter turns to cipher after a clear passage on the Spanish ministers' treatment of French subjects on the coasts of Andalusia.
It speaks of "affaires qui retardent" (affairs that are delaying) and of something "a la Reine" (to the Queen).
A sentence opens "Quant a leurs armees" (as for their armies) and refers to "les affaires … commencees" and "termes".
It reports that a courier has arrived who brought news ("le courrier est arrive qui a apporte la nouvelle").
No person other than the Queen is named on this page in what I could read; place names not recovered.
