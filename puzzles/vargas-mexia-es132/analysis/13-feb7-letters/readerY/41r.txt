## New or doubtful signs
- alpha (α, without the z-tail) = e, not o: L04 "demand-er", L05; also 40v "affaires". Reported as a change from Mousset.
- 3-with-p-tail and hooked z (ʓ) treated as one sign = i: L05 "entreprise", L06 "importence", passim.
- ɑʒ (alpha followed by tailed 3, one sign?): = q in L18 "a quoy", L23 "auquelle"; = j? in L30 "tousjours". Doubtful.
- ϑ (theta loop): s in L16 "pa[s]", l/t elsewhere (L23, L29). Unresolved.
- triple long-s (ꞩꞩꞩ): null in L10 ("alarme [·] pour"), t in L16 "n'est". Doubtful.
- crossed q with long descender: y in L04 "envoye", L18-19 "luy"; n in L17 "prisonnier". Two signs may be confused.
- Ħ / U-box: f in L09 "fort", L19 "faisoit", L22 "Francisque", L28 "François"; g in L10/L12 "grandes", "garde". Two shapes I could not separate reliably.

## Summary
Mentions the rest of "l'armee" (the army) and an "entreprise de tres grande importance" (enterprise of great importance), "en la Moree" (in the Morea).
The cherif (sharif of Morocco) "a pris fort grande alarme" (has taken great alarm) at the great armies assembling in Andalusia ("l'Andallouzie") and keeps a great guard on his coasts.
His caresses to "l'ambassadeur du roy catholicque" (Philip II's ambassador); it is said, not certain, that he holds him prisoner since he asked him for Larache.
He answered he would send to Constantinople for the Grand Seigneur's advice; Francisque d'Ivarre (a Spanish councillor) and Lanssac (Guy de Lansac?) are named.
Lanssac is said to claim Larache could have been taken; persons: the cherif, Philip II's ambassador, Lanssac, Francisque d'Ivarre; places: Andalusia, Constantinople, Larache, the Morea.
