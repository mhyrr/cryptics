## New or doubtful signs
- Three/four-bar crossed sign (⧺, like Mousset's second s "‡‡"): s in `assemblee` L23, `choses` L07 (cf. 60r). Different from the three-upright "quant" sign (L32), which is confirmed by `quant a ce qui touchoit`.
- Theta-like sign: r in `retourna` L08, l in `allé` L10 — the same doubt as 60r.
- Double-bar "‖": f in `fort` L04, `fois` L11, `felicité` L19.
- "Y-fork" family: p in `desplaisir` L12, m in `moyens` L29 and `mectre` L06; u/v elsewhere. By context.
- Tall double loop "Ꝺδ / δδ": null in `inclinerent` L24 and before `il` L33 (agrees with brief's δb = null); I read it as d once (L20, `d'a...`), doubtful.
- "4 with a z-shaped foot" (L21 `che?rcher`): extra r or null. Same sign opens 59v L01 and 60r L01, value unknown.
- Triple-stroke "§§§" (L26 `troisiesme`, L31 `vivre`): read s / null; doubtful.
- Plain "8": `pour` in `pourveoir` L14; unexplained in L05, L26.
- L05–L07 and L11 are poorly understood; glyphs transcribed as seen.

## Summary
The assembly's opposition is described: hearing the prelate's harangue, they raised loud cries against their king and against him.
The prelate went back to his master and reported how the assembly had gone; the King [of Portugal] was displeased that they were so stirred against his good will to provide for their welfare and security.
After another long harangue, he told them that in conscience he believed the succession belonged to the King Catholic (Philip II), and that this was their whole welfare and happiness; he begged them not to seek their own total ruin.
At the second sitting the opinions were divided, many inclining to the king's counsel; at the third, they all but resolved to follow his will and to treat with the King Catholic on terms honest and bearable enough to live contentedly under so great a king.
The page ends on what the king said about the claim of the Duchess of Bragança.
