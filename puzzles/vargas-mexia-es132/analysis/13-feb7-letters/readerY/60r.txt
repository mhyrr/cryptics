## New or doubtful signs
- Big looped "L" flourish (ℒ): que (L03, L04, L06, L14, L16, L21, L25...). Distinct from the Z-with-hook (Ƶ), which is ff in `effect` (L04).
- Tall "7"-shape with long stem (ꟻ): read J'ai (Mousset) at L03, L04; fits "j'ai trouvé que".
- "o with a long cross stem" (ⱦ): o throughout (`roy`, `moi[s]`, `couronne`, `voyoit`).
- Closed theta / ∂-like sign (θ, ϑ): l in `diligences` L03, `diligement` L12, `seulement` L19, `vouloir` L19; but z in `petiz`, `grandz`, `repoz` (L10). Two signs probably look alike at this resolution: closed theta = l, open ∂-with-flourish = z.
- Curl with tail (ϱ): s, but m in `assemblee` L07 and `sembloit` L21 (the second of a pair). Possibly a second curl for m.
- "Y-fork" family (Ϥ, ꝑ, ꝗ): u/v in `longue harengue` L09, `voyoit` L15; p in `pretenseurs` L13, `plus` L20, `portoit` L22; y in `envoya` L07, `luy` L21/L33. I could not separate the fork variants reliably; values are by context.
- Glyph ꝓ / ρ (long-tailed s-curl): ss in `duchesse` L17, `ambassadeurs` L05.
- "#" double cross: g in `grand` L32, `diligences`; but f in `failoit` L32 and `fille` L23 by sense. Doubtful.
- Tall flourished "g·" sign: Portugal (L07, L14).
- "ꝺδ / δδ" pair (L15, L30, before "...ine conscience"): unknown; `sa[ine]` by sense.
- Plain "8" (L26 before `estre`): unexplained, possibly null.
- L01 and L02 are poorly read (white ghosts across band 01); many guesses there.

## Summary
The writer says he made every effort to learn the facts, and found that the ambassadors (of the King Catholic) write that on the 18th of this month the King of Portugal sent a prelate to the assembly of the Estates.
After a long harangue on the king's care for the welfare and repose of his subjects, the prelate asked them to weigh diligently the right and justice of all the pretenders to the succession of the crown of Portugal.
In conscience the king saw only one claim worth weighing besides those of the King Catholic and the Duchess of Bragança, and wished only to determine which of the two had more right and reason.
If the duchess had the advantage of being the daughter of a man who would have been king, the King Catholic was the son of the king's sister and an older man, and so had a large advantage over the duchess.
The council's opinion was that the King Catholic was so great and powerful a prince that one must not think of resorting to force against him.
