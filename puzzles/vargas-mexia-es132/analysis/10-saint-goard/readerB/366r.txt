## New or doubtful signs

- Loop-plus-δ sign (large open loop joined to a small δ; Tomokiyo's null "Ωδ", also near Mousset's d "ꝺb"): read as NULL in L01, L02, L03; the value d would break "en regions", "a trouver", "et la". (L03 instance read null; with d it would give "occasion de t la".)
- ɑ-with-tail (Mousset's third a): reads as NULL here, L02, L03, L04, L06, L07; as a it gives "aoccasion", "d'auen-ataige", "sante a et".
- Swash "ɤp"/"tp" sign (Tomokiyo null "ep"): NULL, L03, L04, L05, L06, L07.
- "18"/"ɿ8": NULL (Tomokiyo), throughout.
- Y-shaped p with hooked foot (looks like Cyrillic ч): p, L02, L04, L05, L06 (puise, aplicque, prier, prosperite). Probably a cursive form of Mousset's Y.
- Same shape with a crossbar on the stem: m, L07 ("monarque") = Mousset's first m.
- Crossed δ (δ with horizontal stroke through it): u, L02, L03, L05 (puise, trouver, pouvant, du tout, Dieu). Probably Mousset's "-◇-".
- Plain "s"-shaped sign: m, L01, L04, L07 (remottes, m'aplicque, monde) = Mousset's fourth m.
- U-shaped open box: f, L06 ("face") = Mousset's f "⊔".
- q with crossbar: y, L05 ("-ray").
- "8" (bold): cc in L03 ("occasion"); in L05 it is followed by "sa" and read [Par] ("pour sa"?) — doubtful; a slashed 8 in L07 also read [Par], sense unclear.
- L07: after the [que] sign, a sign like A whose stem runs down as a 4 (possibly the tail of [que]); value unknown, left ?.
- ℘-shaped s (Tomokiyo s): L02 "puise".
- L01/L02 "et os lo u · aignees": sense suggests "esloignees"; the glyphs as read give extra o/u/a. Left as read.

## Summary

The cipher on 366r is the last seven lines of the letter: Saint-Goard says he is posted in regions so remote and far off that he cannot find occasion to serve Henri III further.
Unable to do more for the King's service, he will give himself entirely to praying God for the King's prosperity and health,
and that God make him "monarque de tout le monde" (monarch of the whole world).
Clear closing: he asks the Creator to grant His Majesty growth of his states, health and a long life; dated Madrid, [23] December 1579.
Signed "De Vivonne" (Jean de Vivonne, seigneur de Saint-Goard) as the King's very humble and obedient subject and servant; docket "23 decembre".
