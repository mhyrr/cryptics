## New or doubtful signs
- Circle on a short stem (φ-like; Mousset lists φ under c and a boxed T-sign under p): read p here, fixed by
  "les plus prochains" (L28-L29, twice: "p l u s", "p r o · c h a") and "-p o r" (L26, L27), "p e ? p l e" (L30). Doubtful: it may be the
  same sign as Mousset's second p written round. If so Mousset's c "φ" needs checking against this hand.
- Epsilon with a long crossed descender: m by context ("marines" L20, L29; "marcher" L24; "marie" L28). Mousset's first m
  (a crossed y-shape) is the nearest match; flagged because the shape is closer to r "ε" with a stem.
- Hook-over-8: taken as the Tomokiyo null; it falls inside words ("f a · i c t" L22, "l i · e u l x" L29, "p r o · c h a" L28),
  which supports null. It is the commonest sign on the page. Lines: every cipher line.
- "ze" (Mousset "Fait, quelquefois nul"): read null at L15, L16, L19; "fait" does not fit at any of them.
- Rho-with-tail (looped s-sign, Tomokiyo's s/ss variants): read s in "grossissant" (L18); also L26-L29. Could be ss.
- H with a dot or loop inside: read b (Mousset b, boxed H) at L21, L23, L25, L29; t (plain H) is possible. Doubtful.
- Arch with feet (Ω): h, by "marcher" L24. Matches Mousset h.
- ∂ with a b-bowl (Mousset d "∂b"): read d, but L30 "p e ? p l e" wants u. Doubtful. Lines L15, L21, L30.
- Double-cross (# / ‡‡): g, in "grossissant" L18; also L17, L21, L25, L26.
- ∞ vs σo: both look like two joined loops; read ∞ = l and open σo/oo = d. The two are not always separable (L16, L21, L25).

## Summary
Saint-Goard to Henri III, Madrid, 23 December 1579. In clear: he wrote on the 3rd of the month; the Catholic King and Queen are well and in Madrid.
Six days ago he had an audience, about the French merchants trading at Bilbao and a further matter (a guerre/affaire "de [F]eltz", reading uncertain).
Philip II answered graciously and said he would order it settled justly; Saint-Goard believes the King means it but doubts his ministers will follow.
He will press as hard as he can and report. The cipher then begins (L15). Read fragments: "menace", "c'est", "en grossissant", "leurs pr[…]".
The cipher appears to concern troops and the coast: "trois mil [ho]mmes", "marcher", "les plus prochains lieulx des marines", "forcer le peuple". No personal names read in the cipher.
