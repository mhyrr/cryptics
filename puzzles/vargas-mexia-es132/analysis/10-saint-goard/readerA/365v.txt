## New or doubtful signs

- Hook-topped "i8" (a reversed-hook stroke joined to a figure 8). Extremely frequent, often
  between letters of a word read cleanly on both sides (L08 "travers·e", L16 "evne·ment").
  Read as a null (Tomokiyo's "~8"/"18" null column). Lines: every line.
- "ʒꝭ" (a 3 with a looped p-like tail). Frequent; drops out of every clean word. Read as null
  (Tomokiyo "ꝗe"). Lines: most lines, e.g. L03, L12, L18, L21, L24.
- "ϱ" (open c-loop with a long descending tail, not the small-ring "s" of Mousset). Read as s
  from context: L08 "traverse", L22 "est oubl…", L23 "Sebastien", L31 "serve". Lines as listed.
- Small-ringed c with a straight stem below (looks like a 9 or Mousset s-first). Proposed b
  (Mousset b first, "9") from L11 "cembre"; doubtful, since it gives nonsense in L07, L32.
- "∂" (reversed-6 / delta-like stroke). Proposed n in L16 ("evenement"); same sign in L13, L20,
  L25 where n is not obviously right. Doubtful; Tomokiyo lists a similar shape as a null.
- Star-shaped glyph (x with extra cross strokes). Not matched in either key; left '?' throughout
  (L04, L05, L06, L26, L29 region).
- Large tall-stemmed "ʃ" with a hook at top (distinct from double-ʃ "§" = t). In L08 it stands
  where "t" is needed ("[t]raverse"); left as '?' elsewhere (L09, L13, L14, L20).
- Capital-E / Ŧ-like sign with cross-bar: read y (Mousset y second "E"), L29 "s'y", L32 "que y";
  doubtful in L32.
- "Et" (ɛ with a long crossed stem): unknown; in L06 it stands before "on" after [que] ("que l'on"
  would make it l); in L32 it gives nonsense as l or r. Left as '?' where it occurs.
- "#" (double-barred H, ‡‡): read g once (L14 "ge de vo…"), elsewhere left '?'; may be the
  Tomokiyo null "#".

## Summary

Reader A could not get a continuous reading of 365v; only short stretches decode, mostly at line ends.
Clear fragments: "je trouve" (L02), "traverse" (L08), "pour deçà" (L09), "[dé]cembre" (L11), "événement" (L16).
Also readable: "est oubli[é]" (L22), "Sébastien" (L23), and "vouldra que la serve…" (L31).
The only person named is Sébastien (presumably King Sebastian of Portugal); no place name or word sign for a country was found.
31% of the column-2 tokens are '?', and that undercounts the problem: in the dense left parts of the lines, glyph counts are approximate and many letters are low-confidence. The page needs a second reader before any content claim rests on it.
