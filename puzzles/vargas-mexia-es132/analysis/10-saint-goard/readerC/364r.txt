## New or doubtful signs
- Single heavy S-curve (ʃ) without the doubled stroke: t in L31 ("peult"); read t in L01 ("cest"). Doubtful in L01. L01, L31.
- Small "x" with a long hooked tail, after "pou": proposed rr ("pourra"). Only in L05. Doubtful.
- "=" with a vertical stem (∓, "two bars on a staff"): reads p or pp in "dep-pend" (L07), "a pro-pos" (L15, L18); in L12 it stands where u/n is needed ("bo?e", "vol?nté"). Value p/pp proposed; L12 unresolved.
- "τι" followed by a small b-loop in L02 (between "place" and "est"): unread (?). Possibly a qui-variant.
- "k-with-cross" (ꝁ) beside the star in L06 ("vouldra" needs o) and at L07 start (needs L of Lisbonne) and in L34 ("entend?" needs u): value not fixed. L06, L07, L34.
- Alpha with long underline (∝ + L) in L09 before "face": que? or par/pour?; context wants "que je". The following star-sign would then be i/j (doubtful). L09.
- Long free vertical strokes (often crossing a glyph) in L13 ("ε|", "|x", "|3ρ"): treated as nulls or as tall m; L13 "comme" needs one m from them.
- Hooked tall ʃ before "a" in L28 ("[s]avoir"): proposed s; and the triple tall stroke after "voir" (L28): unread.
- Seated "8" with a loop under (ʂ) after "don Anthoine de" in L23: Portugal sign without tilde? Doubtful. Also a plain "8" before "dont" in L07: read as null.
- ϑ (9 with loop) = l (Mousset's third l) confirmed in L10 (particulier), L11 (celle), L23 (elle), L27 (qu'elle), L34 (elle).
- Crossed q (ꝗ): y in "j'auray", "luy", "ay", "moy", "qu'il y", "voaye"; m in "majesté", "moy", "m'escript", "mende". Two values, partly distinguishable by a small loop on the m form. L09, L13–L17, L19, L21, L24–L26.
- z-with-tail (ʓ/ʐ) = rr in "Pierre" (L22, L30), "verra" (L23), "marri" (L24); elsewhere a null ("ze") at line ends (L03, L04, L33, L34).
- ∂ (looped d-shape) = s in "Cascais" (L02), "apropos" (L15, L18); null-like elsewhere.
- The "ɕo" crossed star = u (jusques L32, plus L19); the 4-armed star = u/v (tout L02, vouldra L06, sceusse L16, venir L15).

## Summary
Saint-Goard says a man (don Antonio's party?) is determined; there is a place, Cascais, which is the key to the entrance of the river of Lisbon, on which all Portugal depends, and whoever holds it can act in safety.
He awaits Henri III's pleasure on what to answer, and will write a word to don Antonio (Anthoine) so that he keeps his good will and does nothing until told.
He thought it unwise to meet don Antonio without knowing the King's wishes, and sees inconvenience until the King decides what is for his service.
Pierre Dor has written to him about the infante don Antonio, as the King will see from the same letter, which he could not forward by a quicker route; the King should decide soon.
If possible Pierre Dor should keep don Antonio in hand until the King commands his will; the page breaks on "the usurpation" (continued on 364v). Names: don Antonio (Anthoine), Pierre Dor, Cascais, Lisbon, Portugal.
