## Summary
Saint-Goard reports that the King and nobility of Portugal want Philip II ("le Roi catholicque") as their king, and the King of Portugal is said to want it too (L01–02).
Someone who opposes it "se pourra aysement" be won over; "douziesme" (the twelfth, perhaps a date) recurs in L06 and L14.
The middle lines (L08–L20) are only partly read. They mention earnest requests ("tres grandes instences"), an answer given "par escript", a congé, Portugal being strung along until a "resolution", and something done "par force".
The Portuguese "despeschent courriers" (send couriers) daily and none has come back, which surprises them. Don Anthoine (de Castre?, presumably Dom António, Prior of Crato) is named in L27.
Saint-Goard thought it more reasonable to send the same letter, and is sure the person concerned is "grandement affectionné" to the service of Henri III ("vostre Majesté", L32, L37).
