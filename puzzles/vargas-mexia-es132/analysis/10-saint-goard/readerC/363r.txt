## Summary

Saint-Goard writes from Madrid (23 Dec 1579) that he had an audience six days earlier with Philip II ("le Roy Catholicque") about poor French subjects trading at Bilbao and the "Camarin" affair.
Philip received the complaints well and promised to settle them, but his ministers do not follow through; Saint-Goard will keep pressing and report.
The cipher begins with Spanish preparations "plus que jamais": provisions and "appareil de guerre" on the frontiers and the maritime districts, connected with Portugal and with "des [pré]tendans qui sont trois" (the Portuguese claimants).
Troops are to march "la volte" (towards Portugal); ships to embark at Cartagena(?); galleys and Doria are named; he was assured of something at "Calis" (Cádiz) and El Puerto de Santa María, "les plus prochains lieux des marines de Portugal".
The page ends on rumour ("le broit") and "forcer le peuple v-", which continues on 363v. Names: Henri III, Philip II, Bilbao, Doria, Cartagena(?), Cádiz ("Calis"), Port Sainte Marie, Portugal.
