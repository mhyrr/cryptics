# Philip II and Antonio Pérez to Vargas Mexía, 1577–1580 (BnF Espagnol 132)

**Catalog entry:** `catalog/entries/charles-v-unsolved-cipher-letters.md` (the es. 132 row).
The folder is named for the volume because the dive covers only es. 132.
**Status:** opened 2026-10-03 · keys published by Tomokiyo (2020); no full reading published.
First target: f. 198–199, Antonio Pérez to Vargas Mexía, Madrid, 15 April 1579 (Cipher 4).

## Brief
BnF Espagnol 132 is the ambassador Juan de Vargas Mexía's file of letters from Philip II
and Antonio Pérez, December 1577 to May 1580: about 129 items, many partly or wholly in
cipher. Tomokiyo identified the four ciphers in 2020 (one cracked by him, three matched to
keys printed by Devos 1950) and wrote that this "will allow historians to decipher all the
letters in the volume". Nobody has published that reading. The letters run through the
Escobedo murder (31 March 1578) and Pérez's fall (arrest 28 July 1579). F. 198, Pérez's own
letter of 15 April 1579, reportedly names Escobedo and the Marqués de los Vélez and speaks of
"mi inocencia" and "falso testimonio". The dive's job is a full, key-consistent, checkable
reading of the cipher, starting with f. 198, then the rest of the volume.

## What a solution would have to do
1. Decode every cipher token of a letter by one published key, applied in code to a
   transcription that two independent readers produced from the Gallica images. No token is
   assigned a value by context.
2. Report every token the key does not cover (unknown codes, doubtful marks) as unknown, with
   its position. Do not fill gaps silently.
3. The decoded passages must read as continuous period Spanish and join grammatically to the
   clear text on either side of each cipher run. The clear text is the check written into the
   letter itself.
4. A value added beyond Tomokiyo's tables (a new code word, a cluster) must hold at every
   occurrence in the volume, not only where it was found.
5. Any historical claim (Escobedo, los Vélez, Pérez's defence) rests on the full decoded text
   of the passage, quoted with its token positions, never on selected words.
6. Where a letter is summarized or printed elsewhere (catalogues of 1844/1847, CODOIN, Pérez's
   *Relaciones*, Mignet), the reading must agree with it.

## Scope of this dive
First: f. 198–199 (Cipher 4), with two blind transcriptions and Tomokiyo's key. Then: the other
Cipher 4 letters (September 1578 – April 1579), then Ciphers 1–3. Out of scope for now: an
edition of the clear text of the volume.
