# French court replies to Saint-Goard, Jan–Apr 1580: search of printed letters

Searched 2026-11-10. The question was whether Henri III or Catherine de Médicis answered Saint-Goard's cipher
letter of 23 Dec 1579. That letter forwarded the letters of Dom António de Castro, lord of Cascais, and reported the
approach from Dom António, prior of Crato, made through Pierre Dor. The search covered 1 Jan – 30 Apr 1580 and
looked for any reply on Cascais, Castro, Dom António, or "les affaires de Portugal".

**Result: the printed letters contain no reply on Cascais or Castro.** No printed letter of Catherine in this window
names Cascais, Dom António de Castro, Dom António of Crato, or Pierre Dor. Her only letter to Saint-Goard in the
window (27 Mar 1580) is a short acknowledgement. Brémond d'Ars paraphrases one royal line in the window: Henri III's
"resolution, which was not one" to give the Portuguese quiet favour without declaring himself. Brémond knows it only
as it is echoed in Saint-Goard's own letters, and it says nothing about Cascais.

Method: OCR text searched with Python for Gouard/Goard, Portugal, Cascai(s/es), Castro, Anthoine/Antonio, Crato,
Dor, Lisbonne. The OCR is poor in places, so a miss is not proof of absence. That applies above all to two-column
pages, where letter text is garbled.

## 1. Lettres de Catherine de Médicis, ed. Baguenault de Puchesse, vol. 7 (1579–1581)

URL: https://archive.org/details/lettresdecatheri07cathuoft (OCR djvu.txt). Tier: PRIMARY (critical edition).

**(a) Catherine to Saint-Goard, 27 March 1580.** Minute, BnF fr. 16107 f. 179v. Vol. 7 pp. 231–232. This is the only
letter to Saint-Goard printed between 1 Jan and 30 Apr 1580. The OCR of p. 232 is badly garbled. My reading of the
legible parts follows; the words in brackets are reconstructed from the garbled OCR.

> "Monsieur de Saint-Goard, j'ay, grâce à Dieu, recouvert mon entière santé et convales[cence] … [du] Roy
> catholicque s'en allant à Guadeloupe et vous prie continuer à nous advertir de toutte occurrence et mesme de la
> réception des lettres que Longlée vous a portées, et de celle que nous avons escripte le [xiii?] de ce mois par le
> marchand, par lequel vous nous avez adverty du décès du feu roy de Portugal, que Dieu absolve…"

English: "Monsieur de Saint-Goard, thank God I have recovered my full health and convalescence … the Catholic King
going to Guadalupe, and I ask you to keep informing us of every occurrence, and in particular of the receipt of the
letters that Longlée brought you, and of the one we wrote on the [13th?] of this month by the merchant, by whom you
informed us of the death of the late king of Portugal, God absolve him…"

Nothing in it about Cascais, Castro, Dom António or Dor. It also points to two earlier royal letters to Saint-Goard
that are not printed: letters carried by Longlée, and a letter of "the [13th?] of this month" sent by a merchant.
Those letters, or the King's own dispatches, are where a reply would be, if one was made.

**(b) Editor's note to (a), p. 231 n. 1.** The minutes to Saint-Goard begin again in 1580, after a gap from
1 Apr 1578. Saint-Goard's letters to Catherine survive for 31 Jan, 7 and 20 Feb, 2, 8 and 20 Mar, and later dates
(fr. 16107). Extract from 7 Feb: "Quant à ce qui se présente du voiage qu'est près de faire en Séville le roy
catholicque, pour donner challeur aux affaires de Portugal…" (English: "As for the journey the Catholic King is about
to make to Seville, to give heat to the affairs of Portugal…").

**(c) Appendix XX: Saint-Goard to Catherine, Madrid, 20 Feb 1580** (fr. 16107). Vol. 7, pp. ~620–621 (page numbers
from OCR, approximate). This is an outgoing letter from Saint-Goard, not a reply. He sends the sieur de la Rocque to
the King with a dispatch on "l'estat auquel sont les affaires de Portugal depuis la mort du roy don Henricque"
(English: "the state of the affairs of Portugal since the death of King Henrique"). He then writes: "il n'y a temps à
perdre à qui les vouldra favoriser" (English: "there is no time to lose for whoever wants to favour them"). He does not
mention Cascais or Castro. The phrasing implies he had no instruction yet.

**(d) Other Portugal items in the window, none of them to Saint-Goard:**
- Catherine to d'Abain (Rome), 28 Feb 1580, p. 228: "vous recommander ce qui me concerne en Portugal" (English: "to
  commend to you what concerns me in Portugal"), and she asks him to sound out the Pope. This is Catherine's own claim
  to the throne.
- Editor's note to a letter to du Ferrier (Venice), 18 Mar 1580, p. ~230: France "avait résolu de soutenir" (English:
  "had resolved to support") the five governors of Portugal, and asked Venice to lobby. Only the editor's note was read
  here; the letter text was not checked.
- Introduction, p. ~xxx: the editor quotes Saint-Goard calling Pierre Dor a "petit brouilleur et finot" (English: "a
  petty meddler and a sly one").

## 3. Brémond d'Ars, *Le père de Madame de Rambouillet, Jean de Vivonne* (Paris: Plon, 1884)

URL: https://archive.org/details/lepredemadamede00dargoog (OCR djvu.txt). Tier: SCHOLARLY. Page numbers are taken
from OCR running heads and may be off by one.

- **pp. ~129–130.** A different Castro: **Don Rodrigo de Castro**, whom Brémond calls 4th count of Lemos and cousin of
  the duke of Braganza. He came to the embassy at night and masked, on Braganza's behalf. Brémond's sources are
  Saint-Goard to Henri III, 7 Feb and 16 May 1580 (fr. 16107). Brémond does not mention Dom António de Castro of
  Cascais or Cascais itself; the OCR has no hit for "Cascai".
- **p. ~130.** When the Portuguese ambassador in Madrid asked for French help, "Saint-Gouard ne put répondre que par
  des paroles évasives : les Valois n'étaient pas disposés à se compromettre encore". English: "Saint-Gouard could
  answer only with evasive words: the Valois were not yet willing to commit themselves." Source cited: Saint-Goard to
  Henri III, 7 Feb 1580.
- **p. ~132.** This is the only royal answer Brémond reports for the window:
  > "Henri III lui fit part de sa résolution, qui n'en était pas une, « de donner à la sourde faveur aux Portugais,
  > ne se déclarant que aultant qu'il verroyt luy venir le plus à propoz »."

  English: "Henri III told him of his resolution, which was not one: 'to give quiet favour to the Portuguese,
  declaring himself only so far as he should see it most to his advantage.'" "Sourde" is my reading of the OCR's
  "souitle". The nearest footnote is Saint-Goard to Henri III, 3 Apr 1580 (fr. 16107), so the King's letter is known
  only through Saint-Goard's reply. The King's own text was not found in print.
- **p. ~131.** Saint-Goard's view of Pierre Dor: "entièrement gagné par don Antonio" (English: "entirely won over by
  Dom Antonio"). Sources: Pierre Dor to Saint-Goard, 10 Feb 1580, and Saint-Goard to Henri III, 18 May 1580.

## Could not check

- *Lettres de Henri III*, vols. IV–V: not online.
- BnF fr. 16107: the King's minutes and dispatches to Saint-Goard for Jan–Apr 1580. This includes the letters carried
  by Longlée and the letter of "the [13th?]" of March sent by a merchant, both referred to in the letter of 27 Mar.
  Gallica was not tried, as instructed.
- Vol. 7 p. 232: a clean page image is needed to confirm the garbled lines of the 27 Mar letter.
- Any letter from the court to Pierre Dor in 1580: none is printed in vol. 7.

## Unverified claims

- That a royal reply on Cascais or Castro existed and was lost. This is neither shown nor ruled out. The printed
  record is consistent with a deliberately non-committal court.
- Brémond's identification of Don Rodrigo de Castro as count of Lemos was not checked against another source.
