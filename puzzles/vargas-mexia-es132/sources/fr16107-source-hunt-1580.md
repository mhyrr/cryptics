# BnF Français 16107, Jan–Mar 1580: does Saint-Goard name his source?

Source: BnF Français 16107, Gallica ark `btv1b9009661v`. Tier: PRIMARY. Session 2026-11-10.
Full-size spreads fetched to `sources/cache/fr16107/F{18,22,23,28,36,88,90,92,116,134,142,102}.jpg`; read from crops (bands at native resolution for ff. 18–19, whole-page skims at about 1/3 scale for the rest).
Transcription is a working reading, not an edition. Spelling as written; `[?]` = doubtful word; `…` = not read.

**Bottom line: a source is described but not named.** Twice in the letters to Villeroy, Saint-Goard credits "un amy", once "fort confident" (13 Jan) and once "de tres bonne qualité … et qui manie de bonnes afaires" (20 Feb), the second of whom tipped him about the Spanish king's printed book, a copy of which he "recovered". He names no clerk and gives no payment, pension or reward to an informant. "Despanse" appears only in his offer to pay for a counter-printing. He names Çayas and Idiáquez, but as officials he complained to about his opened packet, not as sources. Pérez and Éboli appear once (13 Jan).

## Passages bearing on the question

**1. f. 18v, 13 Jan 1580, to Villeroy: an "amy fort confident" reports what his note did inside the Spanish court.** Context: the French prisoners held in the court's prisons. He wrote a *billet* to Philip II asking for a particular judge.
FR: "Un amy fort cofidant me vient d'avertir que mon billet a heu telle forse qu'il a faict metre la main sur la consiense [?] de maniere que le Roy catholique a commandé qu'il fust prouveu [?] en ceste afaire de maniere que j'y eusse satisfaction, sur quoy il a esté deliberé que l'on envoiroit en toute diligense" (continues f. 19r:) "pour que l'on ne usast poinct de ceste premiere provision mais qu'au lieu d'icelle l'on sortist les prisonniers soubz fiance".
EN: "A very trusted friend has just told me that my note carried such weight that it made [them] put a hand on [their] conscience [?], so that the Catholic King ordered that the matter be provided for in a way that would satisfy me. On which it was decided to send in all haste that the first order should not be used, and that the prisoners should instead be released on surety."
Shows: an informant who knows what the King ordered and what was then decided. Not named.

**2. f. 85r, 20 Feb 1580, to Villeroy: "un amy … qui manie de bonnes afaires", and a book "recouvert".**
FR: "Monsieur, comme j'ay [?] eu fermé toutes mes despesches, un amy que j'ay de tres bonne calité et tres avisé, et qui manie de bonnes afaires, m'est venu visiter, lequel m'a avisé que l'on a trouvé ysi que le livre que le Roy catholique a faict imprimer de sa pretantion est du tout bastant a declarer [?] que sad. pretantion est tres mal fondée, et que pour se ils ont deliberé de bruler les troes cans qu'ils ont faict imprimer, et que jusques a ceste heure ils ont tenus si cachés et serrés, croiant asurement qu'il n'y a homme qui en ait jamais rien veu ne oui parler. Toutefois j'ay heu de si bons avis que j'en avoest recouvert un, que je vous envoie avecque une despesche que je adrosse au gouverneur de Baionne le 7e de janvier …" Later: "je seroes d'avis que pour leur fere un bon tour l'on en fist imprimer quelque nombre soubz le nom du prince d'Orange ou de seux de Geneve, et pour moy … je vanderes plustost jusques a ma chemise pour en fere la despanse".
EN: "Sir, when I had closed all my dispatches, a friend of mine of very good quality, very shrewd, who handles important business, came to see me. He told me that it has been found here that the book the Catholic King had printed on his claim [to Portugal] is enough to show that the claim is very ill-founded, and that they have therefore decided to burn the three hundred copies printed, which they have kept so hidden and locked up until now that they are sure no one has ever seen or heard of it. All the same, I had such good intelligence that I had recovered one, which I send you with a dispatch I addressed to the governor of Bayonne on 7 January … I would advise that, to play them a good trick, some copies be printed under the name of the Prince of Orange or of the Genevans; for my part … I would sooner sell my very shirt to pay the cost."
Shows: "recouvert" used of a Spanish state document obtained before 7 Jan 1580; an informant "qui manie de bonnes afaires", a phrase for an official or someone in business. Cost: none stated for the source. The "despanse" is for reprinting.

**3. f. 83r, 20 Feb 1580, to Villeroy: Çayas and Idiáquez as officials, about his opened packet.**
FR: "… [j'ay] trouvé enfin que c'estoit la miene du 7e en laquelle je advisoes de la mort du Roy de Portugal, et … l'on asure que l'on l'avoit ouverte et atanté [?] de la deschiffrer, et que n'y aiant peu mordre l'on l'avoit refermée et r'envoiée. Et aiant entandu tout seci j'ay ausi tost envoié une grande querelle a don Ioan de Hidiaques et a Caias, aux mains duquel elle est venue … r'envoiant fort et ferme pour la seconde fois a Caias luy fere une recharge … il n'a seu nier, et mandé que c'estoit une sotise qu'avoit faict le corrigidor de Bourgues …"
EN: "… I found at last that it was mine of the 7th, reporting the King of Portugal's death, and … I am assured [?] it was opened and they tried to decipher it, and having been unable to get into it they closed it again and sent it back. On hearing all this I at once sent a strong complaint to Don Juan de Idiáquez and to Çayas, into whose hands it came … sending a second time to Çayas … he could not deny it, and said it was a stupidity of the corregidor of Burgos …"
f. 111r (2 Mar) returns to this: "l'on me asure qu'elles ne [furent] ouvertes; si ne le croies pas pour l'avis que j'ay au contraire"; "j'ay grand peur que Mondragon en ait esté le laron [?]". EN: "I am assured they were not opened; I do not believe it, because of the information I have to the contrary"; "I am much afraid Mondragón was the thief [?]."

**4. f. 87r, 20 Feb 1580, to Villeroy: named Portuguese sources for Portuguese papers.** He sends letters given him by "le seigneur don Rodrigue de Lancastre son neveu [?]" (letters of the Duke of Bragança) and a dispatch given him by "l'ambassadeur de Portugal qui reside en ceste court" (from the governors of Portugal), each giving them "en confidanse", separately. EN: "Lord Don Rodrigo de Lencastre, his nephew [?]"; "the Portuguese ambassador resident at this court". These are Portuguese, not Zayas, papers.

**5. f. 129r, 8 Mar 1580, to Villeroy: Longlée.** "tant de bons ofises que Mr de Longlé me faict entendre que me faictes" ("so many good offices that M. de Longlée tells me you do me"). Longlée is a channel to Villeroy here, not a source in Madrid.

## Pérez and Éboli (f. 19r, 13 Jan 1580, to Villeroy)

FR: "Antonio Peres est gueri et est toujours en sa maison a Vegnes [?] gardé sans qu'il ait liberté de parler a personne ne estre visité. Toutefois la commune opinion est que ses afaires se porteront bien. S'il vient a estre ainsi, cest action donnera a … entandre [?] qu'il y a heu plus de caprice que de raison. La prinsesse d'Evoli est toujours en mesme estat, observée et traictée avecque toute la rigueur possible. Le duc d'Albe est toujours en son destierre sans qu'il se parle de luy moins que s'il fust mort."
EN: "Antonio Pérez has recovered and is still in his house at Vegnes [?], guarded, without liberty to speak to anyone or be visited. Still, common opinion is that his affairs will go well. If so, this action will show … that there was more caprice than reason in it. The Princess of Éboli is still in the same state, watched and treated with all possible rigour. The Duke of Alba is still in his exile, with no more talk of him than if he were dead."
Not found in the pages read: Mateo Vázquez, "commis", "secrétaire" (as a source), "pension", "gratification", "recompense", "celuy qui me donne advis", "copie".

## What each letter is otherwise about

- 13 Jan (ff. 18–19): his poverty and debts; asks for the abbey of Valasse [?] or leave to go home ("cinquante ans"); Condé; Orange's intrigues; the death of Marshal Bellegarde and Saluzzo; French prisoners; the death of Mehmed Pasha; Alba, Granvelle. Margin of 18r: the Savoy ambassador, the Italian princes' ministers, a passport, what the Portuguese have published.
- 20 Feb (f. 83r): his 7 Feb packet opened at Burgos.
- 20 Feb (f. 85r): the hidden printed book on Philip's claim.
- 20 Feb (f. 87r): Bragança and governors' dispatches; Francisco Giraldes's [?] mission.
- 2 Mar (f. 111r): Queen Mother's illness; the Burgos packet; Mondragón; Abbadie.
- 8 Mar (f. 129r): thanks via Longlée; poverty; whether to follow Philip to Portugal; Granvelle stays.
