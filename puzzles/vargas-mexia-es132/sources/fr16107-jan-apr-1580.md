# BnF Français 16107, views 1–215 (Jan–Apr 1580): court letters to Saint-Goard

Source: BnF Français 16107, Gallica ark `btv1b9009661v` (IIIF manifest has 380 canvases). Tier: PRIMARY.
Images cached at `sources/cache/fr16107/` (`vN.jpg` at 900 px; `FN.jpg` at full size for the views read closely).
Session 2026-11-10. Method: contact sheets of the top strip of each spread (views 1–215), then full-size crops of court letters and of Saint-Goard's letter of 3 April.

**Bottom line: no reply was found, but the result is weak.** Four letters from the court to Saint-Goard fall in Jan–mid-Apr 1580. All four are rough minutes in a very fast secretary's hand that I could read only in fragments. In those fragments I did not see Castro, Cascais/Cascaes or "don Anthoine". Pierre Dor may be named in the minute of 4 April (see below). Treat this as "could not determine", not as proof that no reply exists.

## Transcriptions (partial; [?] = doubtful, … = not read)

### 16 March 1580, Du Roy au Sr de St Goard, f. 139r–v
FR: "Mr de St Goard, depuis mes autres lettres [?] j'ay donné audience a l'ambassadeur de Portugal, lequel m'a parlé [?] de la duchesse de Bragance … a la Royne madame et mere … suivant que je vous ay mandé par mes autres lettres … les vaisseaux … arresté … port … armement … ledit ambassadeur … des gouverneurs [?] … de la disposition de cette affaire …" On f. 139v: "… en Portugal … Castille … le Roy Catho[lique] [?] …"
EN: "Monsieur de Saint-Goard, since my last letters I have given audience to the ambassador of Portugal, who spoke to me about the Duchess of Bragança … to the Queen my lady and mother … as I wrote to you in my other letters … the ships … seized … port … arming … the said ambassador … of the governors … of how this affair stands …" On f. 139v: "… in Portugal … Castile … the Catholic King …"
Castro, Cascais and Dor: not seen in what I could read. The last six lines of f. 139v are illegible to me, and one word there could be "Cast…".

### 27 March 1580, Du Roy au Sr de St Goard, ff. 178–179v (slips)
Fragments only: "… le Roy Cath[olique] … voiage de Guadeloupe … ambassade … le pape … cardinal …"
EN: "… the Catholic King … journey to Guadalupe … embassy … the Pope … cardinal …"
No Castro, Cascais or Dor seen. Mostly unread.

### 3 April 1580, Saint-Goard to the King, decipherment f. 188r–189r
FR: "Sire, le xxviije du passé je receus par Françoys James les despesches de Vostre Mté du … dudict mois, par lesquelles il luy a pleu me faire entendre ce que l'ambassadeur de Portugal avoit negotié avec Voz Majestés sur les despesches des gouverneurs … de la duchesse de Bragance … les responses qui luy ont esté faictes … néanmoins pardonner a ung seigneur portugais qui se sera avancé a vouloir [?] … le Sr … Rodrigue [?] … pour avoir entendu le bon zele qu'il porte a la deffence de la liberté de sa patrie, comme aussi de la particuliere affection qu'il avoit a … vouloir assister …" On f. 188v: "… [il] me demanda si Vostre Mté … voudroit favoriser les affaires de Portugal … les Portugais prendroient plustost toutes … partys … que [de se rendre aux] Castillans …" On f. 189r: "… duc de Bragance … favoriser … Portugal … la bonne volonté qu'elle porte au Roy de Portugal [?] …"
EN: "Sire, on the 28th of last month I received by François James Your Majesty's dispatches of the … of that month, in which you were pleased to tell me what the ambassador of Portugal had negotiated with Your Majesties about the governors' dispatches … of the Duchess of Bragança … the answers given to him … nevertheless to pardon a Portuguese lord who has gone so far as to want … Lord … Rodrigo [?] … having heard of the good zeal he bears for the defence of his country's liberty, as also of the particular affection he had to … wish to help …" On f. 188v: "… [he] asked me whether Your Majesty … would favour the affairs of Portugal … the Portuguese would rather take any course … than [submit to the] Castilians …" On f. 189r: "… Duke of Bragança … favour … Portugal … the goodwill she bears the King of Portugal …"
Note: I did not find the exact phrase "sourde faveur" in the lines I could read. Castro and Cascaes: not seen. The "seigneur portugais" who wants to defend his country's liberty and asks whether France would favour Portugal is a possible match for Castro, but I could not read his name.

### 4 April 1580, Du Roy au Sr de St Goard, f. 200r
FR: "Mr de St Goard, depuis mes autres lettres escriptes l'ambassadeur de Portugal m'a … des gouverneurs … leur deliberation … ce que ledit ambassadeur m'a … ma despesche du … du passé … du duc de Bragance … me prier, comme bon roy, qu'il traitast … rebelles [?] … Marseille … en Portugal … quantité d'armes [?] … ce que je luy ai … accorder … favoriser … ~~[le consul?]~~ Pierre / … de ce mois … Gabry [?] est arrivé, par lequel j'ai receu voz depesches du … et … Castillans [?] …"
EN: "Monsieur de Saint-Goard, since my last letters the ambassador of Portugal has … of the governors … their deliberation … what the said ambassador … my dispatch of … last month … of the Duke of Bragança … to beg me, as a good king, that he might deal … rebels … Marseille … in Portugal … quantity of arms … which I … grant … favour … ~~[the consul?]~~ Pierre … of this month … Gabry [?] has arrived, by whom I received your dispatches of … and … Castilians …"
**Most promising lead.** "Pierre" stands at a line end after a struck-out word. It could be "Pierre Dor" or a courier "Pierre … Gabry"; I could not settle which. The rest of the minute (f. 200r lower half through f. 201) is not read.

- 15 April minute, f. 208 (view 213).
- 27 March minutes, ff. 178–179v: almost all of the text.
- 16 March minute f. 139v: last six lines.
- 3 April decipherment: most of each line. The cipher original (ff. 180–187) was not attempted.
- Queen Mother to Saint-Goard: I identified no separate docket for a letter from her in Jan–Apr apart from the 27 March group.
- Any undocketed slips: the scan covered only the top strip of each spread, so a minute without a docket at the top of a page could have been missed.

The wall: the court minutes are in a very fast secretarial hand (probably Villeroy's office). Reading them needs a palaeographer, or a longer session at full resolution, line by line.
