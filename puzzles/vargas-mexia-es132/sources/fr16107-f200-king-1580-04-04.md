## Answers

1. **Portugal.** f. 200r ll. 1–13: the Portuguese ambassador came from the **governors** to tell him their deliberation (l. 4 "leur deliberat[i]on"). Bragança is named at l. 8 ("du duc de Bragance"). They begged him "comme bon roy" (l. 9) to let them carry from **Marseille** into Portugal "quelque quantité d'armes" (ll. 10–11). He says he has granted it ("accordé", l. 12), "desirant leur faire paroistre que je les desire [assister] … en tout ce que je pourray" (ll. 12–13). I did **not** see "favoriser" on this pass. The marginal word at l. 13 reads "assister" [?]. Later, on f. 201r ll. 3–5, he would have "grand regret" if the duke of Bragança and Don Antonio were divided. I saw no duchess; "Madame … Lancastre[?]" at ll. 6–7 is doubtful. At f. 201r l. 33 he has resolved to send "bien tost" a "personnage de qualité" (destination unread).
2. **Pierre.** f. 200r ll. 14–16: "~~le [courrier?]~~ Pierre … Gabry est arrivé, par lequel j'ai receu voz depesches". On this reading Pierre (Gabry?) is the **courier** who brought Saint-Goard's dispatches. I did not see "Dor". This is reported, not established: two lines with a marginal "2" stand between "Pierre" and "Gabry".
3. **Don Antonio: yes** (f. 201r ll. 3–4, quoted above). **Castro: not established.** I saw "de Cas[?]" at f. 200r l. 24 and "dom Rodrigue de […]" at f. 201r l. 5, and could not settle either. **Cascais: not seen.** Saint-Goard's dispatches are acknowledged (f. 200r ll. 16–18, "la premiere en passant par Bayonne"), but **I could not read the dates**. Nothing ties them to 23 December 1579.
4. **"Sourde faveur" / declaring himself only as far as suits him: not found** in ff. 200r–201r as read. It could still be on the unread f. 201v or f. 202r.
