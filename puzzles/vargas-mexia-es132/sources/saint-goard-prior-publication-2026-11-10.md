# Saint-Goard 1579 cipher passages (BnF fr. 16106): any prior full decipherment?

Survey of 2026-11-10. Web only, no agents. Question: has anyone deciphered the cipher passages of Saint-Goard's 1579
Madrid dispatches (fr. 16106, from f. 207) in full and published or quoted them? If not, what we read from the cipher
is new.

## Verdict

**Not found. No full decipherment and no quotation from one.** No source checked prints, or quotes from, a
decipherment of Saint-Goard's 1579 cipher passages:

- **Brémond d'Ars (1884)** cites fr. 16106 for 1579 only through these items: the Pierre Dor letters from Lisbon, a
  genealogical table, a cleartext money complaint of 12 Nov 1579, and a note that the volume holds Spanish and
  Portuguese documents. He says nothing about the Pérez/Eboli arrest, Escobedo or Lansac from these dispatches. For
  1579–80 he relies on 1580 dispatches (fr. 16107) and on Simancas papers at the Archives nationales.
- **Mousset (1912)** prints Longlée's cipher key, but only Longlée's 1582–1590 dispatches. For the 1579 Lanssac
  affair he uses the Spanish side, Vargas Mexía to Philip II, 24 Aug 1579 (AN K 1557 n° 110). He does not use fr. 16106.
- **La Ferrière / Baguenault, *Lettres de Catherine de Médicis* vol. 7**, quotes or summarises the cleartext of
  Saint-Goard's 12 Nov 1579 letters only (ff. 359, 361).
- **Gachard (1877)** describes the 1579 part of the volume and excerpts the 12 Nov 1579 dispatch (cleartext).
- **Tomokiyo (Cryptiana)** gives key reconstructions as images and quotes no deciphered text from fr. 16106–16108.
- **Gellard, Ribera and Vázquez de Prada** could not be read (paywalled or not online). What is known of them second-hand
  does not suggest any edition of the cipher text, but this is **not verified**.

Best wording for the record: *"Not found in print, in the sources checked. The ones we could not read are Gellard
2014/15, Ribera 2007/2018, Vázquez de Prada 2004, Léonardon's 1889 École des chartes thesis and the SHF* Lettres de
Henri III *vol. IV."* The court decipherer's marginal précis seem to be all that has ever been used, and there is no
evidence anyone used even those.

**Caution on what the volume contains (Gachard, Brémond).** Gachard does not describe the 1579 section as mainly
Saint-Goard's own dispatches. He describes most of it, pieces LIX–CXIV, as *"Pièces concernant l'affaire de la
succession de Portugal en 1579 : lettres écrites par et à Philippe II ; consultes, mémoires, avis de ministres ; deux
lettres écrites de Lisbonne à Saint-Gouard."* ("Papers on the Portuguese succession affair in 1579: letters written by
and to Philip II; consultas, memoranda, opinions of ministers; two letters written from Lisbon to Saint-Gouard.")
Only CXV (12 Nov 1579, to the King) is analysed as a dispatch. Brémond agrees (p. ~118 n.): *"Saint-Gouard envoyait à
Henri III tous les documents qu'il pouvait se procurer. Il y en a de fort curieux en espagnol et en portugais,
concernant les menées de Philippe et de ses agents en 1579, Ms. Bibl. nat. F. fr. 16106"* ("Saint-Gouard sent Henri III
every document he could obtain. Some are very curious, in Spanish and Portuguese, about the schemes of Philip and his
agents in 1579"; the OCR reads "1370" for 1579). So the volume holds **copies of Spanish internal state papers that
Saint-Goard obtained in 1579**. This bears on the 13 Sep 1579 leak question in es. 132 and should be checked folio by
folio against the cached canvases. Gachard's piece numbers (LIX–CXXI) still need matching to folios.

## 1. Brémond d'Ars, *Le père de Madame de Rambouillet, Jean de Vivonne, sa vie et ses ambassades près de Philippe II et à la cour de Rome* (Paris: Plon, 1884)

URL: https://archive.org/details/lepredemadamede00dargoog (Google scan; the OCR text was searched). Tier: SCHOLARLY
(19th-c. archival biography). Page numbers come from OCR running heads and are ±1.

- **Sources (pp. ~400, "Sources" section).** He lists BnF fr. 16104–16108 and describes 16106 as "lettres de 1574 et
  1579, parmi lesquelles une de 1578". He notes earlier partial use by Groen van Prinsterer and "l'analyse succincte" by
  Gachard, and says he went through the correspondence again himself.
- **Cipher (p. 112).** He says only, in general terms: *"Il était dans les mœurs de se dérober en pleine paix … les
  lettres … joies de la maraude, si l'on parvenait à déchiffrer … Philippe avait créé un service d'Italiens … qui se
  piquaient de pénétrer tous les chiffres. M. de Saint-Gouard, de son côté, possédait un secrétaire, nommé Longlée, fort
  bon maître en cet exercice."* ("It was the custom, even in peacetime, to steal one another's letters … the joy of the
  raid, if one managed to decipher … Philip had set up a service of Italians … who prided themselves on breaking every
  cipher. M. de Saint-Gouard, for his part, had a secretary named Longlée, a very good master of this exercise.") The
  footnote is lost in the OCR.
  - Pp. 112–113: the corregidor of Burgos opens Saint-Goard's letters, but the indiscreet man could not "mordre au
    chiffre" ("bite into the cipher"). Sources: letters to Villeroy of Feb and 3 Apr 1580, fr. 16107.
  - P. 113: Tassis's courier robbed at Blois; Henri III receives Tassis only "après avoir déchiffré le grimoire"
    ("after deciphering the scrawl"). That is 1580–81.
- **Nothing on how he read cipher passages.** He nowhere says he deciphered anything or used the marginal précis.
- **1579 citations of fr. 16106 found:**
  - Pierre Dor to Saint-Goard, 15 Mar [?], 15 May and 8 Oct 1579 (pp. ~117–118). These are Portuguese-succession
    narrative: the Cortes of April 1579 and Philip's letter to the Lisbon Câmara.
  - The Medici genealogical claim table (p. ~116).
  - Saint-Goard to Catherine, 12 Nov 1579, a money complaint (p. ~102 n.).
  - The note on Spanish/Portuguese documents (p. ~118 n.).
  - 26 Dec 1578 to Henri III (p. ~96 n.).
- **Antonio Pérez, Eboli, Escobedo.** Not discussed from the dispatches. The only mention is p. ~128 n.: *"Granvelle
  avait pris la tête des affaires à la chute d'Antonio Perez (1579) … Çayas … un des rares débris du parti politique fondé
  par le prince d'Eboli. V. Mignet, Antonio Perez et Philippe II, chap. III."* ("Granvelle had taken the lead in affairs
  at the fall of Antonio Pérez (1579) … Çayas … one of the few remnants of the political party founded by the prince of
  Eboli.") Escobedo: no hit. Mignet (1846) never names Saint-Gouard (OCR search of archive.org
  `antonioperezetp00migngoog`).
- **Lansac / Larache.** No hit anywhere in the book.
- **Longlée.** Pp. 112, 132–133, 1581 onward.

## 2. Albert Mousset, *Dépêches diplomatiques de M. de Longlée, résident de France en Espagne (1582-1590)* (Paris: Plon, 1912)

URL: https://archive.org/details/dpchesdiplom00longuoft. Page images: `…/page/n67_w1400.jpg` = p. lviii and `n68` = p. lix.
Copies are saved as `sources/cache/fr16106/mousset1912-p-lviii.jpg` and `…-p-lix.jpg`. Tier: SCHOLARLY (critical
edition), PRIMARY for the printed dispatches.

- **pp. vii–viii.** Longlée was courier between court and Saint-Goard from 1578. Receipt of 28 Jul 1578, 400 écus, for
  carrying letters to the ambassador in Spain. *"Il [Saint-Gouard] profite d'un chiffre adroitement reconstitué par
  Longlée pour faire un chaleureux éloge de celui-ci"* ("He takes advantage of a cipher deftly reconstituted by Longlée
  to praise him warmly"), citing Saint-Gouard to Villeroy, 16 Jan 1580, fr. 16107 f. 19. Note that *reconstitué* is
  ambiguous.
- **p. x.** Henri III to Saint-Goard, 13 Jul 1581 (misfiled at fr. 16107 ff. 332–335 under 1580): *"J'entendz que
  Longlée demeure par delà … lui laisserez vostre chifre et l'introduirez en ladicte charge"* ("I intend that Longlée
  stay there … you will leave him your cipher and introduce him into the said post"). This is the documentary basis for
  "Longlée inherited Vivonne's cipher".
- **pp. xiv–xv: the Lanssac affair, August 1579.**
  - Lanssac (Guy de Saint-Gelais) offered Philip a plan to take Larache. Saint-Goard had Longlée investigate. Longlée
    reported to Villeroy, who reprimanded Lanssac.
  - A councillor of Philip's had taken Longlée in his carriage to the house where Lanssac's emissaries lodged.
  - Source: **Vargas Mexía to Philip II, AN K 1557 n° 110, 24 Aug 1579**, which says one must *"avoir l'œil sur
    Saint-Gouard et savoir avec certitude quels sont ceux qui ont des relations avec lui, car il sait et fait profession
    de connaître beaucoup de détails … qui ne peuvent venir que de personnes au courant des affaires"* ("keep an eye on
    Saint-Gouard and find out for certain who is in contact with him, for he knows, and professes to know, many details …
    that can only come from people privy to affairs"). Mousset's French paraphrase; the Spanish is partly quoted in his
    note.
  - Mousset does **not** cite fr. 16106 for this.
- **pp. xlvii–xlviii.** Lists Gachard, *La Bibliothèque nationale à Paris* II (1877) pp. 566–569, for 16109–16110. Notes
  that "l'ignorance du chiffre" ("ignorance of the cipher") caused "déchiffrements déplacés … interversion de folios
  chiffrés" ("misplaced decipherments … cipher folios out of order") when the volumes were bound.
- **p. li.** Spanish interception: Luis Valle de la Cerda deciphered Longlée's 1590 letters and built a "contre-chiffre"
  (AE Espagne 237 ff. 113, 132–133). It was "assez infidèle" ("rather unfaithful") for word-signs.
- **pp. lii–liii.**
  - Dispatches to the King are almost always ciphered, to Villeroy sometimes, to the Queen generally in clear, and never
    entirely ciphered.
  - The table includes nulls and word-signs. Abbreviations occur inside the cipher.
  - fr. 4053 ff. 99–100 holds a "chiffre attribué à Longlée" ("cipher attributed to Longlée") that seems never to have
    been used.
  - *"La clé que nous publions a été reconstituée d'après les dépêches elles-mêmes"* ("The key we publish was
    reconstituted from the dispatches themselves").
- **pp. lvi–lvii (Avertissement).** Ciphered dispatches are printed from the cipher originals, not from the official
  decipherments, which "offrent … de trop nombreuses négligences" ("show … too many careless errors"). This is Mousset's
  own decipherment, **for 1582–1590 only**.
- **Key table, pp. lviii–lix:** "CHIFFRE DE LONGLÉE, reconstitué d'après l'interprétation de ses dépêches" ("Longlée's
  cipher, reconstituted from the interpretation of his dispatches"). The symbols are hand-drawn glyphs. Descriptions
  below are our approximations from the page image (see the saved JPGs for the true forms).

## 3. Matthieu Gellard, *Une reine épistolaire* (Classiques Garnier, 2014/2015)

Publisher record: https://classiques-garnier.com (chapter PDFs give metadata only). Text **not accessible**:
Classiques Garnier returns 403 or metadata-only, and Google Books API was rate-limited (429).

Second-hand via en.wikipedia "Jean de Vivonne" and "Pierre de Ségusson" (SECONDARY):

- Gellard p. 255 says about half of Saint-Goard's correspondence was encrypted, and that ciphers did not guarantee
  security.
- p. 255: Longlée's letters were intercepted and deciphered by Valle de la Cerda.
- p. 260: in 1574 a Spanish agent penetrated the ambassador's household. This is the only such case in 1559–89.
- p. 600: Longlée's 1579 mission to Portugal.

Nothing indicates Gellard prints deciphered 1579 text. **Unverified.** No separate Gellard article on Saint-Goard's
ciphers was found.

## 4. Vázquez de Prada (2004); Ribera (2007/2018)

- **Vázquez de Prada, *Felipe II y Francia* (EUNSA 2004).** Not online. The review by J. Orlandis, *Anuario de Historia
  de la Iglesia* 14 (2005) 540–541 (https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/355/35514092.pdf, SCHOLARLY), says only that it rests on
  Simancas and the BnF. The review says nothing about Saint-Goard or ciphers. Content **unverified**.
- **Ribera, *Diplomatie et espionnage* (Champion 2007; Classiques Garnier 2018).** Not online (metadata PDF only).
  Second-hand via en.wikipedia:
  - pp. 197–198: the Lanssac/Al-Araish (Larache) secret negotiation, uncovered by Saint-Goard in 1579; the Spanish
    ambassador in France surprised at Saint-Goard's information in Aug 1579 (= Vargas, matching Mousset p. xiv).
  - pp. 293–298: on ciphers. About 60% of Longlée's dispatches are encrypted, usually only in part. Letters to Henri are
    mostly encrypted; letters to Catherine and Villeroy only exceptionally.
  - Nothing reported about printing deciphered fr. 16106 text. **Unverified.**

## 5. Databases and editions

  - "Longlee's cipher" runs from fr. 16106 f. 207 ("at least from 1579") through fr. 16107–16108, citing Mousset p. x.
  - fr. 16107 f. 63: a Dor passage of 10 Feb 1580 in a separate cipher. f. 225: an Abadie letter of 2 May 1580 in
    another cipher.
  - The reconstructions are **images only**. **No deciphered passages** from fr. 16106–16108 are quoted.
  - Gellard is not cited.
- **DECODE (de-crypt.org).** No record for fr. 16106–16108. This comes from the full-list check in
  `bnf-survey-2026-10-15.md` and was not re-checked today.
- **Gachard, *La Bibliothèque nationale à Paris* II (Brussels 1877)**, n° 240, pp. ~452–457.
  - URL: https://archive.org/details/labibliothquen02gachuoft. Tier: SCHOLARLY/catalogue.
  - Describes Harlay 228 vol. 3 as pieces I–LVIII (1574), and LIX–CXXI (1579), with the Portugal-papers description

  - For 1574 he prints decipherments ("En chiffres, avec le déchiffrement", "In cipher, with the decipherment") of

## 6. *Lettres de Catherine de Médicis*, vols 6 (1578–79) and 7 (1579–81)

archive.org `lettresdecatheri06cathuoft`, `…07cathuoft`. Tier: PRIMARY (edition) / SCHOLARLY (notes).

- **Vol. 6, 10 Jan 1579 (p. ~214).** Longlée arrives with a dispatch from Saint-Goard on Portugal. Catherine asks that
  "ledict Longlée, qui vous y a desjà faict service" ("the said Longlée, who has already served you there") be sent to
  Portugal.
- **Vol. 6, p. ~288 area.** Catherine cites "la dépesche que le s' de Saint-Gouard vous a faicte le premier de ce mois"
  ("the dispatch the sieur de Saint-Gouard sent you on the first of this month") on the duchess of Brunswick and the
  Anjou–England marriage. This is a paraphrase, not a quotation.
- **Vol. 7, p. 91, Catherine to Henri III, Grenoble, 18 Aug 1579, autograph postscript.** *"Quant au fest de Lansac,
  je serès d'avys que luy en parliziés … pour savoyr de lui s'il a envoyé ces jeans en Ayspagne"* ("As for the Lansac
  business, I would advise that you speak to him about it … to find out from him whether he has sent these men to
  Spain"). This is the Larache affair from the court's side.
- **Vol. 7, p. ~101, 23 Aug 1579, to Villeroy.** Lansac the father is "désespéré de ce que l'on dist de son fils"
  ("in despair at what is said of his son"). The editor's note identifies Gui de Saint-Gelais.
- **Vol. 7, p. 228 n.** The editor quotes **Saint-Goard to Catherine, 12 Nov 1579 (fr. 16106 f. 361)**: "l'on n'a
  advancé ne innové aucune chose depuis ce temps-là" ("nothing has been advanced or changed since then", on Portugal
  to 18 Oct). He paraphrases the same day's letter to the King (f. 359): Philip aims at "la réunion des deulx couronnes"
  ("the union of the two crowns"), with armaments and Mondéjar as captain-general. These are cleartext or précis-level;
  no decipherment is claimed.
- **Vol. 7 p. ~215 n.** The editor sends readers to Brémond d'Ars for Saint-Goard's negotiations.
- **No Pérez, Eboli or Escobedo hits** in either volume.
