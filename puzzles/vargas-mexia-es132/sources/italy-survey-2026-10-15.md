# Italian holdings bearing on es. 132: source survey, 2026-10-15

Scope: what Italian archives and libraries hold, or what has been published from them, that bears on the open questions
of this dive. This is a source survey only: no decipherments were attempted. Roughly 30 web/fetch calls. Downloads are in
`sources/cache/italy-survey/` (full-text OCR from the Internet Archive):

- `meister1906_djvu.txt`: Aloys Meister, *Die Geheimschrift im Dienste der päpstlichen Kurie* (Paderborn 1906),
  https://archive.org/details/diegeheimschrift00meis
- `hinojosa1896_djvu.txt`: Ricardo de Hinojosa, *Los despachos de la diplomacia pontificia en España*, vol. 1 (Madrid
  1896), https://archive.org/details/losdespachosdela00hino
- `cspvenice7_djvu.txt`: *Calendar of State Papers … Venice*, vol. 7, 1558–1580 (1890),
  https://archive.org/details/calendarofstatep0007late

"verified [text read]" = I read the passage in a downloaded or fetched text. "search only" = known only from a
search-result summary or catalogue record.

## Summary: best leads

1. **The "teatino" of f. 123 has a strong candidate: Trigoso, Don John's go-between in 1577.** Don John's own letter to
   Philip II (Jan.–Feb. 1577, printed by Mignet from the Hague Pérez manuscript) names "un Teatino, que se llama
   Trigosso", who "vino de Amberes con Octavio [Gonzaga] y Escobedo" and was used "por concertador como religioso" in the
   negotiation with the Emperor's deputies and the States (Mignet 1846, appendix pp. 428–429, from "Ms de La Haye", verified
   [text read], `sources/cache/mignet1846.txt` l. 20829). A Jesuit Pedro Trigoso de Calatayud (1533–1593) is documented,
   with 181 calendared documents mostly in the Archivum Romanum Societatis Iesu (ARSI), Rome (Vázquez Janeiro,
   *Antonianum* 1986, SCHOLARLY, abstract read). In Spanish usage "teatino" was a common name for Jesuits; the attestations
   I found are 17th-century (see Unverified claims). **What it would breach:** the "teatino" identification
   (`readings-v2.md`, OVERVIEW open question) and H6. A Jesuit in Don John's service who had travelled with Escobedo is
   exactly whose "papeles o cartas" the King would want after 1 Oct 1578. **Test:** identify the Mignet Trigosso with
   Pedro Trigoso SJ (Vázquez Janeiro's calendar; ARSI *Hisp.*/*Germ.*/*Gall.* letters 1577–78); find whether he was in
   France in Oct. 1578.
2. **Sega, nuncio in Madrid, reported Pérez's and Éboli's arrest, and his dispatches survive as originals and
   decipherments for every month of our window.** Hinojosa's inventory: AAV, Nunziatura di Spagna vol. 11 (originals
   and *descifrados*, Sega → Como, Aug. 1577 – Dec. 1578), vol. 22 (Jan.–Dec. 1579), vol. 25 (1580, with the Portugal
   *pareceres*), vol. 20 (Como's minutes to Sega, Jul.–Dec. 1579); copies in Bibl. Barberiniana LVIII 6 (29 Oct. 1578 – 20
   Jan. 1579) and Arch. Borghese II 462. Hinojosa lists among their subjects "la prisión de Antonio Pérez y de la Princesa
   de Éboli" and "los oficios para mover á Felipe II á aceptar la decisión del Papa en la sucesión de la Corona portuguesa"
   (verified [text read], Hinojosa pp. ~230–231, SCHOLARLY finding aid). Como's 1577 instructions told Sega to work on the
   England enterprise "ya por conducto del secretario Antonio Pérez". Sega had also been special envoy to Don John in
   Flanders in early 1577 (en.wikipedia citing Fernández Collado 1991 p. 27, SECONDARY). **What it would breach:** an
   independent, dated, outside view of Madrid court news (13 Sep. 1579 Saint-Goard leak), of who was urging what in April
   1579 (Quiroga/Éboli), and of the arrest. Not digitized as far as I found; the modern study is Á. Fernández Collado,
   *Gregorio XIII y Felipe II en la Nunciatura de Felipe Sega (1577–1581)* (Toledo 1991), not seen.
3. **A papal cipher key for correspondence with Don John, and a nuncio-in-Spain key set, from exactly our years, are
   printed in Meister.** Meister's "Schlüsselsammlung VI" edits 21 keys from BAV, Cod. Ottob. 3208–3209, dated 1573–1587,
   including "Cifra col ser.mo signor Don Giovanni" (nomenclator: Nostro Signore, Sua maestà cattolica, Principe d'Orange,
   Regina d'Inghilterra, Li Stati, Re di Danimarca, Olanda e Zelanda, Re di Francia, Duca d'Alanson), "Cifra tra mons.
   nuntio di Spagna et il nuntio di Fiandra", "Cifra tra mons. nuntio di Spagna et li nuntii Portia et Caligari" (Caligari
   was nuncio in Poland 1578–81, which dates it to 1578 or later) with the note that names "nella cifra che hora si manda
   col nuntio di Francia" serve here too, "Cifra con Don Bernardino di Mendozza", and "Cifra con mons. di Barlemont
   arcivescovo di Cambrai" (Meister pp. 262–282, verified [text read], PRIMARY transcription in a SCHOLARLY edition).
   Cod. Ottob. 3208/3209 is **not** on DigiVatLib (view URLs 404; Ott.lat.1 returns 200 as a control). **What it would
   breach:** reading any cipher passage in Sega's 1578–80 dispatches or in papal–Don John letters without a
   cryptanalysis. These are papal, Italian-language keys; they do not read es. 132's Spanish ciphers.
4. **Dandino, nuncio in Paris 1578–81, is the subject of Vargas's 28 Mar. 1580 complaint, and his dispatches are
   unpublished.** The only edition-level work is Ivan Cloulas's typescript thesis *Introduction à l'édition des dépêches
   diplomatiques du nonce Anselmo Dandino (1578–1581)* (Paris 1966, XXXIX + 227 ff.; catalogue record read, SCHOLARLY).
   *Acta Nuntiaturae Gallicae* has Salviati 1572–78 (vols. 12–13) and Castelli 1581–83 (vol. 7) but, as far as I could
   find, no Dandino 1578–81 volume. **What it would breach:** the other side of the "vida y costumbre del nuncio"
   question and what Dandino reported about Vargas, Guise and the Portugal question from Paris. Vatican shelfmark not
   found (see Unverified claims).
5. **Calibration point: the papal cipher office broke a Spanish royal cipher in 1557.** Meister prints "Cifra del card.
   di Burgos con il re Philippo, decifrata alli X febraro 1557 in Bologna" and a "Cifra del Re Philippo con li suoi
   ministri d'Italia … tempore Pauli 4" in the Argenti collection (verified [text read]); the Granada cipher museum page
   attributes the break to papal secretary Triphon Bencio (SECONDARY, citing Meister and Devos). Matteo Argenti also
   says the Spanish court "did not take much care over ciphers" (Meister p. ~71, verified [text read]). **What it would
   breach:** nothing directly. It shows that Rome collected and broke Spanish keys, so the Argenti volumes (BAV, Chigi
   and Vat. lat.; AAV) are a place where a Spanish key of 1577–80 could have ended up. Meister's 1555–93 and 1580–91
   Argenti books list no Spanish ambassador key of 1577–80 that I could find by heading.

## Checked and empty

- **CSP Venice vol. 7 (1558–1580):** no Antonio Pérez (only Gonzalo Pérez and an Alfonso Pérez), no Escobedo arrest
  or murder entry, no Theatine, no Saint-Goard in 1579, no Dandino. The only 1578 Vargas Mexía mention is L12. The
  calendar selects English affairs only, so this is a negative about the calendar, not about the Venetian dispatches.
  Venice's ambassadors in France were Lippomano (1577–79) and Lorenzo Priuli (1579–80) (preface, verified [text read]).
- **Hinojosa vol. 1:** no Escobedo, no Vargas Mexía, no Theatine connected to Don John; one generic "alumbrados y
  teatinos de España" (l. 12887).
- **Meister 1906:** no key with Sega's or Dandino's name in a heading; no Spanish key of 1577–80; no mention of Vargas
  Mexía or Pérez.
- ***Acta Nuntiaturae Gallicae*:** no Dandino 1578–81 volume found (two searches, Cambridge review list); the Dandino
  volumes in the series are Girolamo Dandino's 1540s nunciatures.
- **DigiVatLib:** Ott.lat.3208, 3209 and 3210 return 404 (not digitized); a Barberini probe returned 404. I did not run
  a general sweep for mis-catalogued Spanish cipher pages; DigiVatLib's search is a JavaScript application and it
  produced nothing usable from urllib.
- **Medici BIA:** unreachable. **ASMo Estense inventories:** connection timed out and then refused. Both are walls of
  access, not negatives.
- **Not checked:** DECODE/DECRYPT records from Italian archives; Biblioteca Ambrosiana and Marciana digitized
  collections; Albèri's *Relazioni* (Spain volumes); Tomokiyo's Cryptiana for papal ciphers beyond the cached Vargas
  page. The cached Cryptiana page says Vargas's Cipher 3 (Devos Cp.30) was shared with Bernardino de Mendoza (London) and
  Juan de Borja (Prague), with no Italian holder named.

## Unverified claims

- That "teatino" was the usual Spanish name for Jesuits **in the 1570s**. The attestations found are 17th-century
  (Puebla 1647 Inquisition case, BUAP thesis, POPULAR/SCHOLARLY thesis; Chimalpahin 1610 via the Nahuatl dictionary
  site, SECONDARY). Unverified for 1578. The DLE entry returned 403.
- That Mignet's "Teatino … Trigosso" (1577) is Pedro Trigoso de Calatayud SJ. Same surname, right period, a Jesuit
  later turned Capuchin; no source I read puts Pedro Trigoso in the Netherlands. Unverified.
- Exact date of the Don John letter naming Trigosso: Mignet introduces a letter of 2 Jan. 1577 (Ms de La Haye fol. 76)
  and quotes further Don John text before the Trigoso passage, with a later footnote "Ms de La Haye, fol. 4 à 12". The
  content (the Emperor's deputies; Don John agreeing to go to Malines or Louvain) fits Jan.–Feb. 1577. Precise letter
  and date not pinned.
- Hinojosa's page numbers (~230–231) are inferred from the OCR running heads; the volume and content descriptions are
  read.
- Sega's 1577 Flanders mission and dates (en.wikipedia citing Fernández Collado 1991 and Biaudet; SECONDARY). The
  Fernández Collado book was not seen; whether it prints documents mentioning Pérez's arrest is unknown (the review
  links I tried returned an access-check page).
- Whether Meister's Ottob. "Cifra col ser.mo signor Don Giovanni" is from 1576–78 (Don John's governorship) or from
  his 1577 England plans. The nomenclator (Orange, the States, Alençon, Queen of England) fits 1576–78; Meister gives no
  date in the passage I read.
- Location of Dandino's dispatches in the AAV (probably Segreteria di Stato, Francia, and/or Fondo Borghese): not found.
- Whether the AAV Nunziatura di Spagna volumes have been digitized since Hinojosa: not checked beyond the absence of
  any hit; the AAV has no general public image portal for the Segreteria di Stato that I know of. Training-data
  impression only.
- The Florentine (Medici) and Venetian ambassadors in Madrid in 1579 and whether they reported the arrest: not
  established in this session.
