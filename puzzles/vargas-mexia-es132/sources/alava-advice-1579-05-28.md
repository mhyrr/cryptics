# Don Francés de Álava's advice on the Portugal enterprise, Madrid, 28 May 1579 (BnF fr. 16106 ff. 210r–215v)

Test of H21. Read 2026-11-10 from the cached spreads `sources/cache/fr16106/v218.jpg`–`v224.jpg` (PRIMARY: a
contemporary copy in the French ambassador's file; images from Gallica, already cached; nothing fetched). The heading
and the dorsal endorsement were checked on 2× and 3× crops. Transcription is semi-diplomatic: abbreviations expanded
silently where certain, doubtful words marked [?]. Not a full transcription: heading, frame and key passages only.

## Physical description

- Six leaves, ff. 210–215, a secretary hand in Spanish, clear. Leaves are narrower than the volume, so each spread shows
  strips of the neighbouring leaves; the reading order below follows catchwords (each page runs on to the next).
- f. 210r, left margin in a French hand: "28 may 1579". Foliation "210" and an older "61".
- f. 215r ends the text with date and place. f. 215v, Spanish endorsement (same or a contemporary Spanish hand, written
  sideways): **"Copia del Parecer de Don Frances de Alava sobre la empresa de Portugal. En Madrid 28 de mayo 1579."**
  ("Copy of the opinion of Don Francés de Álava on the Portugal enterprise. In Madrid, 28 May 1579.")
- The next document, f. 216 (v224), is the Marquis [of Vila Real] to the [French] King, in Portuguese, docketed "11 Juin
  1579"; Álava's paper is complete at f. 215.

## Heading and opening (f. 210r)

> S. C. R. M.^d
> [French docket:] Advis de Dom Francesco de Alava au Roy Catholique sur l'entreprise de Portugal
> ("Advice of Don Francesco de Álava to the Catholic King on the Portugal enterprise")

> Por mandado de V. M. a los xxv de Mayo nos juntamos en cassa del Marques de Aguilar, el y Don Juan de Silva, el
> contador Garnica, Sancho de Avila y Pedro Bermudez, el secretario Delgado y yo, a platicar y dar nuestros paresceres
> sobre la forma que se ha de tener para apoderarse V. Mg.^d de Portugal en caso que se uviesse de venir a las armas.

"By Your Majesty's order, on 25 May we met at the house of the Marqués de Aguilar, he and Don Juan de Silva, the
accountant Garnica, Sancho de Ávila and Pedro Bermúdez, the secretary Delgado and I, to discuss and give our opinions on
the way Your Majesty is to take possession of Portugal if it should come to arms."

- **Writer:** Don Francés de Álava. **To:** Philip II (S.C.R.M.). **Place/date:** Madrid, 28 May 1579 (f. 215r:
  "En Madrid 28 de Mayo 1579").
- **Junta of 25 May 1579, at the Marqués de Aguilar's:** Aguilar; Don Juan de Silva; contador Garnica; Sancho de Ávila;
  Pedro Bermúdez; secretario Delgado; Álava.

## Summary, paragraph by paragraph

1. **Frame (f. 210r).** The matter is grave; from "las pláticas que se han tenido en la dicha junta y relaciones que de
   particulares se han visto" ("the discussions held in the said junta and the reports of individuals that have been
   seen"), he first offers "tres puntos de esencia" ("three points of substance"), then "lo que entiendo se deve guardar
   en el effecto desta empresa, satisfaziendo en todo a los cabos principales que en la junta se propusieron" ("what I
   understand should be observed in carrying out this enterprise, answering all the main heads proposed in the junta").
2. **Point 1, justification (f. 210r)** "con Dios y su Vicario en la Tierra y con los principes y potentados
   xpianos" ("with God and His Vicar on earth and with the Christian princes and potentates"); otherwise "se deve temer
   el mal successo" ("a bad outcome is to be feared").
3. **Point 2, sacrifices and prayer (ff. 210r–v)**, as the Emperor did; Algiers (1541) failed because "se olvido lo
   principal, que fue pedir a Dios el buen successo" ("the main thing was forgotten, which was to ask God for success");
   Prince [Andrea] Doria and Antonio Doria cited.
4. **Point 3, reputation (f. 210v–211r).** Nothing would harm it more than "apoderarse de lo que tan claramente es suyo con
   todo el rigor de las armas" ("seizing by the full rigour of arms what is so clearly his"), though there have been
   "atrevimientos en aquel Reyno por escripto y de palabra" ("insolences in that kingdom in writing and by word"). First
   try "con todas las dadivas y ofertas y por todas las otras vias que se pudiessen sufrir, a traer a obediencia aquel
   Reyno sin llegar a rompimiento, por evitar la effusion de sangre" ("with every gift and offer and every other tolerable
   means, to bring that kingdom to obedience without coming to a breach, to avoid bloodshed"). If that works, the forces
   could be used elsewhere, "permitiendolo las negociaciones que dizen que andan" ("if the negotiations said to be under
   way allow it").
5. **Fleet (f. 211r).** "Presuponese que se juntaran cinquenta [?] y nueve galeras y treynta naos que haran quatorze mill
   toneladas y doze zabras" ("it is presupposed that 59 [?] galleys and 30 ships of 14,000 tons and 12 zabras will be
   assembled"); the Marqués de Santa Cruz thinks all of it necessary; embargo 8 or 10 more ships.
6. **Army vs. resistance (ff. 211r–v).** The King has ordered 36,000 men. Portuguese strength from reports "de muchos y
   particularmente de Don Juan de Silva, Don Alonso de Vargas y Manuel Doria [?]": Lisbon alone 40,000+; batallones
   50,000; 250 militia companies between Guadiana and Tagus; little cavalry ("la mejor ... perdieron con su Rey en
   Berberia", "the best ... they lost with their King in Barbary"). 50,000 would not be too many, but with the 36,000
   ordered: "la ymportancia y sustancia deste hecho consiste todo en tomar a Lisboa" ("the whole weight of this business
   lies in taking Lisbon"). 3,000 Spanish recruits and 3,000 Italians on the galleys; the rest, with 4,500–5,000 horse,
   in one body.
7. **Route (ff. 211v–212r).** Not by Coria (mountains impassable for artillery); "por entre Merida y Badajoz a salir a
   Alburquerque", taking Olivenza, Elvas and Portalegre; then to the Tagus, "arruynando las tierras del Duque de
   Bergança quando no viniesen a la rrazon" ("ruining the Duke of Braganza's lands if they do not come to reason"); bridge
   the river and march between Tagus and Douro. Siege train: two half-cannon, three medios [?], two field pieces.
8. **Diversions and threats (f. 212v).** Feints from Ayamonte (Andalusia) and Galicia; "franceses, ingleses y rebeldes
   flamencos de los Payses Baxos vendran a socorrer a portugueses" ("French, English and Flemish rebels will come to help
   the Portuguese"); Bayona and La Coruña in bad order; artillery by sea; reports expected from Antonelli and others who
   reconnoitred Lisbon, the Torre de Belém and S. Julião.
9. **If the King of Portugal dies before the Italian troops arrive (ff. 213r–v).** Santa Cruz's galleys are too weak; a
   show of force would only "avivar a los enemigos de aquel Reyno" ("rouse that kingdom's enemies"); guard the coast "con
   color de que se anda sobre navios ... de Turcos y Moros" ("under colour of hunting Turkish and Moorish ships"). If he
   lives, keep the Italian troops afloat or at Ibiza and Formentera rather than land them in Spain.
10. **Logistics (ff. 213v–214v).** Train the new infantry early; Juan Andrea [Doria]'s galleys and 1,000 infantry to
    Cartagena; arms for 14,000 (10,000 arquebuses, 10–12,000 pikes from Guipúzcoa and Vizcaya); victuals beyond six
    months; 400–500 mounted arquebusiers; staff (general, maestre de campo general, veedor, contador, pagador); artillery
    train, mules, carts.
11. **Close (f. 215r).** "Concluyo el recuerdo y parescer que V. Mg. me ha mandado" ("I conclude the memorandum and
    opinion that Your Majesty ordered of me"); also secure the French frontier: viceroy to Navarre, captain-general to
    Guipúzcoa, Aragon to watch Jaca, Catalonia Salses; "no dudo sino que por todas partes querran divertir las fuerças y
    designio de V. Mg." ("I do not doubt they will try on every side to divert Your Majesty's forces and design").
    "En Madrid 28 de Mayo 1579."

**Is it "an opinion on the means of taking Portugal by force"?** Yes. Its stated subject, "la forma que se ha de tener
para apoderarse V. Mg. de Portugal en caso que se uviesse de venir a las armas", is almost word for word Paz's title. It
also argues that peaceful means should be tried first.

**Council of War's view or one man's?** One man's. It reports a junta convened by royal order, but the voice is first
person throughout ("lo que me paresce", "what seems right to me") and it closes "el recuerdo y parescer que V. Mg. me ha
mandado". It never calls itself a paper of the Consejo de Guerra. The Spanish endorsement calls it "Parecer de Don Frances
de Alava".

**Against or open:**
- Each junta member was told to "dar nuestros paresceres". Five or six sibling papers from the same 25 May meeting would
  match Philip's description equally well (Juan de Silva's or Sancho de Ávila's, for instance). Philip names no author,
  so the fit is to the *class* of paper, not to Álava's paper in particular.
- We have not seen the K 1554–55 item. Paz's "del Consejo de guerra" could be Paz's own label, or the docket on Vargas's
  copy reflecting the Paris attribution; it does not tell us whose paper it was.
- fr. 16106 is Saint-Goard's (French) file, but nothing on ff. 210–215 says how or when it came to Paris. H21's timing
  caveat stands: it must have been in Paris, and reported by Vargas, well before 29 Nov.

## Verdict

**Consistent, not established.** Álava's paper fits every checkable detail of the disowned paper: subject, title wording,
"parecer" genre, a private opinion that could pass for a Council paper, the 1579 date, presence in Paris. But the
description fits any member's parecer from the same junta, and Philip names no author.

**Settling document:** the copy in AGS Estado K 1554–55 that Paz lists. If its text opens "Por mandado de V. M. a los
xxv de Mayo nos juntamos en cassa del Marques de Aguilar …" and closes "Concluyo el recuerdo y parescer …", H21 is
supported. If it is another member's parecer, H21 is refuted. Second best: Vargas's covering letter (Oct–Nov 1579) that
enclosed it.

## Limits

- Read from 1800 px spreads plus two crops. Numerals in the fleet paragraph ("cinquenta [?] y nueve") and a few names
  (Manuel Doria [?]) are uncertain. Nothing here is a full transcription.
- The decode of the 29 Nov letter is a candidate reading (two witnesses, ~76% agreement). The words "uno de [ellos]" are
  reconstructed.
- Álava's own office in 1579 (whether he sat on the Council of War) is not verified here. It would sharpen the "allá
  dezían que es del consejo" confusion. **Unverified.**
