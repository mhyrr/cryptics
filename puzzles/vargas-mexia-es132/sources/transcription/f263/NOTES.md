## Conventions I had to improvise (guide gives no form)
- `w` = a recurring open sign like an omega or a flat "w"/"cu" with no mark; it is very frequent. It could be a letter form (not 6, not u). Could also be written differently by another reader.
- `x`, `x+`, `xp`, `x.`, `B+`, `m`, `t a`, `2um`, `um` = letter-like forms as seen. `2um` is a 2 plus u-m ligature (a 2 with a long looping tail).
- `<sign:big-curl-below>` (8 places) = a large looping stroke descending below a 6/26 (e.g. `26<sign:big-curl-below>`), probably a very flourished 6 or 26 with a big tail; not a mark in the guide.
- `<sign:d-loop>` (5) = a d-like or ∂ loop sign standing alone. `<sign:small-2-above>` (4) = a tiny numeral 2 over a sign. `<sign:blot>` (5) = ink blot covering a sign. `<sign:cil>`, `<sign:p-alone>`, `<sign:u-23-flourish>` = one-off oddities.
- Numbers in the 10s written with an apparent leading "1" plus `5`/`6`: I wrote `15+`, `16+` etc. when the second sign had its own mark.
- Many "Spanish de" in the middle of cipher lines: `[[de]]` written in clear (6 places on 263v, 264r, 264v).
- Slash `/` at line ends = a virgula/end-of-sentence stroke, written as its own token; these appear regularly at paragraph ends (and a heavy blot-slash at several line ends).
