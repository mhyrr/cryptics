## Letter-forms and special signs used
- `f`: 11r l.28 (`f_`); 11v l.1 and l.20 (`f_`); 12r l.6 (`f<bar>`).
- `p` (long-descender p, often with foot serif): 11r l.29 (`pe`); 11v l.1 (`p+`), l.2 (`pe`), l.5 (`p^`), l.6 (`pe` at line end); 12r l.1 (`p^`), l.3 (`pe`), l.11 (`p+`), l.18 (`p+`), l.23 (`pe`); 12v l.3 (`pe`).
- `n`: 11r l.29, l.31 (`n^`); 11v l.27 (`n^`), l.29 (`n^`); 12v l.3 (`n^`).
- `m`: 11v l.1 once (`me`, m with hook).
- `R`: 11v l.18 (`Re`), l.21, l.24 (`R+`); 12r l.2 (`R+`), l.11 (`R+`), l.14 (`R+`, twice), l.17 (`R+`); 12v l.12 (`R+`). Always capital R, mostly with cross.
- `C`: 11v l.14 (`C^`), l.24 (`C^`); 12r l.2 (`C^`), l.17 (`C^`); 12r l.22 (`Ce`).
- `P`: 12r l.1 (`Pe`). Another large-descender p-like glyph (11v l.5 `p^`, 12r l.1 `p^`) was written `p`.
- `v`: 11v l.22 (`v^`), 12r l.10 (`v<bar>`), l.11 (`v_`, `v+<bar>`), l.19 (`v<bar>`, `v^?`), 12r l.1 (`ve`), 11v l.29 (`ve`).
- `h`: 12r l.6 (`h+`).
- `<rho>`: 39 times. Rule I used: the large loop-plus-tail sign with a long tail dropping well below the line and curling,
  with no mark attached. A `g`-shaped sign with an attached `+` or `e` was written `9+` / `9e`. The two classes shade
  into each other (see hardest places).
- `<venus>`: 11r l.30 (once); 11v l.24 (`<venus>+?`), l.25, l.28; 12r l.9, l.10. A small circle with a bar below; a dot
  sometimes sits under the bar and was not coded.
- `<bar>`: 17 uses (over `2`, `13e`, `5e`, `19+`, `f`, `11e`, `v`, `v+`, `2`).
- Not in the guide's notation: `11+e` (11v, 12r l.16) for a sign that shows two uprights, a cross AND a hook. `5e_?`
  (12r l.19) for an S with a hook and a dot below.

## Hardest places (do not trust these)
1. **Free rho vs `9`.** Many g-shaped signs have a tail that curls; I called a sign `<rho>` when the tail dropped well
   below the baseline and curled back with nothing attached, and `9` when it had a `+`, `e`, or `.` attached or a short
   tail. Expect several mismatches against another reader.
2. **`5` vs `3`.** An S-shaped sign with a rounded top was written `5`; a flat-topped ʒ with a tail was written `3`. The sign
   at 12r l.7 after `<rho>` (dot below) was first read `3_` then re-read `5_`.
3. **11v l.15, the long horizontal stroke** before `0+`: a long dash with a short upright at its left end, then `0+`. I wrote
   `20+?`; it is plausibly an elongated 2 but I did not code it as a new sign.
4. **12r l.7 start**: `7. 6 12 1? <rho>` - a short upright `1`-like stroke sits between `12` and the rho.
5. **Bars.** The bar over `19+ 13 2` (11v l.26) spans three signs; I coded `<bar>` on each. Over `5e` (12r l.6, l.18) clear.
   A pass-1 bar over `13e` on 11v l.28 disappeared in pass 2.
6. **`11+e`, `17e`, `15e`, `13e`, `12e`**: where a hook and a cross stand together the `+` wins; hooks after `1x` signs are
   small and sometimes join the next sign.
7. **Dots between lines** on all four pages, especially 12r l.8-14 and 11v l.14-29; every dotted sign marked `^?`, `_?`
   is a guess by distance. Dots at the very bottom of a sign above the next row, close to both, were assigned by the
   nearer row.
8. **Cut line ends** on 11v and 12v: the last 1-2 signs of many lines are partly hidden under the overlay.
9. **Faint/ghost ink**: a few signs on 12r (l.14 `18`, l.15 `6 13`) are fainter than their neighbours; read as normal. In
   12r l.10 the second token (`5e`) could be `5+`.
10. A small lighter-ink `5+` stands at the right margin below the last cipher line of 11v (bleed or catchword): not coded
    as a sign of the text.
11. Stray dots not attached to a sign: 12r l.8 after `16+` at line start; 12r l.11 between `p+` and `19`.

## Clear text
Diplomatic, not normalised. Uncertain clear words carry `?` inside `[[ ]]` (`hauian?`, `lo?`, `sen cosas?`). The word
"cer" (12r l.21, bold with a blot) is a line-end break ("cer-ca" over to l.22).
