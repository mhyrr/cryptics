Conventions I chose:
- Looped "q/9" signs inside clear Spanish (very frequent, e.g. "lo (9) conuiene") written {q} within [[ ]]: I read them as the abbreviation for "que", not cipher. Forms with a cross inside ({q+}) and one open form ("lo G E" 179v) are flagged. This is an interpretation; if they are signs, they number ~40 on 179r, ~12 on 179v, ~12 on 180r.
- "/_" = virgula with dot below (the tall stroke with dot under it, 179v). "/." on 179v/180r = virgula + period, as in clear punctuation.
- "Xe", "du", "ro", "co" written as joined letter pairs, one token each; could be two signs or clear-text letters (X e might be a long-s "se").
- <sign:Z-with-crossbar> = a Z-like sign with a middle bar and dot below (179v L16, 180r L14); possibly crossed 7.
- Digits "7" (bold, angular) vs "y" (curly with tail): kept as seen; many "7"/"y" in the clear text are the conjunction, so clear/cipher call is uncertain at "7"/"y" in cipher runs (e.g. 179v "d+ y a_ 3", 180r "7 9+ y 20").
- "14+" read for a "1" + 9/4-like glyph with plus; the same glyph recurs ("19+"|"14+" possible). Hardest.
- Plus signs are read as marks after the sign per brief; they may be the digit 4 in this hand.
