## Method and honest limits of the second pass
- Pass 1: left to right on native strips, line by line, then reconciled the left and right halves on the overlap.
- Pass 2 (right-to-left re-check): this was done fully only where the left/right overlap forced a re-read of the same glyphs (every line, at the join of the two halves), and as an independent zoomed re-read on f. 25r lines 1 to 3 and on a few hard stretches (f. 22r lines 1 to 2, f. 22r line 8, f. 23v lines 1 to 2, f. 23r line 12, f. 23v line 19). It was NOT a complete independent second read of all ~190 manuscript lines. Changes from the re-reads:
  - f. 22r: normalized `2 0` to `20` throughout (guide: numbers run 2 to 23; "20" is one sign). Small hooks that I first wrote `p` (f. 22r lines 3 and 4) are `e` (hook), not the letter-form p.
  - f. 23v line 12 / f. 22v lines 14 to 15: line identities were re-checked by matching the overlap between halves; two lines I had mis-assigned were corrected.
- Treat dot marks (`^`, `_`, `.`) as the least reliable layer on the dense, show-through-affected pages (f. 22r, f. 23r lower half, f. 23v, f. 24v). I did not re-check every dot.

## Show-through (important)
The scan is high-contrast black and white. Verso ink shows through, mirrored, and is dark enough to be mistaken for text:
- f. 22r: faint mirrored f. 22v text in the margins and between lines (ignored).
- f. 23r: strong mirrored ink from f. 23v in the lower half and between lines (ignored; lines 22 to 29 are heavily affected).
- f. 23v: lines 1 and 2 are covered at the left by DARK mirrored ink from f. 23r; only the right-hand non-mirrored part is transcribed. The real left part of those two lines is not visible to me (wall, see below).
- f. 24v: mirrored f. 24r text at the left of every line and between lines; f. 25r: mirrored numerals along the right margin (ignored).
- f. 23v bottom: below the clear paragraph a thick struck-through scrawl, then only mirrored ink.

## Letter-forms and special signs used (and where)
- `<rho>`: 91 occurrences, on every cipher page. Typical contexts: `12. <rho> 3`, `<rho> 20+ 10` (recurring formulas; search the file). Some may be a 9 with a long tail or an 8 with a long tail; they were hard to separate from `9`/`8` in the dense hand. Uncertain ones are marked `<rho>?` (e.g. f. 22r 16, f. 23r 1, f. 24v 5).
- `<venus>`: once, f. 22r line 25 (the line `16+ 5+ <venus> 18 <rho> ...`).
- `R` (21), `C` (23, mostly with a dot above or below, e.g. `5 C 12 18`, `C^`), `f` (26, long f with dot after, e.g. `18 f. 20 18`), `p` (28, mostly in `p p` / `p e` pairs), `n` (17, also `n+`, `n_`), `m` (3: f. 22v 6, f. 23r 12, f. 23r 25), `h` (3: f. 23r, f. 25r line 4, and f. 23v; search `h `), `P` (2: f. 22r 23 and 27, written `P e 13`), `v` (26, "v" with a leading horizontal stroke, e.g. `2 v 14`, `6 v 14`).
- A bare circle `0` (with or without a cross: `0+`, `0e`) occurs repeatedly (f. 22r 16, 23, 25; f. 23r; f. 24v; f. 25r). The guide has no 0. It might be a "10" whose 1 is a long stroke, but I wrote `0`/`0+`/`0e` as seen. When "2 0" was clearly one numeral I wrote `20`.
- `a`: a small "a" with a dot below at the start of f. 25r line 1 (`<sign:a-shaped letter>_`) and an "a"-like sign in f. 23r line 12 (`<sign:a-like with flourish>`). `d`-shaped sign with a cross, f. 24v line 13. A tall capital-I-like sign, f. 25r line 6, f. 22r line 3, f. 22v line 1.
- `<bar>` (33): bars over `6+`, `12`, `18`, `2`, `19+`, `11`, `7`, `10`, `16`, `0e`, `1578`.
- `/` is used for the thick slash that ends paragraphs (f. 22r 8, 22v 8 and 9, 23r 4, 9, 23v 19, 24r 8, 24v 5, 22, 25r 8, and mid-line slashes). `,` virgula-type commas used where seen as separate small marks.
- Clear Spanish is in `[[ ]]` with diplomatic spelling; abbreviations were not expanded. Many words are marked `?`.

## Every `?` (summary; each is at the token it follows in `transcription.txt`)
97 `?` marks in total (f. 22r 25, f. 22v 12, f. 23r 25, f. 23v 15, f. 24r 9, f. 24v 9, f. 25r 2). They fall into four groups:
1. Unsure sign identity inside a clean run (e.g. `9?` vs `8`, `4?` vs `9`, `3` vs `5`, `12` vs `17`).
2. `<rho>?`: loop-and-tail signs that could be `9` or `8` with long tails.
3. Clear-text words that are partly guessed (f. 22r line 2 "yhe?", f. 23v "seria?", "embo?", "Chrmo?", "meledro?", "enfuya?", f. 24r "seruira?", "assi ?", f. 25r "Antseros.?", "viij.?").
4. Right-edge signs cut by the page margin or gutter (e.g. f. 22v 15 `1 8?`, f. 24v 18 `5?`, 23 `2?`, f. 26 `18?`).

## Hardest places
- f. 22r line 8 (`<sign:smeared run ...>`): a blotted or struck run of about six signs after `16+`, and a second smear after `2` (overstrike). f. 22r lines 2 and 20: partly show-through-overlaid.
- f. 23r lines 22 to 29 and f. 23v lines 1, 2, 5 to 7 and 18, 19: mirrored ink overlaps real ink; several runs are given as `<sign:smeared run>`. f. 23v line 19 has a heart-shaped blot before the final `/`.
- f. 24r lines A to E (cipher after the clear paragraph, lines 3 to 6 of that block): smeared runs at the line starts, not resolved.
- f. 24v lines 14 to 23: many dots, bars and `0+` signs; the `<bar>` placement (which sign it covers) is the weakest decision.
- Dots between lines: `^` vs `_` on f. 22v and f. 23v could not always be told apart.
- Clear text: f. 22r line 1 `14?` (the numeral is a "q"-like 4), line 4 numerals "26 / 28. 29 ... 6. 10. 13 y 18." (dates; written as clear text numerals), f. 23v lines 5 to 10 (struck words), f. 24r lines 3 to 6.

## What is not part of the letter (not transcribed)
- f. 22r: "D. p. 12" pencil at the foot of the page; the page number "22". f. 23r "23", f. 24r "24", f. 25r "25" at top right.
- f. 25r: rotated archival annotations and a faint circular seal in the lower left; mirrored numerals in the right margin.
- Signatures on f. 25r: a large flourish (probably the king's "Yo el Rey") and below it "Antseros." with a large flourish; the reading of the second is uncertain.

## Wall
Left halves of f. 23v lines 1 and 2: dark mirrored ink from f. 23r masks them completely at this scan quality, so whether real cipher stands there is undecided. A different scan or a raking-light image would be needed.
