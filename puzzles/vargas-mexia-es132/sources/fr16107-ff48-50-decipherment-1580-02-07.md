# BnF fr. 16107, ff. 48r–50v: court decipherment, "7 Febvrier 1580, Dechiffré de la precedente"

**Source.** Gallica `btv1b9009661v`, views 52–55 (https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b9009661v). PRIMARY.
Read 2026-11-10 by the main thread from full-size images (cache `fr16107/full/v52–v55.jpg`, bands in `fr16107/feb7/`).
The sheet is a French court clerk's decipherment in a fast secretary hand. **This is a working reading, not an edition.**
Words in `[ ]` are doubtful or supplied by sense; `[…]` marks words not read. About one word in six is uncertain.
The sheet deciphers the letter "sur la mort du Roy de Portugal" (ff. 51r–56v): its first 27 letters match that letter's first two cipher lines (`analysis/12-sweep-fr16107/join-7feb.md`). The first words are "Ilz sont".

## Summary in English (what the sheet says)

1. **Madrid's alarm (f. 48r).** "They" (the Spanish court) are astonished that the people of Portugal have held so firm since
   the late King's [declaration], protesting they will stay free sooner than be subjects of the Castilians. Since the news of the
   death came, the grandees hold council on Portugal. A [grand master of artillery] is sent in haste to inspect all the
   frontiers of Portugal and put the artillery in order. The news that the Portuguese claim the right to elect a king
   troubles them greatly.
2. **The twelve theologians (f. 48r).** "Le Roy catholique a assemblé douze des plus fameux théologiens qui se retrouvent en
   ceste cour" ("The Catholic King has assembled twelve of the most famous theologians found at this court") and asked
   whether he may [in good conscience] force the Portuguese by arms to receive him as king if they will not. All assured
   him [that he may].
3. **Forces (f. 48r).** The forces now on foot are put at fourteen thousand men; the Italians are dying fast and others
   [desert]. No news of the lansquenets; it is said they would not embark. The cavalry is badly armed. "Ung amy m'a dit"
   ("a friend told me") that the Catholic King's [first equerry] has orders to have the stable, arms and [equipment] ready
   with nothing left to wait for.
4. **Dom António (ff. 48r–48v, 50r).** The rumour persists that Dom António is in Castile; Saint-Goard does not believe it.
   Six thousand arquebuses have reached Lisbon, said to come from the armouries of the duke [of …].
5. **A night visit (ff. 48v–50r).** "A ce mesme instant, qui sont les dix heures de la nuit, m'est venu secrettement
   [visiter] le seigneur don Rodrigue de Lancastre, cousin germain du duc de Bragance" ("At this very moment, ten o'clock
   at night, the lord Dom Rodrigo de Lencastre, first cousin of the duke of Bragança, has come secretly to [see] me").
   - Dom Rodrigo reports that Philip sent the secretary **Çayas** to him that morning with "infinies offres", to have him
     press the duke of Bragança to come to terms early and to keep out of "les bras des françois [ny] des anglois"
     ("the arms of the French or the English"), who would bring the ruin of the Portuguese.
   - He asks Saint-Goard's advice on what the duke should do, and says the Portuguese would elect a king of their own
     nation and die to a man sooner than live under the Castilians.
   - Saint-Goard asks whether the duke is a man to defend his claim and what means he has. Dom Rodrigo: the kingdom's
     forces are [twenty] thousand men, poorly [armed]; they need "la faveur d'un Roy puissant" ("the favour of a powerful
     king"), and Henri III could have that if he would undertake it.
   - Saint-Goard says he is surprised the duke did not declare himself to Henri III sooner, but that it can still be done;
     he sets out what France can supply (men, victuals, munitions, the ease of the sea passage). He advises that the duke
     send someone to Henri III "avec bonne instruction pour traitter", and that the King could send someone to the duke.
   - They agree, "avec tout le secret du monde", that each will report: Dom Rodrigo to the duke, Saint-Goard to the King.
   - Saint-Goard's own advice to Henri III: keep a man in Portugal, of good judgment, to report and to deal with the duke.
   - Asked why the duke had not armed earlier, Dom Rodrigo answers that the late King "l'avoit tousjours entretenu, le
     trompant" ("had always strung him along, deceiving him"), promising to put the kingdom peacefully in his hands.
   - Dom Rodrigo believes Dom António is not in Castile but hidden in Lisbon, or treating with the duke of Bragança.
6. **Blockade (ff. 50r–50v).** The Spanish are doing all they can to stop ships from the Biscay coast carrying victuals
   or munitions to Portugal. Saint-Goard suggests warning French subjects trading [under cover of grain] to look to
   their navigation.
7. **Close (f. 50v).** The ambassador of Portugal has just sent word that the Catholic King told him and Dom Rodrigo de
   Lencastre that he would soon take them with him into Portugal, where he means to go.
