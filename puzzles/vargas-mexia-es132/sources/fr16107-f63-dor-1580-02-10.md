# BnF Français 16107 f. 63r–64r: Pierre Dor to Saint-Goard, Lisbon, 10 February 1580

- **Object:** BnF Français 16107, f. 63r (Gallica `btv1b9009661v` view 68, right page), f. 63v (view 69,
  left page), f. 64r (view 69, right page: end, date and subscription). PRIMARY.
- **Images used:** `sources/cache/fr16107/F68.jpg`, `F69.jpg` (8756 x 6210), cropped into bands at
  native or 0.6–0.85 scale. Read once by one agent under a 30-call budget; there was no second reader.
- **Heading (archival, top of 63r):** "10 febvrier 1580 / Lisbonne" — "De Pierre Dor au s[ieu]r de S[ain]t Goard". Foliation 63 (old "18" struck).
- **Hand:** one secretary hand for the body. The cipher has a **contemporary decipherment in the left

- Conventions: `[?]` doubtful word; `[...]` illegible; `[x]` supplied. Line breaks are the MS's.

## Summary (English)

Dor wrote on 3 Feb from Almeirim announcing the King's death; this letter answers Saint-Goard's of
20 December and 4 January. He has held back enclosures from a well-informed man (to whom Beauvais[?]
and Longlée spoke when lately in Lisbon) because the road is unsafe. Portuguese affairs are in great
confusion: the Cortes broke up without resolving anything; before dying the King told the people he
was ready to give sentence for the King of Castile, and they answered they would never be Spain's and
nearly rioted. Part of those in power want to give the kingdom to Spain; the people will not hear of
it. Government rests with the Archbishop of Lisbon, don João Mascarenhas, Francisco de Sá, don João
Telo and the governor of Lisbon, whom the people distrust and want to replace. Two masters of
requests are to summon Philip II to lay down arms and submit his claim to justice or declare war; if
he refuses they will at once make the Duke of Braganza king. Braganza and don Antonio are at odds,
each wanting the crown; the people are trying to reconcile them and want don Antonio as king.
In cipher (per the marginal decipherment): Dor gave "the letter" to don António de Castro, who is
pro-Spanish; don Antoine told Dor he wishes to put himself in the King's hands and at his will; a
reference to the Queen Mother. In clear: Dor offered his services to the bearer (Pierre Juncos[?]) as
ordered; complains he has had no letters or news from France for a long time and is amazed so little
account is taken of so important a matter; he understands what the King's service needs and would do
more if assured of being **avoué** (backed); the Estates have summoned him to Almeirim for the week,
he thinks to show him the pretenders' acts, which he requested on behalf of the Queen Mother's claim;
he leaves tomorrow. The Portuguese are resolved never to take Spain as king; known Spanish partisans
risk "a St Bartholomew". He could not find the "singe[?]" Saint-Goard asked for. Plague in Lisbon.

## Targets checked

- **don Anthoine / l'infant:** yes — clear text "le s[ieu]r don Antonio" (twice, 63r end); margin
  decipherment "Don anthoine m'a dict qu'il se veult mectre aux mains du[?] Roy ...". "L'infant" not seen.
- **don Anthoine de Castre:** margin "don anthonio de Castro, il est espaignol" — recipient of "la lettre".
- **Cascais:** not found. **Governors:** yes, named (63r). **Bragance:** yes, "le duc de Bregance".
- **French ships, men or money:** no request found in the clear text or the marginal decipherment.
- **Instructions from Saint-Goard / the King:** "suivant ce que me commandés par la vostre" (offer to
  the bearer); "le singe[?] que me commandés"; Dor asks, implicitly, to be "avoué" before acting.

## What is unread

- The cipher itself was **not decoded independently**: 0 of ~150 glyphs assigned a value by me, i.e.
  ~100% unknown glyph-by-glyph. Its content is known only through the marginal decipherment.
- Margin lines 7–12 ("[?] [?] et sur[?]", "veult[?] [?] mand[?] les[?] [?] Ma[jes]tez") are partly
  lost in the gutter shadow.
- Spot-check of margin vs glyphs: **inconclusive.** Group `* cc∫ H` fits "a don" if `H` = n, and the
  first group `T̶ x°ʎHʎ` could be a J'ay sign + "donne"; but the margin's first line sits beside C2, not
  C1, and I could not make one line's glyph count match its plaintext. The decipherment is accepted as
  a contemporary reading, not verified.
- Several clear words marked [?], notably "Beauvais", "Juncos", "singe", "cortes", "avoué" sense.

## Limits

- **The cipher is not Longlée's cipher as reconstructed by Mousset (1912) or Tomokiyo.** Of their
  signs only `#`/`###` (s), `∞` (l) and perhaps a crossed-T word sign recur; the commonest signs here
  (`d`-loop, `H`, `ʒ∫`, `Ꝙ`, `ɤ`) are absent from both keys. Probably Dor's own table with Saint-Goard.
  Contested/unknown; do not merge with the 10-saint-goard sign list.
- Single reader, one pass on the clear text (no second pass on the cipher was useful without a key).
- Show-through on 63v is light and did not obstruct reading; the gutter darkens the margin note.
- Whether this "answers" Saint-Goard's 23 Dec 1579 instruction (keep don Antoine in hand until the
  King's will is known) is an interpretation: the marginal "Don anthoine ... se veult mectre aux mains
  du Roy ... sa volunté" is consistent with it, but the King is not named as France in what I could read,
  and Dor complains of having no instructions from France.
