# Hypotheses

Numbered, never renumbered. Status: `open` / `supported` / `weakened` /
`refuted` / `superseded by Hn`. Every hypothesis names the test that would
move it. Results are recorded in `research.md` and in `analysis/`.

| # | Hypothesis | Status | Test that would move it | Evidence so far |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | F. 539 is a three-layer cipher: a homophonic letter alphabet of unmarked signs, a marked-sign syllabary or small nomenclature, and numeric code groups for names. | open | Blind transcription: do marked and unmarked signs have different frequency profiles? Do numbers repeat in name-like positions? | Bourdeau's reading of the structure (one reader). |
| H2 | The letter layer is breakable ciphertext-only the way Marmont was (annealing on French n-grams). | open | Length gate (≥ 300 letter-layer tokens), then a matched synthetic control, then the target. | Bourdeau: 234 letter tokens; controls 1–18%. Against, pending our own counts. |
| H3 | F. 539 shares a key with f. 555 (Lasry), f. 530 (Pelissier–Joyeuse) or the Caulet–Joyeuse cipher of August 1593. | weakened | Shape comparison of the sign inventories; trial decode with a published key. | Tomokiyo and Bourdeau say no for all three. |
| H4 | The code numbers belong to a numeric nomenclature shared by the Rome office, the same one as the "ij^c xiiij" style numbers in f. 553 (Joyeuse to the duc de Joyeuse, same day). | open | Find a key or a deciphered letter that uses the 100–300 numbers; check the repeated 152. | Bourdeau notes f. 553's numbers sit in the same range. Suggestive only. |
| H5 | `par`, `til` and `dd` (Bourdeau's labels) are nulls or separators. | open | Positional statistics in the blind transcription: spacing to neighbours, adjacency to numbers. | Untested. |
