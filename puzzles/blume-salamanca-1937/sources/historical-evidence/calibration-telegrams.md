# Calibration telegrams (Spanish, c.1930-1939) -- CALIBRATION ONLY

**This file is NOT evidence about the BLUME SALAMANCA telegrams.** It collects real Spanish-language telegram and cipher-message texts with a known source, so that letter statistics of Spanish telegraphese (letters only, no N-tilde, no accents) can be calibrated. Written 2026-10-20 by the procedure/calibration research agent; companion to `agent-procedure.md`.

## What was and was not achieved

- The target was 5-20 messages, ideally 1,500 letters or more, preferably commercial wool or trade telegrams from c.1930-1939, each independently sourced. **No commercial telegram text from this period was found online in transcription.** Searches of PARES, Biblioteca Virtual de Defensa, prensahistorica, Cervantes Virtual and the USF Spanish Civil War History Project turned up only images without transcription (USF items), pages that could not be fetched (a Vasconia article with 1938 Aguirre-Irujo telegrams, HTTP 400; prensahistorica, HTTP 429; USF PDFs, HTTP 403), or OCR too garbled to use. See the search log in `agent-procedure.md`.
- What is here: one 1937 plain-language telegram (P1, seen as a transcription only), eight 1937 Republican official telegrams (M1-M8, deciphered, printed in a modern edition, **not independent of each other**: one source), and four Spanish military messages from 1916 (H1-H4, from an American handbook, **out of period and probably constructed**).
- **Orthography caveat.** M1-M8 carry accents and N-tilde because they are deciphered texts typeset in a 1994 article, not what went over the wire. A transmitted version would drop both (Madrid Telegraph Regulations 1932, Art. 12, see `context-cribs.md` section 2). Do not use M1-M8 to count N-tilde. P1 keeps the telegraph form (capitals, no tilde) but the editor left a few accents in. H1-H4 are uppercase and unaccented as printed.
- **Source dependence.** M1-M8 are one source. P1 is another. H1-H4 are a third. That is three independent sources, not 13.

## P1. Valencia to the Spanish Embassy, Buenos Aires, 5 June 1937 (plain language, telegraph form)

- **Date:** received 5 Jun 1937 (stamp; the text itself has no date). Service line: `DAH2 VALENCIA 349/348 4 21.00 NIL DIVLN`; address `LO EMBAJADA DE ESPANA BAIRES`; signed DOMENCHINA. Carried by The Western Telegraph Company, Buenos Aires.
- **Source:** Maria Teresa Pochat, "Un telegrama de hombres de ciencia, artistas y escritores a la Embajada de Espana en Buenos Aires", *Olivar* 9(11), 2008, pp. 145-149, https://olivar.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/article/download/OLIv09n11d01/pdf/4691 (downloaded, text extracted with pdftotext). **Tier: SCHOLARLY** (editor's transcription of a PRIMARY document). Original image: only the cover page is reproduced in the PDF; the full four-page original was **not seen**.
- **Editor's statement of method:** capitals, abbreviations and "ortografia original (ausencia de tildes y grafema n-tilde)" (original spelling, no accents, no n-tilde) were kept. The editor nonetheless prints a few accented capitals (AFIRMACIÓN, AQUÍ, JOSÉ); these are probably editorial, and are left as printed.
- Hyphen line-breaks are the printed ones and are kept. Service lines between telegraph pages are kept in the text.
- **Letters in the block below:** 1654 (counts service lines and signatures).

## M1-M8. Republican ministerial telegrams, February-May 1937 (deciphered; printed edition)

- **Source for all eight:** Jose Luis Gordillo Coucieres, *Historia y Vida*, April 1994, as reproduced on Arturo Quirantes' site, https://www.ugr.es/~aquiran/cripto/museo/marcantabrico.htm (downloaded as HTML, text extracted; accents in the page were mis-encoded and were recovered by decoding as Latin-1). Gordillo says the texts come from decipherments made by the Balearic Command's "Negociado de Informacion" (Mallorca) of Republican traffic in the keys "Bocho" and "Victoria", from notes in the private archive of General Enrique Puig Guardiola. **Tier: SECONDARY** (a magazine article in a university researcher's web page; the underlying decipherments are not independently seen). **Original image: not seen.** Spanish; no English needed beyond the glosses below.
- **Why they are still useful:** they are real, dated, official Spanish telegrams, written in telegraphese (articles and prepositions dropped), with the dropped-word style that a commercial telegram would share. They are **not** commercial and they are **not** in transmitted spelling.
- Line breaks are the page's own and carry no meaning. A few words in the page text show OCR slips of the 1994 article (for example "corno" for "como", "v" for "y", "Y" for "y"); they are left as found, so counts may be off by a letter or two.

### M1  13 Feb 1937, 09:15

- Valencia to Bilbao; Minister of Navy and Air (Prieto, per the article) to President of the Basque Government. Clave Bocho. Letters in body: 903.

```
Estamos examinando la idea de establecer en Bilbao una
fábrica aviones en que se construiría bien el aparato americano "Martin Bombers"
y el "Fokker 31". Ruégole llame al ingeniero aeronáutico Cerro de servicio en
Lamiaco y exponiéndole esta idea nos diga dados los elementos que ahí podrían
reunirse y clase materiales que han de emplearse en uno y otro modelo cual
estima preferible para fabricación ahí, o si simultáneamente pudieran
construirse ambos.  Primera condición es encontrar local adecuado y en
condiciones relativa seguridad y con terreno próximo que pueda acondicionarse
para aeródromo.  Sigue clave T. en T. Díceme que en caso reúna ambas
condiciones Zamudio donde instalóse fábrica Esperanza que antes se hallaba en
Marquina y otro aspecto a estudiar inmediatamente es posibilidad reunir ahí con
rapidez y urgencia caso requiere todo elemento mecánico para producciones
referidas y suplico V.E. tome con máximo empeño esta iniciativa y me dé cuenta
su desarrollo v previamente deme seguridades que espero respecto posibilidad
realizar este proyecto. (Fin del comunicado.)
```

```

### M5  7 Mar 1937, 21:15

- Valencia to Bilbao; Minister of Navy and Air to President of the Basque Government (relays a telegram from the ambassador in Mexico). Clave Bocho. Letters in body: 695.

```
Embajador España en México en despacho recibido hoy me
comunica lo siguiente: Me telegrafía vapor "Mar Cantábrico" diciendo llegará
frente Santander lunes tarde y pregunta si es mejor entrar por la noche.
Creyendo ese punto debe decidirlo V.E. se lo comunico para que haga transmitir
sus órdenes a barco la estación de Santander.  Le digo que caso no recibir esas
órdenes entren de noche.  También me dice "Mar Cantábrico" que si es protegido
por aviación conviene que aeroplanos no vuelen sobre el barco para evitar
confusiones y corno añade que no comunicará con estación Santander ni Bilbao más
que en caso forzoso, creo convendría fuera ésta o escuadra quienes le llamaran
para establecer contacto.  Me permito encarecer a V.E. emplee nuestros barcos de
guerra para proteger "Mar Cantábrico", que seguramente estará muy vigilado por
el enemigo. (Fin.)
```

### M6  10 Mar 1937, 05:00, urgentisimo

- Santander to Valencia; Government delegate for Santander, Burgos and Palencia to Minister of Navy and Air. Clave Victoria. Letters in body: 574.

```
No obstante ser previstos todos los servicios aptos para
mejor resultado aún seguimos sin tener referencias "Mar Cantábrico".  Informes
extraoficiales parecen indicar que "Mar Cantábrico" fue sorprendido por faccioso
"Canarias" y los tripulantes de aquél optaron por incendiario hundiéndolo sin
que se tengan más referencias y por tanto desconocerse suerte tripulación.  Esta
referencia repito no asegura veracidad aunque indicios parecen acusar
verosimilitud.  Presencia "Canarias" este litoral dificulta grandemente
navegación entre puertos leales, pues barco faccioso con aviación que porta
vigila estrechamente zona leal. Le tendré corriente cuantas noticias obtenga.
Salúdole.
```

### M8  7 May 1937, 00:35

- Valencia to Bilbao; Minister of War to President of the Basque Government. Clave Bocho. Letters in body: 488.

```
Recibo su despacho descifrado en que me comunica haberse
hecho cargo mando Ejército Euskadi.  He de participarle a usted que como
ministro de la Guerra desapruebo dicho acuerdo Gobierno autónomo. Ni el
presidente del Gobierno de Euskadi ni el consejero de Defensa del mismo están
autorizados legalmente para ejercer mando militar.  Este corre a cargo del
general jefe del Ejército Operaciones Norte que mientras no sea destituido

Glosses. M1: the Navy minister asks the Basque president to consult the aeronautical engineer Cerro about building an aircraft factory in Bilbao (Martin Bombers, Fokker 31). M2: a relayed telegram says Mexico will not allow ships under the Mexican flag to leave. M3, M4: a reminder of earlier instructions about the steamer Mar Cantabrico. M5: the Mexico ambassador reports the Mar Cantabrico will reach Santander Monday afternoon (the telegram the Nationalist cruiser Canarias is said to have exploited). M6: unofficial reports say the Mar Cantabrico was surprised by the Canarias. M7: acknowledges a telegram about deserting destroyer officers. M8: the War minister disapproves the Basque government taking command of the army.

## H1-H4. Spanish plain texts from Parker Hitt, *Manual for the Solution of Military Ciphers* (1916) -- OUT OF PERIOD, probably constructed

- **Source:** Parker Hitt, *Manual for the Solution of Military Ciphers*, Army Service Schools, Fort Leavenworth, 1916, Project Gutenberg #48871, https://www.gutenberg.org/files/48871/48871-0.txt (downloaded and searched; plain text, not page images). **Tier: SCHOLARLY** (a handbook). The texts are worked examples. Hitt says only that of "hundreds of actual cipher messages" examined, nine-tenths were solved; he does not say these four are real. They are Mexican-border military Spanish, 1916, so they say nothing about 1937 commercial wording. **Original image: not seen** (Gutenberg text from Internet Archive scans).
- **Why they are listed:** H1 and H4 show X as a trailing filler or stop in a Spanish transposition and a Spanish substitution example from 1916 (H1 ends `FINISX`; H4 ends `NOCHE X.`). H3 shows `(X) Fin`. This is the only pre-1937 evidence we found of X as a stop or filler in Spanish-language cipher examples, and it comes from a US handbook, not from Spanish practice.

### H1

- Spanish transposition message, text solved by Hitt's alternate-diagonal route (120-letter cryptogram, 5 x 24 grid, 5-letter groups). Plaintext as printed in the book, reassembled across three quoted fragments (pp. of Case 1-f). Letters: 120.

```
ME SITUO SOBRE PARRAL PORQUE ME PRESENCIA FUE REVELADA POR UNA PAREJA QUE SE ME ACERCO Y HUBO QUE RECHAZAR POR EL FUEGO ALLI ESRERO ORDENES FINISX
```

### H2

- Spanish military message, Caesar-type shift, Hitt Case 4-a (cipher OBQFO BPBRP QBAML OBHIF PILFQ FJBOX OFLNR BIXOZ EL; plaintext computed by script from the shift A=X in Hitt's 24-letter alphabet). Letters: 42.

```
RETIRESE USTED POR EL MISMO ITINERARIO QUE MARCHO
```

### H3

- Spanish military message, six-alphabet substitution with key TOLEDO, Hitt (the multi-alphabet example solved by frequency tables). Plaintext as printed, lower case in the book, accents absent. Letters: 385.

```
Una fuerza de caballeria enemiga procedente de Aranjuez y Villaseca se halla en Azucaica. Marche usted con su compania partiendo de la casa de la serradero por las alturas de lo este y norte de Azucaica con el fin de reconocer su numero y clase de fuerzas y en disposicion que se halla. (Q) Esta acantonada (Q) Se hallan otras tropas detras de ella (Q). El resultado del reconocimiento necesito saberlo dentro de tres horas y media cuando mas. Pongo a sus ordenes un ciclista (X) Fin.
```

### H4

- Spanish military message, running key VIVA_ADERO (ten-letter key word), Hitt Special Solution for Case 7. Letters: 60.

```
PARA EL ATAQUE CONTRA TORREON ORDENO QUE SUS TROPAS MARCHEN ESTE NOCHE X.
```

The ciphertext of H1 as printed (24 x 5 grid, five-letter groups) is in Hitt's Case 1-f example; reproducing it here is not needed for calibration.

## Total

All texts together: 5929 letters. Of these, 5322 are 1937 Spanish telegrams (M1-M8 plus P1), not independent beyond two sources, and 607 are 1916 handbook examples. The 1,500-letter target is met only by counting the M-set, which is one source and not commercial. **Treat statistics from this file as a rough check on Spanish telegraphese, not as a reference corpus.**

## Not included, and why

- Gordon Ordas' instructions to the captain of the Mar Cantabrico, 17 Feb 1937 (same web page): a letter (a despacho), not a telegram, about 1,900 letters. Available at the same URL if letter statistics are wanted.
- Telegram of 26 Jul 1936, Morocco command to Las Palmas, in two-digit groups, only five words decrypted on the page (Boletin ENIGMA 11, https://www.ugr.es/~aquiran/cripto/enigma/boletin_enigma_11.htm): too little plaintext.
- Franco and Mola radiograms printed in Palma newspapers, 31 Jul and 4-5 Aug 1936 (prensahistorica.mcu.es): fetch refused (HTTP 429) and search-snippet OCR is garbled; not transcribed here.
- USF Spanish Civil War History Project telegrams to Josefa Oural, 1936 (https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/span_civil_war/74 and neighbours): images only, PDF download refused (HTTP 403), no transcription on the page.
- Aguirre-Irujo telegrams, 22 Jul-15 Nov 1938, transcribed by George Hills in *Vasconia* (https://ojs.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/index.php/vasconia/article/view/467/459): the page returned HTTP 400 to every client; would be a good source if reachable by other means. 1938 and political, not commercial.
- Wool or other commercial telegrams: none found. FRUS 1935-36 prints US consular telegrams about the cotton trade in Barcelona, but in English.
