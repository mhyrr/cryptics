# Plain-text guide — item 2946 / 2947 (frozen 2026-11-19)

You transcribe Italian written in 1439 in a chancery hand: the clear words of the letters, and the small words
a second hand wrote between the lines over the cipher. Write what the scribe wrote.

## The page
- A letter line holds clear Italian words, runs of cipher signs, or both. The signs are invented shapes
  (crosses, deltas, forks, loops, bars). Do not transcribe the signs.
- On four pages (images 691–694) a second, smaller hand wrote words in the space **above** many cipher runs.
  That is the gloss. It belongs to the line **below** it. Some gloss words stand at the right margin, at the end
  of a line.
- Page c. 215 has no gloss. Tell us if you find any.

## How to write a line
One manuscript line per row:

    L07 y=410 | Hora venendo a rispondere [cipher]{partissemo} ale parte [cipher] [cipher]{tanto bene}

- `L07` is the line number, counted from the top of the page. Count only letter lines; a gloss row is not a line.
- `y=410` is the ruler figure (left edge of the strip) level with the middle of the line's letters, at its start.
- Clear words as written.
- `[cipher]` once for each unbroken run of signs, at its place in the line. Start a new run where clear words or a
  wide gap split the signs.
- `{...}` directly after a `[cipher]`: the gloss words written over that run, in their order. A run with no gloss
  gets no braces. If one gloss phrase spans two runs, give it to the run under its first word and say so in NOTES.
- A gloss word at the right margin, not over a run: write it at the end of the row as `{margin: ...}`.

## Spelling
- Keep the spelling, including u/v, i/j, doubled letters, `et`, `ch`/`c`, missing h.
- Do not expand abbreviations silently: write the letters you see and put the supplied letters in round
  brackets, `M(agnificen)tia`, `p(er)`. A raised ending is written on the line.
- A word you cannot read: `[...]`. A doubtful word: the word then `[?]`. Part of a word: `parti[...]`.
- A word struck out: `<struck>word</struck>`. A word inserted above the line **in the main hand**: `^word^`.
- No punctuation is required. Keep capital letters as written.
- The gloss is small and faint. An honest `[...]` is worth more than a guess that reads well.
