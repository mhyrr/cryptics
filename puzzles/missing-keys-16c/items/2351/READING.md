# No. 2351 — the reading so far (2026-11-18)

Roberto Acciaiuoli, Florentine ambassador in France, two letters in cipher from Amiens, 23 October 1513
(ASF, Mediceo avanti il Principato, filza LXXII, docs. 190–192). How each figure was got: `README.md`.

Rules of this file. Decoded lines are script output (`results/b-decode.txt`); `?` is a sign with no value or
an unread place. Word division, letters supplied in square brackets and all English are interpretation and
are mine. Names of people and the historical setting are from memory and are not verified.

## The key
`key-v0.tsv`, 36 entries. Thirty come from B. Dudout's reconstruction of Francesco Pandolfini's cipher of
1514–15 (CLAIMANT; Bourdeau's `cyphersolver`), mapped onto our sign names; 22 of them are confirmed on
letter A's pair. Six were added from that pair. A figure above a consonant sign gives the vowel after it:
3 = i, 4 = e, 5 = a, tilde = o. About a third of the signs have no value yet, among them the commonest
unmarked sign (`delta`) and the word signs.

## Letter A (to "the Magnificent Giuliano")
Its chancery copy in clear is c. 233. English from reader PE's transcription: see `readers/PE/NOTES.md`. In
short: the Pope's dealings with France sit badly in many hands; keep them to Monsignor de Marseille alone;
Acciaiuoli has worked on Robertet about the embassy of obedience; the French will do what the Pope wants.

## Letter B (to the government, "Magnifici Domini"): no decipherment in the file
1,885 signs read, 1,291 valued. The decode runs to 41 lines in `results/b-decode.txt`. What it says, as far
as it goes:

- Lines 1–4. By his last of the 22nd he told them of [the news] … that [the King] had ordered Monsignor …
  sent into Burgundy with [some] hundred lances and … thousand [foot] … "maraviglia che inespectatamente"
  (a wonder that, unexpectedly) … "la spesa" (the expense) … "in pericolosi …" (in dangerous …).
- Lines 5–8. "felicemente" (happily) … "ancora maraviglia" … "non … potevono passare più avanti che l'impeto
  de' [Svi]zzeri" (they could go no further than the onset of the Swiss) … "con la copia delle lettere"
  (with the copy of the letters) … "sopra questo disegno et pensiero" (on this plan and thought).
- Lines 9–13. "le parole del [Re] col quale habbiamo parlato: si vede li ha dato questa cosa molestia et
  rotto qualche disegno" (the words of the King, with whom we spoke: one sees this matter has troubled him
  and broken some plan). "Da un canto lo preme la paura … venissino in Borgogna, dall'altro lo sdegno contro
  a' S[vizzer]i, de' quali mi parlò molto ingratamente" (on one side the fear presses him that they come
  into Burgundy, on the other his anger against the Swiss, of whom he spoke to me very ungraciously) … "la
  paura dello esercito inglese" (the fear of the English army).
- Lines 14–18. The King, quoted: they "had no regard for me nor [my] kingdom … in danger … tried to make me
  lose it". They had sent to Monsignor [..] to learn "qualcosa dello animo del Re" (something of the King's
  mind), and "Monsignore li haveva risposto che nessuno poteva meglio sapere l'animo di [Sua Maestà] che
  loro medesimi, e quali erono consci se li havevono facto iniuria o no, et che examinassino le conscientie
  loro" (my lord answered that none could know the King's mind better than they themselves, who knew
  whether they had wronged him or not, and that they should examine their consciences); finding they had
  offended him, let them hope for [nothing]; finding they had done him no wrong, let them await …
- Lines 19–21. "la stagione del verno potria ramor[z]are lo sdegno et volgere li animi a riposa[rsi]" (the
  winter season could damp the anger and turn minds to rest) … "questo romore et demonstratione".
- Lines 22–32. "Le gente disegnate per andare in … non sono ancora andate, né son mosse" (the troops meant to
  go to … have not yet gone, nor moved) … a count of "… mila et … cento lance et … mila" … "el quale
  esercito" … "si ne licentia" (is being dismissed) … "da pochi giorni in là" … "el resto della gente" …
  "in queste frontiere" (on these frontiers) … "scrivete liberamente" (write freely).
- Lines 33–41. "Circa lo spirituale" (as to spiritual matters): "si fa l'ordinatione del mandare" (the order
  for sending is being made) … "credo passeranno molti giorni" (I think many days will pass) … "questi del
  consiglio … sono deliberati … da non li negare cosa nessuna" (those of the council are resolved to deny
  him nothing) … "sendo seguita la tempesta … le cose in termine che in questo verno non sono per alterarsi,
  ci pare che Dio ci monstri la via … da venire al fine desiderato di quietare la Cristianità, di che Dio li
  conceda gratia; che di qua credo sia per trovare quella facilità che desidera" (the storm having passed
  … with things so placed that they will not change this winter, it seems God shows us the way to the
  desired end of giving peace to Christendom, for which God grant him grace; for here I believe he will find
  the ease he desires).
