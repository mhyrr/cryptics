# Sign guide — cipher of item 2351

Frozen 2026-11-18 before any cipher reader runs. **Shapes and names only.** Nothing here says what a sign
means, and you must not guess. Your job is to write down which shape stands where.

Made by eye from parts of letter A (bands A01, A03, A04, A11). It will have gaps: section 3e says what to do.

## 1. What the page looks like
Lines of signs written one after another, with no word spaces. A sign is a **base** standing on the line.
About half the bases carry one small **mark** written above them: a tiny figure, a tick or a wavy stroke.
Bases are ordinary letters, figures, capitals and a few invented shapes. A few clear words open and close
the letter.

## 2. How to split the line
1. One sign = one base, plus the mark above it if there is one. Write `base^mark`: `x^4`, `y^5`, `3^~`.
2. A mark belongs to the base directly under it, or, if it sits between two bases, to the base on its
   **left**. A mark never stands alone and never makes a token of its own.
3. Long tails from the line above (of `y`, `q`, `p`, `flong`, `Q`) cut into the line you read, and they pass
   through the row of marks. A stroke that starts in another line is neither a sign nor a mark.
4. Doubled bases are written twice: `8 8`, `3 3`.
5. A sign the scribe struck out with a long stroke is left out; say so in NOTES with its line. A sign under a
   crease or stain that you cannot read is a bare `?`, one per sign.
6. Clear words go in double square brackets, letter by letter as written: `[[adi xxi la sera]]`.

## 3. Bases

### 3a. Small letters and figures: write the letter or figure itself
`c` `n` `o` `p` `q` `r` `s` `t` `x` `y` `z` and `2` `3` `4` `5` `6` `7` `8`. Points to hold to:

| name | what it looks like | do not confuse with |
|---|---|---|
| `x` | a large cross of two diagonals; the commonest wide sign | `t` (upright, with a foot) |
| `t` | short upright with a crossbar; looks like a small `+` or `†` standing on the line | `x`; `tt` |
| `r` | short stem with an arm to the right, often like a small `v` or `γ` | `7`, `2` |
| `7` | one flat or sloping arm and a short leg, like `>` or `7`, no crossbar | `r`, `2` |
| `2` | a low wavy `2` or `z` drawn in one stroke, often with a long flat entry stroke on its left like `-2` | `z`; `dash` |
| `z` | angular: flat top, diagonal, flat base, often with a bar through the middle | `2`, `3` |
| `3` | figure 3 on the line; its lower bowl may drop below the line | `z`, `brace` |
| `5` | small 5 or s with a flat top | `s` |
| `s` | small round s, no flat top | `5`; `S` (capital, full height) |
| `8` | figure 8, often small and filled | `beta`, `delta` |
| `6` | bowl with a rising curved stem, like `6` or `b` | `delta`, `beta` |
| `4` | open-topped 4 on the line | `q`; `L4` |
| `q` | bowl on the left with a straight stem down; like a `9` | `4`, `y` |
| `y` | two short arms and one long tail down to the left | `q`, `7` |
| `c` | small open curve | `sigma` |
| `o` | small plain ring on the line | `sigma`, `delta`, a raised `^o` |
| `n` | two short stems joined at the top; `m` has three | `tt` |
| `p` | bowl on a stem that goes below the line | `q`, `phi` |

### 3b. Capitals: write the capital
`A` `B` `D` `G` `H` `M` `Q` `R` `S` `T` `Z`. `D` is a squarish capital D and is common. `Q` is a round
bowl with a long tail sweeping right under the next signs. `M` is a pointed capital M; a similar sign with a
looped first stroke, like `ZM` or `2h` run together, is `zm`. `R` often has a small ring or dot after its
foot: that is part of it. `S` is a full-height capital S.

### 3c. Invented shapes

| name | what it looks like | do not confuse with |
|---|---|---|
| `flong` | tall stem with a crossbar near the top and a loop or hook at the foot far below the line, like a long ſ or `f`. Very common | `gamma`, `t` |
| `gamma` | tall stem with a flat arm to the right at the top, like `Γ` or `[`, no crossbar, no loop at the foot | `flong` |
| `brace` | tall thin zigzag like `}` or `ʒ` or a slim `3` that reaches from above the small letters to below the line | `3`, `5` |
| `delta` | ring with a stroke rising from it and bending left, like `δ` | `6`, `8`, `o` |
| `beta` | tall looped sign like `ß` or a `B` open at the foot | `8`, `B`, `6` |
| `sigma` | ring with a flat bar running right from its top, like `σ` | `o`, `6` |
| `omega` | wide double bowl under a flat top bar, like `ϖ` or `ω` with a lid | `sigma`, `n` |
| `tt` | two or three short stems under one crossbar, like `tt` or `H` or `ttt` (write `tt` for two stems, `ttt` for three) | `hash`, `n` |
| `hash` | two slanted stems crossed by two bars, like `#`, often ending in a looped tail like `#y` (the tail is part of it) | `tt` |
| `L4` | a large `4` whose flat stroke runs out to the left along the line under the sign before it, like `L4` or `44` joined | `4` |
| `Lz` | a large `L` or `[` with a small `z` or `x` sitting in its corner | `gamma` |
| `phi` | ring with a long stroke through it, like `φ` or `Ø` | `p`, `q`, `o` |
| `triangle` | small closed triangle, like `Δ` or a filled `A` without legs | `A`, `D` |
| `dash` | a long flat stroke on the line, standing alone between two signs. The flat entry stroke of a `2` is not a `dash` | `2` |
| `alpha` | round loop with a tail curling up to the right, like `α` or `&` | `Q`, `o` |
| `i` | one short upright stroke | a `^1` mark, which floats above a base |
| `pt` | a `p` and a `t` joined by one stroke | `p` then `t` apart |

### 3e. A shape that is not in the tables
Give it a short name made from what it looks like (`boxbar`, `hook`), use the same name every time, and
describe it in NOTES with its line and place. Do not force it into the nearest listed name. A name uses
letters and figures only.

## 4. Marks above

| mark | what it looks like |
|---|---|
| `^1` | one short upright or slanted tick |
| `^3` | tiny 3 |
| `^4` | tiny 4 |
| `^5` | tiny 5 or s |
| `^~` | short wavy or curved stroke, like a tilde or a small arc |
| `^n` | tiny n or m shape, like a low double arch |
| `^o` | tiny ring |
| `^.` | one dot |

Another tiny figure or letter above is `^` and that character (`^2`, `^a`). Two marks on one base are
written lower first: `x^3^~`. Marks are small and faint: look at the base first, then straight above it.
A `^3` and a `^5` differ at the top: `^5` has a flat stroke, `^3` is round.

## 5. Notation
- One manuscript line per row. Tokens separated by single spaces. First row of the file is `# A`.
- Doubt: add `?` to the token (`sigma?`). Two close readings: `6|delta` (likelier first). A doubtful mark on a
  sure base: `x^4?`. Unreadable sign: bare `?`.
- Nothing else in the file. Remarks go in NOTES.

## 6. Worked example
Band A01, third `c`, second line: after the clear words "adi xxi Lasera" the cipher begins:

    [[adi xxi lasera]] alpha^1 i^o x^4 x^4 tt beta 7^4 3^3

- `alpha^1`: a round loop with its tail up to the right; a tick stands above-right of it.
- `i^o`: a short upright with a tiny ring over it.
- `x^4 x^4`: two large crosses, each with a tiny 4 above.
- `tt beta`: two stems under one bar, then a tall looped ß; no marks.
- `7^4 3^3`: the tiny 4 sits over the 7, the tiny 3 over the 3.

In the next line of the same strip a long stroke cancels several signs between `7` and `r`: they are left
out, and NOTES says so.
