## Names I made up (guide 3e)
- `bbar`: a 6 (bowl with a rising stem) whose tall stem is crossed by a horizontal bar near the top, like ƀ or "f" over "6". Common (13 times). First seen in row 5, after `q i? i?`.
- `h`: a tall stem with a hooked top, an arch to the right and a tail down-left, like a cursive h. Rows 5 (after R) and 13 (after D^n).
- `loop`: a tall narrow loop leaning right, open at the foot, like an inverted U or ∂. Rows 6 and 7, both times in the sequence `y r^3 loop t^~`. It may be a variant of delta.
- `yloop`: a y whose tail turns back into a closed loop on or below the line, like ɣ or ȣ. In rows 6, 15 and 9 it is followed by a short flat stroke to the right, each time after `r^1`. It has no crossbar. With a crossbar I wrote `hash` instead (see below).
- `Aloop`: a capital-A-like sign whose top is a closed loop at upper left, with a flat base. Row 7, after `L4 hash`.
- `cup`: a U- or V-shaped cup open at the top, with a small dot or ring inside near the top and a tail at upper left. Row 16 (after `3 c`) and row 22 (after `beta c`).
- `pct`: a dot on the line, then a long slash with a foot, then a small ring at its lower right, like "%". Row 17, after `8^~`. The large cross-shaped strokes right after it I read as one large `x` with a hooked right tail. This whole group, `pct x`, is the most uncertain split in the letter. Crop: `crops/A08b_L.png`.
- `dagger`: a tall stem going below the line, crossed by two short bars, like ‡. Row 14, first sign. A crease runs through it.
- `rc`: a reversed c (ɔ), larger than the ↄ-shaped 7. Ends rows 4, 14 and 19, and stands mid-line in row 22 (before `3^3 zm`). It is possibly a line-end sign.
- `theta`: an 8- or θ-like loop with a right leg descending, like ϑ. Row 12, after `tt|hash`.
- `Li`: a large L with a small "1" or "i" in its corner, the way the guide's `Lz` has a z. Row 12, after `R|beta`.
- `dot`: a single dot standing on the line between two signs, written as its own token. It appears 11 times: `3 dot 4` in rows 10, 12, 20 and 23; after `r^5` in row 25; before the clear words in row 27; at the end of row 15; and after Q (written `dot?`) in rows 11, 15, 19 and 25. The dots after Q may belong to the Q's tail. In row 25 the mark after Q looks more like a comma. In row 11 the dot sits inside the sweep of Q's tail.
- `dotbar`: a dot above a short flat stroke on the line. It ends row 22.
- `T`: a capital T (flat top bar, upright stem). The guide does not list it among the capitals. It appears in row 5 (first sign, `T^o`), row 21 (last sign), and row 22 (`T^.`, a dot above).

## Conventions I settled on (check against other readers)
- **ↄ before t.** A small reversed-c shape directly before a `t` (looking like ↄt) I wrote as `7 t`. It appears many times. If it is a separate sign from 7, these all change.
- **tt and hash.** `(tt`, a curved stroke followed by two stems under one bar, is `tt`; the curve is part of it. A y-like sign whose arms are crossed by a bar and whose tail loops (#y) is `hash`, with the tail part of it, as the guide says. So "tt" followed by the looped y is `tt hash` (rows 7 and 19), and "n" followed by the looped y is `n hash`. A plain # without the looped tail after a small z (z#) is `z hash`.
- **L4.** A tall L-stem with a doubled-stem 4 beside it ("44 joined") is `L4`. Zoom: `crops/A02a_L4z.png`.
- **Mark between two bases.** Most marks sit above and to the right of their base. When a mark stood over the gap between two bases, I gave it to the left base, as the guide says. When it stood directly over the left part of the next base, I gave it to that base. The cases I could not settle are flagged in the rows (`?`) or listed below.
- **^5 and ^1.** A slanted tick with a visible s-curl or hook at its top (ʃ) I wrote as `^5`. A plain straight tick I wrote as `^1`. These are the most frequent marks and the hardest to tell apart at this resolution. Expect disagreement here.
- **s and 5.** I wrote `5` only where the top stroke is clearly flat, and `s` otherwise. `S` is full height.

## Things the guide missed
- `T`, the stand-alone dot, and the reversed-c (`rc`) are not covered. Neither is ↄ (I wrote it as 7). The guide also does not say what to do with dots after Q's tail, or with a dot above a sign (for example `i^.` in row 18 and `T^.` in row 22).
- `L4` followed by a looped y crossed by the L4's own bar: I wrote it `L4 hash` (rows 7, 24). It could be one sign.
- A "q" whose tail forms a box-shaped loop under the line occurs often (rows 5, 9, 18, 25), always followed by thin strokes: `q i? i? bbar`, or `q q tt hash` in row 18. In row 18 two q's seem to share one box-shaped tail.

## Method and passes
- Pass 1 went top-down, band by band. I enlarged half-thirds 2× with ImageMagick (`crops/A<band><third>_L|R.png`) and enlarged single spots 3–4× where in doubt. I made every crop in `crops/`, and opened no other images besides the allowed strips.
- Pass 2 went top-down again. For each line I looked at the `-all` band strip first, read the bases off in order and counted them, and then compared that count with the row in `A.txt`. Every row's count matched after I settled one apparent extra "3" in row 9: the overlap had made me see the same sign twice. I re-checked overlaps by matching signs at the joins between thirds. Pass 2 was a count-and-overlap check from the strips. It was not a second full reading of every mark at crop resolution, so mark readings rest mainly on pass 1.
