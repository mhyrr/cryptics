## English summary (my reading, partial)

Acciaiuoli writes privately, beside his public letters, after the arrival of one Bastiano sent from Rome by
(someone of the house of) Sanseverino. He reports how the French handle their dealings with the Pope through
"Monsignor de Marsilia" (the bishop of Marseille, apparently the French agent in Rome). Through Robertet he
dropped a word about Monsieur de Solier: that His Holiness's negotiations do not sit well in so many hands,
that the Pope cannot open his mind to all the agents, and that it matters to restrict the business to Marsilia
alone, since whatever is treated with him there will be approved here. Knowing Solier is a friend of Robertet
he did not press further, so as not to displease. With the Grand Écuyer he will keep to the order given him.
On the embassy of obedience he has already worked with Robertet, and there is talk of sending someone "of the
blood" (doubtful); he will speak to the King when occasion offers. He has not yet raised the resignation of a
benefice (name unread), waiting for the right time. A friend told him yesterday morning that the French will
do whatever the Pope wants in everything, and that any sign they have from the Pope would be enough for them.
