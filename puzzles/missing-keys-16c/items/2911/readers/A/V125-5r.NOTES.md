## Names I made up (section 3e)

- `cbar` — a c shape (as tall as the small letters, sometimes a little taller) with a straight horizontal
  bar inside its mouth, like ∈ or a Greek epsilon. Very common (lines 2, 3, 6, 7, 8, 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17).
  It is not `e` (the eye is not closed) and has a bar, not a tick, so I did not call it `sigma`.
- `Gtail` — a G or ς shape whose lower part drops well below the line as a tail curling left; like a
  large Ꮹ. Lines 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, 16, 18. It is taller than `sigma` and has no tick; it
  is rounder at the top than `3tail`.
- `tbb` — one upright or slanted stem crossed by two short bars, like ǂ or a t with two crossbars
  (no second stem, so not `hash`). Lines 6, 7, 8, 16, 17.
- `c4` — a curl like c joined to a figure-4 shape whose stroke runs down as a tail, like ϥ or ɥ.
  Lines 4, 5, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15, 18. I could not settle whether this is one sign or `c` + `4`; I wrote one token.
  Where it stands next to an `alpha`-like shape I gave `c4|alpha`.
- `eng` — n whose right leg runs down below the line, like ŋ. Lines 4, 7, 11, always followed by `3`
  (`eng 3^o`, `eng 3`).

## Shapes from the tables used with a note

- `4` — the figure 4 is frequent on this page, chiefly in `n 4` (lines 2, 3, 9, 10, 12, 13, 14) and
  `i 4` (many lines). The `i` before a `4` is a short stroke that sometimes leans into the 4 or crosses it
  (`ɫ4`); it could be a `1`. I wrote `i` throughout.
- `k` — plain k letter form: `k k` at the start of line 4 and in lines 12 and 16.
- `triangle` — small closed triangle, frequent after `p` (`p triangle`), lines 3, 4, 5, 9, 10, 16, 18.
- `tt` — includes a π-like sign (two stems under one flat bar that overhangs) at lines 5 (`tt?` after
  `s s`), 11 (start, `tt?`), 12, 14, 15, 16. Could be `n` where I marked `?`.
- `O` (large ring) at lines 7 (twice). `circdot` for the large ringed dot opening line 2: it is much
  larger than the other `circdot`s.
- `h h` (open below, unlike the closed `b b`) at lines 3, 4, 10.
