## General: the hand and the resolution

- The hand is not the sample hand. It is smaller and denser, and many shapes are drawn differently. At the
  resolution of the half strips, small marks and the insides of rings (dot vs bar vs empty) are often at
  the edge of what I can see. **Treat this page as lower confidence than the token-level `?` count suggests.**
  Many tokens without `?` are still best guesses between close shapes (b/h, g/q, z/3/3tail, c/e).
- Rule 6 split ("two stems under one bar"): this hand writes a recurring group that looks like `H` followed
  by a free stem (`Hl`): two stems joined by a bar, then a third stem the bar may or may not reach. I wrote
  it `ttt?` everywhere (lines 1, 2 twice, 8, 19, 22, 24, 26, 31, 32 twice). The other reading each time is
  `tt i`. In line 8 the same group (in the same run `d s p s _ moondot` as line 1) could also be `t r i`.
- A flat-topped two-legged sign shaped like π is common. The guide's `n` has an arch and `tt` has a bar; π
  has a flat bar on top and no stems rising above it. I wrote `tt?` for π (lines 1, 2, 3, 4, 9, 12, 13, 16,
  25, 29) and `n` only for a rounded ∩. A reviewer may decide that π is this hand's `n`.
- Slanted two-stem signs with bars: `hash` where I could see two bars or a #-shape, `tt` where I saw one.
  Many of them are borderline.
- `A`: the capital A in this hand nearly always has an extra stroke running from the crossbar out to the
  right, so it looks like "At" (the run `o A g sigma` recurs: lines 3, 4, 9, 13, 28, 31, plus `g A o` in 4, 7).
  I wrote it `A` as one sign, not `A t`. Line 29 has a plain A (`sigma A g`) without the extra stroke.
- `i x` (a tall-ish single stem directly followed by a small x, "1x"): recurs (lines 1, 2, 6, 9?, 11, 21, 23,
  28, 31). I wrote `i x`, two signs. It might be one fused sign (compare the guide's `Lx`, which this page
  also has in its usual "Lt" form on line 19). The stem is taller than a normal `i`.
- `q` vs figure 4: the "43" pair that recurs all over the page has a closed bowl, so I wrote `q 3`, as on
  the sample (sheet 2.3.3). The `y` in the recurring pair "y 8" is drawn open, like a 4 with a long tail; I
  wrote it `y` throughout. If you are checking for figure `4`, these "y 8" pairs are the place to look.
- `d` vs `delta`: the bowl + tall looped stem leaning right is `d`. A ring with a flag bent left (∂) is
  `delta`. Doubled `d d` in lines 13, 17, 19 could be `delta delta`.
- `alpha` vs `ex`: a small loop with one long tail down-left (looks like ꝯ or σ with a tail) is `alpha`.
  A larger e-loop on a cross with a long stroke down-left and a short one down-right is `ex`. Several are
  borderline, and long strokes from the line above often cross these signs.
- `3` vs `3tail` vs `z`: I wrote `3tail` where the lower curve plainly drops below the line, `z` where the
  top is flat and the base flat, and `3` otherwise. Many are borderline (for example line 1 end `z? 2`,
  line 21 `z 2`, line 23 `3tail`).
- `bbar` vs `b`: a b with a ring above usually also has a crossbar here (`bbar^o`). A `b` with no ring I
  wrote plain `b` unless I saw a bar.
- `moondot`: in this hand a tall crescent `(` with a dot. Where the dot is not visible I wrote `moondot?`.
  In line 6 the crescent after `w` has a short **horizontal stroke** in the mouth, not a dot (€-like). I
  wrote `moondot?`; it could be a new sign.
