## General

- **Hand and resolution.** This page is in a different hand from the sample page, and the strips are
  small: the signs are about 15–25 px tall. Many calls are by gestalt, so treat the whole page as
  lower-confidence than the `?` count shows.
- **`qj?` (about 25 places).** This is the commonest doubt. The sign is a bowl on the left with a stem that
  runs down below the line, and the top of the stem rises a little above the bowl and curls (like ꝗ / ɖ).
  It could be `qj` or this hand's `d`. I wrote `qj?` wherever the stem clearly descends. Where the stem
  rises and leans right with no descender ("cl"-like, as in `g d z` on lines 4 and 11 and `d d` on lines
  11, 13 and 14), I wrote plain `d`.
- **`d?` (lines 7, 10, 16).** The same descending bowl-and-stem, but followed by a small raised c-like hook.
  It could be `qj` with a raised mark (`^c`), or a d whose loop is drawn apart.
- **`n` before `4`.** The recurring pair `n 4` (lines 2, 3, 8, 10, 12, 13, 14) has an angular n, almost a
  capital N. I wrote it as two signs, `n` and the figure `4`. It could be one fused sign.
- **`i 4`.** This pair recurs many times. The `i` is a tall upright like the figure 1. I wrote two signs.
- **Figure `4`.** Per the guide, listing that I wrote `4`. It also stands alone (lines 9, 13, 14, 15, 18).
- **`c t`.** This pair recurs, with c and t touching (lines 3, 4, 6, 8). In line 4 (`c t?`) the t looks
  more like a 4.
- **`A t`.** This pair recurs often (`A t qj?`, `G A t`). It is written as two signs, capital A then t, and
  it could be one sign.
- **`G`.** A capital-G letter form with a tail below the line (Ꮹ). It is not in the tables, so I wrote it
  as the letter. It could be a large `sigma`, but it is taller and has a tail.
- **`k`.** A plain letter k (lines 4, 12, 16), written as the letter per the guide.
- **`ybar` / `y`.** Most 7-shaped signs with a long tail seem to have a short stroke across the tail, and
  I wrote those `ybar`. Those without a visible stroke are `y`. At this resolution the difference is
  unreliable.
- **`tt?` for π shapes.** A π-shaped sign (a flat top bar with two legs hanging) appears on lines 11, 12,
  14, 15 and 16. I wrote `tt` under rule 6 (two stems under one continuous bar). It could also be an
  `n` with a flat top.
- **`tt|hash`.** The `#`-like pair of slanted stems, where I could not tell one bar from two (lines 6, 14
  and 18).
- **`r^o^wave`** in the recurring group `3 p t r^o^wave` (lines 2, 6, 7). A ring and a wave sit above
  between t and r. I gave both to r. The wave could belong to t (`t^wave r^o`).

## Names I made up

- `epsbar`: a crescent open to the right, with a horizontal bar at mid height, like ∈ or €. It is the
  height of the small letters or a little taller. It is common, and differs from `moondot` (which has a dot,
  not a bar).
- `tdbl`: one upright stem crossed by two horizontal bars, like ǂ or ‡ (lines 6, 7, 8, 16, 17). It differs
  from `hash`, which has two stems.
- `dash` (line 12, `m dash? 8`): a short horizontal stroke on the line between m and 8. It may be only
  m's exit stroke, in which case it is not a sign.
