# Plain guide — clear-text pages of item 2911 (V/139 cc. 1v and 2r)

Frozen before any reader runs. For the two readers of the clear-text side only.

## What the pages are

Two pages in one fast Italian cursive hand of the early sixteenth century, in brown ink: about thirty
lines each, a heading line at the top of the first page, a short separate paragraph at the foot of the
second. The writer was working quickly: there are deletions, words added above the line, and marks in the
margin. The text is said to be inexact and incomplete. That is a fact about the source: **do not repair it.**

## What to produce

A **diplomatic transcription, line by line**: what the writer wrote, in his spelling, in his line breaks.

- One manuscript line per row. The first row of the file is a comment naming the page: `# page V139-1v`.
  No other comment rows, no blank rows, no line numbers. The heading is a row. A gap between paragraphs is
  not a row.
- **Spelling as written.** No modernising, no correcting, no added accents or apostrophes, no added
  punctuation. Keep `u` and `v`, `i` and `j`, `h`, double and single consonants exactly as written
  (as `havere`, `decto`, `et`). Write capitals only where the writer does.
- **Word division:** follow the manuscript where it is clear. The writer often joins short words to the
  next (as `ilre`, `dellacitta`): keep them joined. Where you cannot tell, divide as the sense asks.
- **Abbreviations are expanded in round brackets**, with only the supplied letters inside:
  `p(er)`, `ch(e)`, `n(on)`, `q(ue)sto`, `m(esser)`. A stroke over a vowel for m or n: `co(n)`, `gra(n)de`.
  The sign for "and" is written `et` if it is spelled out and `(et)` if it is a sign. If you cannot tell
  what an abbreviation stands for, write what is on the page and add `[?]`.
- **Deletions:** `<del>…</del>` around the struck-out letters, read as far as you can
  (`<del>fat</del>`; unreadable: `<del>[…]</del>`).
- **Interlineations:** `<ins>…</ins>` around words added above the line or in the margin, placed where
  they were meant to be read.
- **Unread word:** `[…]`, one per word. **Doubtful word:** the word, then `[?]` with no space:
  `guerra[?]`. Doubt about one letter is doubt about the word.
- **Figures** as written (`5`, `x`, `iij`). Money and number abbreviations as written.
- **Marks in the margin** (crosses, lines, a later folio number, a library stamp): not in the file. List
  them in NOTES with the line they stand beside.
- Nothing else in the file: no translation, no remarks, no editorial brackets other than those above.

## How to read this hand

- Read at full size. Long s and f differ only by the crossbar. `e` is often a bare tick; final `e` and `o`
  are easily confused, so look at each one. `u` and `n` are alike: count minims. `c` and `t` are alike.
  `r` has two forms. `h` in `ch` often carries the abbreviation stroke for `che`.
- A `p` with a stroke through its stem is `p(er)`; with a curl before it, `p(ro)`; with a bar above,
  `p(re)`. `q` with a mark is usually `q(ue)` or `q(ua)`: choose by the letters that follow, and add `[?]`
  if the choice is not forced.
- Use the sense of the sentence to choose between readings, never to supply a word you cannot see.
  If a passage makes no sense as written, transcribe it as written and say so in NOTES.
- Do not consult any edition, catalogue or other transcription of this text.

## Passes

1. **First pass.** Band by band: the whole-width strip to see the line, then the left half (`a`) and the
   right half (`b`) at full size. Each band repeats the last line of the band before as its first line, and
   the halves overlap in the middle: write each word once.
2. **Second pass, independent of the first.** Reader A re-checks from the top band down. Reader B re-checks
   from the bottom band up. Look at the strip again before you look at what you wrote. Check every
   abbreviation, every deletion, and the last word of every line.
3. **NOTES.** List every `[?]` and `[…]` with its line number (counted from the first row after the page
   comment) and what else it could be; every deletion and interlineation; marginal marks; anything this
   guide did not cover.
