# Sign guide — cipher pages of item 2911

Frozen before any reader runs. **Shapes and names only.** Nothing here says what a sign means, and you must
not guess. Your job is to write down which shape stands where.

Made from one sample page (V/140 c. 1r; c. 1v used to confirm). Pictures of every named shape are in
`sign-sheet/sheet-1.jpg`, `sheet-2.jpg`, `sheet-3.jpg` (cut by `signsheet.py`). Open them first. In each
cell the sign meant is the one **under the black tick** at the top of the cell; its neighbours are context.

## 1. What the page looks like

Lines of signs written one after another with small gaps. There are no word spaces: wider gaps are
irregular, so do not record them. The signs are of three kinds mixed together: ordinary letter forms,
figures, and invented marks. Many signs carry a small mark written above them. Now and then a few ordinary
words stand in clear among the signs, usually at the start or end of the text.

## 2. How to split the line into signs

1. **One sign = one base shape, plus any small mark floating above it.** Write the base, then `^` and the
   mark: `a^o`.
2. **A mark above belongs to the base it sits over, or to the base just to its left.** The scribe writes the
   base, then lifts the pen and adds the mark above or above-right. A mark never belongs to the sign after it.
3. **A raised small `o` is a mark; an `o` standing on the line is a sign.** The mark is smaller and its
   bottom is at or above the top of the small letters.
4. **Dots.** The dots of `xdots`, `dotcross`, `moondot`, `sigma`, `circdot` and `boxdot` are part of those
   signs. Any other dot: do not make a token; list it in NOTES with its place.
5. **Doubled signs are written twice.** `bb` is `b b`, `sss` is `s s s`, `aa` is `a a`. Count the copies with
   care: runs of two and three both occur.
6. **Stems under one bar** are the exception, because the bar fuses them: one stem with a crossbar is `t`;
   two stems under one continuous bar are `tt`; three are `ttt`; two slanted stems with two bars are `hash`.
   Count stems and bars at full size before you write.
7. **Fused pairs that are one sign:** `d` (a bowl running into a tall looped stem, looks like "cl");
   `dbbar` (a d and a b whose stems are crossed by a double stroke); `Lx` (a capital L with a small cross on
   its foot); `ex` (a loop sitting on a cross with a long tail).
8. **Pairs that touch but are two signs:** `h` then `y` (the right leg of the h runs into the y); `e` then
   `alpha`; `r` then `i`.
9. **Strokes from other lines.** Long tails from the line above (`y`, `alpha`, `ex`, `f`, `p`, `qj`, `phi`)
   cut through the line you are reading, and tall stems rise from the line below. A stroke that starts in
   another line is not a sign and not a bar. Follow it back before you decide.
10. **Line ends.** The last sign of a line may trail a long flourish. Ignore the flourish.
11. **Damage and cancels.** A sign hidden by a blot or stain is `?` (one per hidden sign, as best you can
    count). A sign the scribe struck out on purpose is left out. Note both in NOTES.
12. **A sign written above the line as an insertion** goes where it was meant to be read. Note it.

### 3e. A shape that is not in the tables

The page you read may be in another hand than the sample. A shape keeps its name when it is drawn a little
differently. When it is truly not in the tables, give it a short ASCII name made from what it looks like (`boxbar`, `hook`, `kbar`), use the same name every
time, and describe it in NOTES with the line and its place. Do not force it into the nearest listed name.
A name may use letters and figures only: no space, `:`, `^`, `|`, `?`, `[`, `#` or `@`.

## 4. Marks above

| mark | what it looks like | sheet | watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `^o` | tiny ring above or above-right of the base. By far the commonest mark | 3.1.1, 3.4.3, 3.4.5 | an `o` on the line; `odiv` |
| `^tick` | short upright or slanted stroke above the base | 3.1.2 | the foot of a tail from the line above |
| `^wave` | short wavy or u-shaped squiggle, like a tilde or a breve | 3.1.3, 3.2.1 | `^bar` (straight) |
| `^bar` | straight horizontal stroke over the base | 3.2.3 | `^wave`; the bar of `tt` |
| `^s` | tiny s or 5 shape | 3.1.5 | `^3` |
| `^3` | tiny 3: two bowls open to the left | 3.1.4 | `^s` |

Another small letter written above is `^` and that letter (`^a`). Two marks on one base are written from the
lower to the upper: `y^o^wave`. A stroke *through* a base is not a mark above: use the names in 3b, or
make a name as in 3e. Do not use `:` at all.

**Marks are part of the sign (decision, with the reason).** The same few marks ride on many different bases:
on the sample page, by eye, `^o` alone sits on letter forms (a, bbar, c, f, g, i, m, p, qbar, r) and on
figures (2, 3). So a mark is a productive part of the system, not a quirk of one or
two glyphs, and you must record base and mark separately and exactly, as `base^mark`. For building the key,
though, each base-with-mark is treated as **one sign of its own** (`a^o` is a different sign from `a`, and
is not assumed to be "a plus something"). The reason is what each choice can get wrong. If base and mark
are scored as two units, the method must give `a` one value wherever it stands, with or without a mark: that
is right only if the marks add a fixed piece of text. If the base-with-mark is one unit, both cases can be
learned: an unrelated value, or the base's value with something added. The price is that a rare
combination is seen too few times to be learned. `units.py` does the joining by script after transcription
(`a^o` becomes `a+o`), so nothing is lost on the page record and the other choice can be rerun.

## 5. Notation

- One manuscript line per row. Tokens separated by single spaces.
- The first row of the file is a comment naming the page: `# page V140-1r`. No other comment rows, no blank
  rows, no line numbers: rows are counted, so a row that is not a manuscript line breaks the count.
- Every written line of the text block is a row, including a line that is all clear words (a heading, a
  closing date, a signature line). Leave out folio numbers, library stamps, an address panel or a docket
  on another part of the sheet.
- Token: `base`, then `^mark` for each mark above. `a`, `a^o`, `p^tick`, `y^o^wave`.
- Doubt: add `?` to the token: `sigma?`. Use it when you chose a name but another reading is possible.
- Two close readings: `q|qbar` (the likelier first). No `?` needed.
- A sign you cannot read at all: a bare `?`. One `?` per sign.
- Clear words: `[[...]]`, letter by letter as written, abbreviations left as they stand, no need to
  understand them: `[[abc de]]`. A clear word you cannot read: `[[?]]`.
- Nothing else: no punctuation, no brackets, no remarks in the file. Remarks go in the NOTES file.

## 6. Worked example

V/140 c. 1r, first line, after the clear words that open it (strips `V140-1r-01a.jpg` and `-01b.jpg`).
Shown here on three rows to fit the page. In your file one manuscript line is one row.

```cipher
[[clear words as written]] qj s bbar^o s a^o ex d f sigma dotcross q 3 g r p^tick h y
phi c circdot? xdots a^o ex ybar g phi p^o g phi
a a 8^3 alpha sigma theta xdots sigma circdot 8
```

Notes on the hard splits:
- `qj`: the first sign after the clear words. A bowl, a straight stem, a blob turned left at the foot. The
  long loop to its left is the tail of the last clear word, not part of it.
- `bbar^o`: looks like a t at first. It has a closed bowl at the foot, so it is `bbar`. The ring above-right
  is its mark. The dot after the ring is a stray: NOTES, no token.
- `a^o ex`: the ring belongs to the `a` on its left. `ex` is one sign: the loop sits on the cross, and its
  long stroke runs down under the next line.
- `d`: one sign, although it looks like c and l.
- `sigma dotcross`: the tick in the mouth of `sigma` and the two dots of `dotcross` are not separate tokens.
- `q 3`: two signs. The q has nothing across its stem.
- `p^tick`: the stroke above the p is short and free of the line above, so it is a mark.
- `h y`: two signs that touch.
- `circdot?`: a small ring with a dot, crowded against the left dot of `xdots`; it could be `theta`.
- `ybar`: the stroke of `ex` before it runs close, but the short bar across the tail is the sign's own.
- `g phi p^o g phi`: each `phi` has a stroke through the ring; each `g` has none. If you come to think the
  straight-tailed and the looped g are two signs, still write `g` and say so in NOTES.
- `8^3 alpha`: the raised 3 belongs to the 8. `alpha` has its loop on the left and one tail.

## 7. Passes

1. **First pass.** Open the three sign sheets. Then read the page strip by strip, left half (`a`) then
   right half (`b`) of each band, left to right, at full size. The whole-width strip of a band is for
   finding your place only. Each band repeats the last line of the band before as its first line, and the
   halves overlap in the middle: use the overlaps to join, and write each sign once.
2. **Second pass, independent of the first.** Reader A re-checks from the top band down. Reader B re-checks
   from the bottom band up, and within a band from the last line to the first. Look at the strip again
   before you look at what you wrote. Count the signs in each line again. Where the second look differs,
   keep the better reading and mark the token `?` or `a|b`.
3. **NOTES.** In the NOTES file list every doubt: line number (counted from the first row after the page
   comment), the token, what else it could be. Also: new names you made, stray dots, blots, cancels,
   insertions, anything this guide did not cover.
