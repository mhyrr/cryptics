Frozen before any reader runs. **Shapes and names only.** Nothing here says what a sign means, and you must
not guess. Made by the main thread from the slip on F 49 inf. c. 44r (the clearest) and a look at the others.

## 1. What the passages look like
Lines of separate signs with no word spaces. Each sign is one shape: a figure, a letter form, or an invented
mark. There are no marks floating above signs. Every sign is one token. Write tokens separated by one space,
one manuscript line per row. Ignore a full stop at the end of a passage, the drawn frame, and stains.

## 3. Doubt and new shapes
- A sign you can read but are not sure of: the name with `?` after it (`pi?`).
- Two candidates: `a|b` with your better guess first (`6|b`).
- A sign you cannot read (blot, hole): a bare `?`, one per sign you believe is there.
- A shape not in the table: make a short lower-case name starting with `n-` (`n-hook`), use it every time,
  and describe it in your notes. Do not force a new shape into a listed name.

## 5. Revision 1 (2026-11-17, after the first two readers; agreement on c269 was 0.779)
Made from the two readers' notes and the table of their disagreements. No value is involved.
- **c269 has 15 lines**, not 13. Every line starts at the left edge of the frame. The lines slope down to the
  right, so in a band the right third shows each line lower than the left third does: follow a line across
  by its overlapping signs, not by its height.
- New listed names: `a` — a small closed bowl with a short upright on its right, no descender and no flat
  base (like a printed ɑ); it is common. `slong` — a tall S-like looped stroke reaching above and below the
  line (like ʃ or §). `tau` — one bar on a single upright (T).
- `R` covers both R and a reversed R (Я).
- Look-alikes, decided by one feature each:
  - `phi`: the upright goes **through** a closed circle and shows above it. `q`: bowl with a tail, nothing
    above the bowl, tail plain. `qx`: as `q`, with a short bar across the tail.
  - `6`: always `6` for a bowl with a rising stem. Do not write `b`.
  - `A` has a crossbar, `lam` has none, `a` is small and round, `tri` is closed at the bottom by a straight base.
  - `K` is plain; `zhe` only when a full extra stroke crosses the whole sign.
  - `hash` has two uprights and two bars; `dcross` has one upright and two bars; `plus` one and one.
  - `o` is empty; `theta` has a bar inside; `odot` a dot or tick inside or an open top; `box` and `trid` have corners.
  - `pi` is full height with a roof; `pis` is half height; `omega` is round on top with feet turned out.
  - `u` ends at the line; `utail` has a tail below the line.
  - `l` is tall; `I` is short.
- Signs written small above a line with a caret: leave them out of the rows and describe them in the notes.
- A stroke drawn over signs: transcribe the signs, describe the stroke in the notes.

## 6. Revision 2 (2026-11-19, after readers J, K, L, M on c. 652r)
Made from the four readers' notes and transcription files and from the strips. No value is involved. Where
this section and an earlier one differ, this section wins. Places are given as strip file, third, and the
listed signs on either side, so you can find each example. Pictures in words: `|` an upright, `-` a bar,
`o` a bowl or ring.

### 6.1 `q`, `qx` and `qxl`: what crosses the tail
Look only at the tail below the bowl, and at what crosses it.

| Name | Shape | Seen at |
|---|---|---|
| `q` | bowl, plain tail going down; nothing crosses the tail | `c652-07b`, middle: after `box`, before `v lam` |
| `qx` | bowl and tail, the tail crossed below the line by a **short** bar, no wider than the bowl, that stops on both sides close to the tail | `c44-L`, top line, 5th sign (after `oplus pibox v l`) |
| `qxl` | bowl and tail, the tail crossed below the line by a **long** bar, wider than the bowl, usually running out under the sign before it (and sometimes the one after) | `c652-06c`, left: after `K 2` (bar runs back under the `2`); `c652-06c`, middle: after `l` (bar from under the `l` to past the tail); `c652-07a`, middle: after the dark `6`; `c652-05c`: after `lam` and after `tri l` |

In words: `q` is `o` over `|`; `qx` is `o` over `|` with `-` across the `|`; `qxl` is `o` over `|` with
`---` across the `|`, sticking out under the neighbours.
- The bar belongs to the `q` whose tail it crosses. The neighbour it also runs under is written as itself
  (`l`, `2`, `6`, …), with nothing added. Say in NOTES which neighbours it runs under.
- A long stroke under a sign that **no** tail crosses (the tail stops above it, or there is no tail): write
  the sign as it is and describe the stroke in NOTES. Do not turn a `2` or an `l` into a `q` because of a
  stroke.
- If you see the bar but cannot tell its length: `qxl|qx` or `qx|qxl`, better guess first. If you cannot
  tell whether anything crosses the tail: `q|qxl` (or the other order).
- Whether `qxl` and `qx` are one sign written two ways is not the reader's question. Keep them apart.

### 6.2 `H` against `N`: where the joining stroke meets the uprights
Both are two uprights (often slanted) joined by one stroke. **Size does not decide; the join does.**
- `N`: the join runs corner to corner, from the **top** of the left upright to the **foot** of the right
  upright: `|\|`. Seen: `c652-05a`, right end, the cursive N after the struck ring (`N 4 hash`).
- `H`: the join meets **both uprights between their ends**, at or near mid-height, level or a little
  slanted: `|-|`. Small forms like н are `H` when the join is in the middle. Seen: `c44-L`, top line,
  `l tri l H A 8` (the clear H).
- If you cannot see where the join meets the uprights: `H|N` or `N|H`, better guess first. Small signs
  between `u`, `M` and `H` (two short strokes): `H` only if you see a join between the strokes; `u` if they
  are joined at the bottom; `M` if there are three strokes joined at the top.

### 6.3 Dots and commas inside runs
A full stop or comma **on the line, in the gap between two signs, touching neither**, is written as its
own token: `.` for a dot, `,` for a comma. Seen: `c652-07c`, left half: a dot low on the line after the
`8` of `hash 8`, and another after the q-sign that follows `lam` a little further right.
- A dot inside a ring or between the arms of a sign is part of the sign (`odot`); it gets no token of its
  own. A tick above a sign: no token, describe it in NOTES (as before).
- A dot that might be an ink speck: write `.?`.
- The full stop at the very end of a whole passage is still ignored (section 1).

### 6.4 Strokes drawn across signs: strike lines, dashes, fillers
- **Strike line** (a thin horizontal stroke drawn through several signs at mid-height): transcribe every
  sign under it as if the stroke were not there. Do not count the stroke. Write `[[strike]]` before the
  first struck sign and `[[/strike]]` after the last, so the row shows the stretch. Seen: `c652-05a`, from
  just after the `boxbar` through `3 omega o` to just before `N`.
  - A short slanted stroke that rises from the **left end** of the strike line and has no stroke of its own
    crossing it (only the strike line itself) is the pen starting the strike: no token. On `c652-05a` this
    is the slant just after the `boxbar`.
  - A ring the strike line passes through, where the line comes out of the ring on both sides at the same
    height, is `o`, not `theta`. Write `theta` only if a bar inside the ring sits at a different height from
    the strike or stops at the ring wall while the strike line does not. The ring at the right end of the
    `c652-05a` strike is `o` by this rule.
- **Dash** (a horizontal stroke between two signs, joining them, not crossing several): no token. A ring a
  dash runs out of is `o` if the bar inside the ring continues straight into the dash; `theta` only if the
  inside bar stops at the ring and the dash starts separately. Seen: `c652-06a`, right end, the ring before
  `pibox` (`o—pibox`): by this rule `o`. Say in NOTES that a dash is there.
- **Line-end filler** (a stroke running on from the last sign of a line to the frame): no token, note it.
  Seen: `c652-06c`, right end, after the last sign.
- **Overline** (a stroke over the tops of signs, not through them): transcribe the signs, describe the
  stroke in NOTES with the signs it covers. No markers.

### 6.5 New listed names (each replaces readers' made-up names; see `rename-r2.tsv`)

| Name | Shape (in words) | Seen at | Not to be confused with |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tribar` | a triangle, point up, closed by a straight base, with a horizontal bar crossing it at mid-height that runs out past its sides, at least to the left; the base may also run out to the left: `-Δ-`, like ≜ struck through | `c652-04b`, right end, after `8 v`, before `R` | `A` (open at the bottom, crossbar inside the legs only); `tri` (no bar) |
| `dollar` | two stacked loops (an 8 or S) with **one straight upright** through both, showing above the top loop and running well below the line: `$` | `c652-04c`, middle, after `pi`, before `v` | `slong` (no separate upright); `phi` (one closed ring); `8` (no upright) |
| `lammast` | a Λ whose two legs meet near the **top** of an upright that rises through the apex; any strokes above the apex are short and fan out like a crown: `Λ` with `|` through its point | `c652-04c`, right, after `dollar v` (heavily inked: read the shape through the dark ink); `c652-07b`, middle, after `pi v`, before `8 box` | `zhe` (the diagonals cross the upright at **mid-height**, full-size arms above and below: Ж, as at `c652-06a` after `7 q`); `K` (arms on one side only); `lam` (no upright) |
| `jarch` | one continuous tall stroke: a small hooked foot on the line at the left, rising into a large round arch over the top, coming down on the right into a small bowl on the line. **One token**: the bowl at its foot is part of it, not a separate `6` | `c652-05c`, right third, after `4 M A`, before `l K pi` | `omega` (small, open at the bottom, no bowl); `6` (bowl with the stem rising on the left and curling right); `slong` (reaches below the line) |
| `c` | a ring open on the **right**, like a plain c, often with a small hook at the top | `c652-12a`, first sign of the line | `rc` (open on the left); `o` with a faint side (if you think a side is only faint, `o|c`) |
| `hash3` | three uprights crossed by one or two bars at mid-height | `c269` (reader C saw it in lines 1, 3, 4, 9, 12, 13) | `hash` (two uprights); `rom3` (bars at top and bottom, not across the middle) |
| `pi3` | a roof on three legs | `c269` line 5 (reader C) | `pi` (two legs); `rom3` (closed by a bottom bar) |
| `shatail` | ш with a tail going down from the lower right (щ) | `c269` line 13 (reader D) | `sha` (no tail) |
| `tcross` | one upright crossed by three bars | `c14-c`, the sign before `x A K` near the end (readers E and F) | `dcross` (two bars); `hash` (two uprights) |

Two looks that are **not** new names:
- A `v` whose left arm starts with a small curl or loop at the top (like ʋ) is `v`. It is `odot` only when a
  dot or tick sits **between the arms**, or the ring is closed. Seen: `v` at `c652-07b` after `box q`;
  `odot` at `c652-07a`, right end under the watermark, after `dcross`.
- A ring open at the top with in-curled tips, or a c-like ring with a hook at its lower right, is `odot`
  (section 2), not a new sign.

### 6.6 Smaller rulings
- `4` against `A`: `4` has its right upright running **below** the crossbar to the line, and the crossbar
  runs out to the left: `-|` on a slant. `A` has two legs meeting at the top and the crossbar between the
  legs, nothing hanging below it. If unsure, `4|A` or `A|4`.
- A sign written over in much darker ink: transcribe the shape you see, through the dark ink, with `?` if
  needed, and say "dark ink" in NOTES. A blot with no shape stays `?`.
- Marks at the start of a line that might be clear letters (line 8 of c. 652r): decide by shape only, as
  the note to readers J to M says.
- Made-up names are still allowed for a shape that is in none of the tables (section 3). Before making one,
  check section 6.5.
