## Most important: c269 has 15 lines, not 13

The brief says 13. I count **15 distinct baselines** inside the frame and wrote 15 rows. Each line's
first signs, as I read them:

1 `R qx A N A`, 2 `l 7 v 3 theta`, 3 `qx q pi x phi`, 4 `hash 7 hash 3 M`, 5 `theta qx E sha`,
6 `trid box omega 4 7`, 7 `odot utail sha`, 8 `sha u n-a phi pi`, 9 `plus H n-a n-a`, 10 `I H theta x lam`,
11 `x l R 6 K`, 12 `3 H A pi`, 13 `u v hash dcross`, 14 `pi trid l x 8`, 15 `boxbar A theta x lam`.

Each of these starts at the left frame and shows in at least two band images (for example 2a shows 3–7,
3a shows 5–9, 4a shows 7–12, 5a shows 9–15, 6a shows 12–15). Lines 14 and 15 lie between line 13 and the
bottom frame rule. Line 15 runs on past the frame's lower corner and ends `... pibox boxbar` just above the
rule, at the left of 6c/7c. If the main thread's count of 13 is right, two of my rows must be one line, but I
could not find such a pair. Please check before you align my output with reader B's.

The lines slope down to the right, so a line that is the Nth visible row in third `a` is often the
(N−1)th or Nth in `b` or `c`. I matched the thirds by their overlapping signs, not by row position.

## Names I made up

- `n-a`: a small closed bowl with a short upright on its right that does not go below the line. It looks
  like a printed `a` or `ɑ`. It has no descender, unlike `q`, and no dot, unlike `odot`. It is very common
  in c269 (rows 3–15). At this resolution it is often hard to tell from `box` or a small dark `o`. Where I
  doubted, I wrote `n-a?` or `box?`.
- `n-slong`: a tall, wavy s-like stroke, like a long ʃ or §, with one or two loops. It reaches well above
  and below the line. c269 row 1 (`v n-slong 8 l`), row 2 (`pi n-slong theta`) and row 6
  (`rc n-slong 7`). In row 6 it is shorter and could be a tall `8`.
- `n-cloop`: an open c-shaped sign with a small loop or hook at its lower right, like ɕ. c269 rows 5, 6
  and 9. All are doubtful.
- `n-bigq`: a large sign like a reversed P or a `9`, with a long top stroke running left over the
  previous sign. Only in c14 (after `qx 2`). It is larger than any `q` on the slip and has no tail cross.

## Name choices that may differ from another reader

- `K` written ꝁ-style, with the leg crossing past the upright. The guide's worked line names this `K`, so I
  used `K` in c44. In c269 some K-like signs have an extra stroke across them. Those I wrote `zhe?`
  (rows 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 13, 14). Only c269 row 13 (`odot zhe box`) and row 9 (`l zhe 7`) look clearly
  like ж.
- `B` shapes (a straight left side with two bowls, or a 日-like closed square with a middle bar): I wrote
  `boxbar` or `boxbar?`. Where it could be a figure 8, I wrote `boxbar|8` (c269 row 4) or `8?`.
- Small ш / ɯ shapes: in c269 I used `sha`. In c14 I could not tell M from ш and wrote `M|sha`.
- Small rounded signs with descenders in clusters (c269 rows 4, 6, 9, 11): I could not reliably tell
  `phi` (stroke through the bowl) from `q`/`qx` (tail at the right). I wrote `phi` where the stroke looked
  central and `qx` where a cross showed under the tail. Expect disagreement here.
- `6` against `b`: where the stem looked tall and straight I wrote `6|b` or `b|6`, but never plain `b`
  except in c269 row 8 (`qx b trid`).

## Things the guide does not cover
- `n-a`, `n-slong`, `n-cloop` and `n-bigq` above.
- Dark filled roundish blots are frequent. Where a blot had a clear shape (a solid point-down triangle)
  I wrote `trid` or `trid?`. Otherwise I wrote `?`.
- A `B` or 日 sign that is not quite a square. I used `boxbar`. The guide may want a separate name.
- An interlinear run (c269 above line 11). The guide has no rule for signs written between lines.
