## Conventions I applied (the guide left these open)

- **Rounded "n" with feet turned out (Л / ɲ / Я-like):** these are common. Following section 5 ("omega is round
  on top with feet turned out"), I wrote `omega` for every rounded one, `lam` only for a sharp Λ, and `pi` only
  for a flat roof at full height. This split carries more doubt than any other. Another reader may write `pi`
  or `lam` for many of my `omega`s.
- **B-shaped sign (two bowls or 日-like, a closed square with a middle bar):** written `boxbar`.
- **Outline ▽ (a v closed by a bar across the top) and solid ▼ (often heart-shaped when blotted):** both
  written `trid`.
- **Ł-like sign (an upright with a cross stroke and a foot):** written `E`.
- **`ь` / `b` / `δ` (a bowl with a rising stem):** always `6`, as section 5 says.
- **Crosses and ticks above a sign:** these are usually the crossed tail of a `qx` or `phi` coming down from the
  line above. I did not add them to the sign below. Examples: the "+" over `omega` in line 9 after `x 6`; the
  tick over `pi|lam` in line 11 after `rc`; the T-bar in line 7 after `3 2`.
- **Dashes joining signs** (`Л-φ`, `4-B`, `-θ-`): I treated these as joins and gave them no token.

## Names I made up

- `n-shatail`: ш (three uprights joined at the bottom) with a tail going down from the lower right, like the
  Cyrillic щ. Seen once, in line 13 after `u v H`.

## What the guide missed

- How to choose among `omega`, `pi` and `lam` for the very common rounded n (Л / ɲ). See Conventions.
- Whether the B-shaped sign is `boxbar`. I assumed it is.
- Whether an outline ▽ is `trid`.
- Several signs have a horizontal stroke drawn through them: the start of line 15, `slong theta` in line 2, and
  the end of line 1. Without the stroke, the barred readings (`theta`, `boxbar`, `A`) would change.
- Descenders from the line above (crossed q tails) often look like a cross or roof on a sign below.
- One interlinear group of ordinary size, marked by a † (line 11).
