# Survey: tephra, glass geochemistry and sediment archives for UE 1453

Slice: what tephra evidence exists or could exist for the 1452/53 CE sulfate event, what open data would support a geochemical match, and how earlier "mystery eruptions" were actually solved. I used 16 web searches and fetches. Anything I took from memory and could not check is listed under Unverified claims.

## Bottom line

- **No published tephra from the 1453 layer exists in anything I found.** Abbott et al. 2021, the key TUNU2013 paper, sampled only the 1477 peak and a particle peak around 1479. It did *not* sample the 1453 or 1458 peaks. It describes both 1450s sources as "currently unknown" and names tephra work on them as future work (Sect. 5.2). [SCHOLARLY] https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/17/565/2021/
- Wikipedia cites Cole-Dai et al. 2013: the "lack of tephra discoveries associated with the 1452/53 sulfate layer" is what prevents geochemical matching. [SECONDARY, citing SCHOLARLY] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1452/1453_mystery_eruption ; Cole-Dai 2013: https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jgrd.50587
- **EGU25-2329 (IGE Grenoble) is about 1458, not 1453.** Petit, Savarino, Delmonte, Gautier, Ginot and Batanova report "Cryptotephra fingerprinting of 1458 CE and 426 BCE volcanic events in East Antarctic ice cores". They found medium-K dacite plus rhyolite at 1458, linked the dacite to Kuwae, and attributed 426 BCE to Ambrym. [SCHOLARLY, conference abstract] https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU25/EGU25-2329.html
- So the open gap is the tephra search on the 1453 layer itself, in Greenland ice. The evidence that would settle the source has not been produced, and as far as I can find nobody has announced it.

## 2. Distal tephra or cryptotephra around 1440–1470 CE

- **No NH peat, lake, marine or alpine layer bracketed to 1440–1470 with an unknown source turned up.** One extended search returned only method literature. This is a gap in what I searched, not evidence that no such layer exists.
- **Method precedent: V1477 in TUNU2013** (Abbott et al. 2021). TUNU2013 is a 213 m NE Greenland core (78.04° N, 33.88° W) covering 280–2013 CE. Sample QUB-1965 (78.515–78.655 m) yielded about 126 shards, of which 16 were analysed. They form one tholeiitic basalt population that matches Veiðivötn–Bárðarbunga, with a slight MgO offset. Method: melt, centrifuge, epoxy mount, polish, then 11 major and minor elements on the QUB JEOL FEGSEM 6500F (WDS+EDS). Shards under 20 µm at 1479 could not be analysed. The 1453 and 1458 ages come from layer counting anchored on the 1477 tephra: "ages of 1453 CE and 1458 CE can be attributed, with high precision." Their conclusion is a NH eruption "in 1452 or early 1453 CE." Sulfate deposition was about 25 kg km⁻² in Greenland, higher than in Antarctica. Data are in the paper's Supplement. [SCHOLARLY] https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/17/565/2021/
- **This bears on what a solution needs:** the 1479 case shows shard size (under 20 µm) is a hard analytical limit. A distant low-latitude NH source might deliver only such fine shards to Greenland. LA-ICP-MS or smaller-beam EPMA would be needed, as in Hutchison 2025.

## 3. Calibration set: how mystery eruptions have actually been solved

| Event | Source assigned | Evidence used | Status | Tier/URL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1257/58 | Samalas (Lombok) | Proximal pumice glass matched shards in Arctic and Antarctic ice, plus radiocarbon, stratigraphy and the Babad Lombok chronicle (Lavigne et al. 2013, PNAS) | Partly contested: Narcisi et al. 2019 (QSR) argue the Antarctic tephra is multi-source (Samalas + a Victoria Land eruption in 1259 + another) | SCHOLARLY https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3801080 ; https://www.taldice.org/pub/taldice/Narcisi_QSR2019_1259_multi_eruption.pdf |

| ~230 CE | Taupō (Ōruanui-type glass) | One shard in RICE core, "indistinguishable within error" | Accepted by authors | SCHOLARLY https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-42602-3 |

The pattern behind these successes:
1. Find shards in the ice at the sulfate onset, or just before it (1831: summer, before fallout).
2. Analyse glass major and trace elements.
3. Compare against proximal glass. This often needed *new* proximal fieldwork plus ¹⁴C, because the matching deposit was not already in a database (Zavaritskii, Samalas).
4. Rule out alternatives with S isotopes.

Several cases ended **multi-source or "unidentified Aleutian."** That outcome should be expected and counted as a legitimate result.

## 4. Who holds the 1453 samples; announced work

- **TUNU2013:** the drilling and chemistry were funded by NSF grants to J. R. McConnell (1204176) and to McConnell and N. J. Chellman (1925417), at DRI Reno. Tephra work was done at QUB Belfast (Abbott, Plunkett et al.). The paper does not say where the physical core is held. [SCHOLARLY] https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/17/565/2021/
- **Antarctic 1450s material:** IGE Grenoble (Petit, Savarino, Ginot) and KOPRI (Ro et al., VK22) both have 1458 shards. Neither reports 1453 shards. Because the 1453 signal is NH-dominant, Antarctic ice is the less likely place to find them anyway.
- **Announced 1453 tephra work:** none found in the EGU 2020–2026 searches or elsewhere.

## What a solution would have to do (tephra slice)

1. Recover glass shards from Greenland ice (or NH ice, peat or lake sediment) stratigraphically at, or just before, the 1452/53 sulfate onset.
2. Show they form a coherent population.
3. Match them in major *and* trace elements to proximal glass of a NH volcano with an independently dated mid-15th-century eruption large enough for about 11 Mt S. Ideally the match should be supported by S isotopes showing a stratospheric injection.
4. Rule out Iceland and the Aleutians, which are the usual sources of shards in Greenland that come without much sulfate.

## Where the evidence currently runs out

The 1453 Greenland layer has not been published as having been searched for tephra. The decisive measurement is cheap in principle: QUB-style sampling of TUNU2013 or other DRI cores at about 1452.5–1453.5. But it depends on access to physical ice held by the US ice-core groups. Without it, any candidate volcano remains only a plausible guess.

## Unverified claims

- I did not verify the access details or formats for GEOROC, EarthChem/PetDB, Tephrabase, RESET, the Kutterolf Central American datasets, the Ponomareva Kamchatka tephra compilation, or the Japanese tephra databases. From memory, GEOROC and EarthChem offer downloadable CSVs, Tephrabase is an older Edinburgh-hosted European/Icelandic database, and RESET is an Oxford/UK Mediterranean–European glass database. These need checking.
- I did not check the 1809 eruption's tephra identification, or the 539/540 and 1108–1110 tephra work (I recall Plunkett et al. 2022/23 papers on 536/540 shards in Greenland, and Sigl/Guillet work on 1108–1110). Not verified.
- A search result attributed a 686 CE Greenland shard match to Newberry Volcano (Oregon) through a EurekAlert release. I did not open the release or the paper. [POPULAR, unverified]
- I did not check Eclipse, Mt Logan, Svalbard (Lomonosovfonna), Kamchatka (Ushkovsky) or other NH alpine ice for 1450s tephra.
- I did not verify where the TUNU2013 core is physically stored (presumably the NSF Ice Core Facility or DRI).
- Ro et al. 2025: the claim that "half matched Kuwae, the rest from a Southern Hemisphere source" comes from a popular write-up (https://www.sciencenewstoday.org/scientists-discover-the-1458-volcanic-double-punch-that-silently-cooled-earth-for-decades).
