## 1. Tree rings: the cold year and where it was cold

**The cold year is 1453 (summer). 1454 had partly recovered.** S1 builds a network of 25 Northern Hemisphere maximum-latewood-density (MXD) chronologies; 20 of them carry robust warm-season reconstructions. Across those 20 sites the 1453 mean anomaly is −2.5 °C, "the coldest reconstructed value over the past 800 years." The anomaly comes back to −0.7 °C in 1454 but stays negative until 1463 (−1.3 °C) and 1468 (−0.5 °C). 1459 is only −1.0 °C, and "neither 1459 nor any other year in the mid-fifteenth century" shows a deviation like 1453 (S1, SCHOLARLY).

S2 ranks 1453 as the 5th-coldest NH summer since 500 CE (−1.3 °C in NVOLC v2), behind 1601, 1816 and 536 (S2, SCHOLARLY). Toohey 2019 classifies the 1452 event as **tropical** (Supplementary Table 3). It gives a sulfur injection (VSSI) of 9.97 Tg S and 3-year mean NH summer anomalies of −0.74 (NTREND2), −0.83 (Stoffel et al. 2015, "STO15") and −0.37 °C (Schneider et al. 2015, "SCH15"). Supplementary Table 1 excludes the 1457 event because 1452 interferes with it (S3, SCHOLARLY).

**The spatial pattern (S1).** 1453 was below average at 19 of the 20 sites.
- Strongest cooling, beyond −3 °C: the Eurasian high latitudes from northern Europe to central Siberia. Sites: Polar Urals/central Siberia "Pol" −6.9 °C; northern Europe "For" −3.5 °C and "Nsc" −3.1 °C.
- Northwestern Canada ("Cam"): −4.9 °C.
- Most of North America: −2 to −2.5 °C.
- Central Europe, the Mediterranean and central Asia: weakest. The Swiss Alps reached only −0.4 °C.
- **British Columbia ("Ath"): +0.6 °C.** This is an unexplained outlier at a well-replicated, well-calibrated site (r = 0.69; 16 MXD series in the 1450s). S1 suggests the Pacific Decadal Oscillation or circulation effects.

Ring-width (TRW) networks, unlike MXD, show a gradual cooling from 1446 with an initial minimum in 1453, and their memory effects blur the dating (S1).

**Frost rings and light rings (cited in S1 and S2; the originals were not read):**
- LaMarche & Hirschboeck 1984 report a widespread 1453 frost ring in western US bristlecone pine.
- Filion et al. 1986 and Delwaide et al. 1991 report 1453 light rings in more than 25% of subfossil black spruce from subarctic Québec.
- Briffa et al. 1998 report 1453 cooling across the boreal network.
- Salzer & Hughes 2007 report bristlecone anomalies in the early and late 1450s.

**Implication for location.** The cooling is hemisphere-wide and concentrated at high latitudes. That pattern is typical of a large stratospheric aerosol load, whatever the source latitude, so the cooling map does not point to a region. Its value is the timing: aerosol was over the NH extratropics by summer 1453. Together with first sulfate deposition in 1453 (S2), that puts the eruption in 1452 or early 1453.

**Open data.** S1's MXD chronologies are listed in its ESM (not opened). NTREND and the Schneider 2015 reconstruction are distributed through the NOAA NCEI paleoclimatology portal (https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/paleoclimatology). I did not resolve dataset-level URLs; see Unverified.

## 2. Documentary records, 1452–1454

What I could verify is thin. Nearly all of it reaches the literature through a single 1993 press release.

- **China.** S7 quotes "the history of the Ming Dynasty":
  - spring 1453: "Nonstop snow damaged wheat crops"
  - "Several feet of snow fell in six provinces; tens of thousands of people froze to death"
  - early 1454: "it snowed for 40 days south of the Yangtze River and countless died of cold and famine," with lakes frozen and the Yellow Sea icebound about 20 km from shore.

  S7 is POPULAR and gives no juan or entry citations. S8 repeats it and adds deaths in the icebound Huai valley at the end of 1453. **Neither source is PRIMARY.** The Mingshi/Ming shilu entries need to be pulled directly.
- **Constantinople, spring 1453.** S7 reports a poor garden yield, violent hail and rain, and "flames" on the dome of Hagia Sophia that Pang read as volcanic twilight glow. S8 adds an "unusual fog" on 26 May 1453. Tier this **POPULAR/CLAIMANT**: it is Pang's interpretation of siege narratives (Barbaro, Sphrantzes, Kritoboulos) in support of a Kuwae attribution that has since been abandoned. It does not constrain the source.
- **Europe.** S7 reports narrow 1453–55 rings in oak panels of British portraits and Swedish grain tithes falling "to zero" (POPULAR, uncited).
- **Mexico.** S8 cites Therrell et al. 2004 (*BAMS*, doi:10.1175/BAMS-85-9-1263) for autumn frosts in 1453 recorded in codices, associated with the 1454 "One Rabbit" famine. The SCHOLARLY original was not read.
- **Korea and Japan.** Nothing verified. The Joseon Sillok are online and searchable at https://sillok.history.go.kr, and Danjong's reign covers 1452–1455. No 1452–54 weather entry was retrieved in this budget.
- **Eruption or ashfall eyewitness anywhere, c. 1452.** **No attested record was found.** Neither S8 nor any scholarly source read here names one.
- **Databases not reached:** REACHES (Chinese gazetteer climate records), Euro-Climhist, tambora.org.

## 3. Forward models: hemispheric ratio to latitude

**The ice-core numbers (S2, citing Sigl et al. 2014 and Toohey & Sigl 2017).**

| Event | Greenland (kg/km²) | Antarctica (kg/km²) | Share of burden | S2's reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1453 | 25 | 5 | 83% in NH | "more likely" a NH source |
| 1458 | 35 | 64 | 64% in SH | SH source |

S2 also notes that the true eruption date is the same year as first deposition or the year before, because of transport lag (citing Marshall et al. 2019).

**A classification tension worth recording as contested.** S2 restates Sigl 2013's description of the 1453 event as "low latitudes… comparable to Krakatau 1883," and then argues for an NH source. eVolv2k and Toohey 2019 (S3) classify it as *tropical*, because a bipolar signal is read as tropical by convention. The ice record therefore supports "NH-biased, probably low-latitude NH." It does not support a precise latitude.

**How tight is the constraint? Not very.** The key results come from the UM-UKCA perturbed-parameter ensemble emulator (S4):
- **Tropical eruptions deposit more on Greenland than on Antarctica even with no hemispheric bias** (also found by Toohey, Krüger & Timmreck 2013). January tropical eruptions favour Greenland; July eruptions favour Antarctica. A Greenland/Antarctica ratio of about 5 can therefore come from a tropical January eruption.
- In S4's examples, the 44 BCE and 266 CE events have ratios of 6.5 and 5.4. Plausible January solutions for them lie **in the tropics**, and July solutions shift to the **NH mid-latitudes**. Season trades off directly against latitude.
- Model noise is about 10 kg/km² (1 SD) for Greenland and 2.5 kg/km² for Antarctica. S4 sets detection thresholds near 20 and 5 kg/km². **1453's Antarctic 5 kg/km² sits at that threshold, and its Greenland 25 kg/km² sits just above its own.** The ratio is therefore poorly determined.
- Injection height is never constrained. S4 says "a wide range of eruptions" of different season, SO2, latitude and height can match a given pair of ice-sheet depositions.
- S4 did not constrain 1453; its eight test events do not include it. That gap is a concrete, cheap next step.

S5 adds that latitude mainly controls aerosol e-folding time, with longer lifetimes at low latitude. That makes post-eruption cooling duration a weak second constraint on latitude.

**Tools and code:**
- **Marshall et al. emulator (interactive):** https://cemac.github.io/volcanic-forcing-deposition. Enter a Greenland and Antarctic deposition range; it returns the plausible latitude, SO2, height and season. Simulation summary data: https://catalogue.ceda.ac.uk/uuid/89cb57d9191b4b81965acb7f154c66df (both from S4).
- **EVA v1.0** (S6) is a Fortran module that turns eruption latitude, sulfur mass and date into aerosol optical properties. Code is in the GMD supplement zip (gmd-9-4049-2016-supplement.zip) at the S6 page; I did not open the zip. EVA prescribes how a source latitude distributes the aerosol between hemispheres, but it is a forward forcing generator, not a deposition model. It cannot be inverted for latitude without an extra deposition assumption.
- **Toohey, Krüger & Timmreck 2013** (*JGR*, "Volcanic sulfate deposition to Greenland and Antarctica: a modeling sensitivity study") is the MAECHAM5-HAM basis for ratio arguments. **Toohey et al. 2016** constrained the 536/540 eruptions using the deposition *ratio* (both cited in S4; not read).

**Bottom line on latitude.** A Greenland/Antarctica ratio of about 5 rules out a mid- or high-latitude SH source. It is compatible with anything from roughly the equator, for a winter (NH) eruption, to the NH mid-latitudes, for a summer eruption. On the emulator evidence, a "NH low latitudes" band of about 0–30°N is the honest reading, and tropical-SH January eruptions are probably not excluded. Season is the missing variable. The documentary first-appearance of effects could pin it: Ming snow in "spring 1453," if genuine, would favour a 1452 eruption.

## What a location constraint from this slice would have to do

It would have to run the 1453 depositions (Greenland 25, Antarctica 5 kg/km², with ice-core spread) through the S4 emulator for both seasons. It would then need to fix the season independently, from the documentary onset or from sub-annual first-deposition timing in NEEM, WAIS Divide and Tunu. Only after that should any candidate volcano be scored against the resulting latitude band.
