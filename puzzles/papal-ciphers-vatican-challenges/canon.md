# Canon — what we believe now

## The object
- Barb.lat.6960 is on DigiVatLib, 194 canvases, black-and-white microfilm images at ~700 px per page. **established** (`analysis/01-download/README.md`)
- DECODE lists 28 letter records plus one volume record for Barb.lat.6960, author Giovanni Battista Pallotto, 1629, Vienna (the first six "Tessalonica", his titular see), all "Non-decrypted". **established** (DECODE listing cache; see `sources/PROVENANCE.md`)
- Image 0005 is a cipher letter headed "Di Vienna 4 di Agosto", addressed to Cardinal Barberini, in continuous digits with no interlinear decipherment. Images 0005–0006 show no decipherment on the same pages. **established** (images read 2026-10-16)

## The text
- Kiewning, *Nuntiaturberichte aus Deutschland* IV/2 (1897), edits or summarizes Pallotto's 1629 dispatches, including the cipher ones ("dechiffr." with the date of decipherment in Rome). His source for them is the Secretariat's deciphered copies, Arch. Vat. Nunz. di Germ. 118–119. **established** (OCR text, e.g. Nr. 6 "dechiffr. 25. Januar", Arch. Vatic. Nunz. di Germ. 118 fol. 25–29; Nr. 153 Germ. 119 ff. 11–19)
- Kiewning prints Nr. 153 (Pallotto, 4 Aug 1629, dechiffr. 22 Aug) in Italian, opening "Hö rieevuta la risposta datami in scritta…" (OCR; read "Ho ricevuta la risposta"). **established**
- The content of the Barb.lat.6960 cipher letters is therefore very likely already known through the Rome decipherments. **likely** (one letter matched; see H2)

## Dating, provenance, authorship
- Kiewning also cites Barberini library volumes (e.g. "Bibl. Barb. LXX 48", "LXX 62") in vol. 2, so in 1897 he used at least part of the Barberini holdings, unlike his 1628 volume, which says the Barberini archive was not accessible. **likely** (OCR lines 5550–5551, 11690; Klausis comment #6 for vol. 1, POPULAR)

## Structure and statistics (from `analysis/`, never from impression)
- Under Tomokiyo's 1628 key, 19 of 22 groups of image 0005 line 1 are in the key, and the decode aligns with Kiewning's Nr. 153 opening position by position, with two mismatching groups. One reader. **likely** (`analysis/02-key-check/`)

## Prior scholarship: settled points
- Vatican Challenge Part 1 (Barb.lat.6956, Pallotto 1628) was solved by Bosbach, Biermann and Tomokiyo; the key uses 1 and 8 as one-digit word separators, two-digit homophones and syllables, and three-digit codes. **established** (Tomokiyo, cryptiana vatican.htm, SECONDARY)
