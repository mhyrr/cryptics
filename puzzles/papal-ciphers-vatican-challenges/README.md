# Papal ciphers in the Vatican archives — dive

**Catalog entry:** `catalog/entries/papal-ciphers-vatican-challenges.md`
**Status:** opened 2026-10-16 · first target Barb.lat.6960 (Pallotto, Vienna, 1629): the 1628 key probably reads it, and its content is already printed by Kiewning (1897) from the Rome decipherments.

## Brief
The papal Secretariat of State's cipher correspondence with its nuncios survives in
the Vatican Library (Barberini volumes, free on DigiVatLib) and the Apostolic Archive
(images through DECODE only). Much of it is read. This dive asks which parts are
genuinely unread, and reads them. The first target is Barb.lat.6960, 28 cipher
letters from the nuncio Pallotto in Vienna, 1629, all marked "non-decrypted" in DECODE.

## What a solution would have to do
1. Name the key for each letter read, and read every letter assigned to it as continuous period Italian under one parsing rule.
2. Where a contemporary decipherment or a printed edition exists (Kiewning 1895/1897; Becker 2009), match it. A mismatch is a finding about the transcription or the key, and is recorded.
3. Confirm each code-group identification on at least two occurrences or against a glossed leaf.
4. Claim a reading as new only after checking it against Kiewning, Becker, DECODE, the DECRYPT papers and Tomokiyo, and say which were checked.
5. Every digit transcription has two independent readers before a reading is called established.

## Scope of this dive
In: Barb.lat.6960 and its relation to Barb.lat.6956 and Nunz. di Germ. 118–119.
Out for now: the AAV nunciature records of 1573–81 that bear on es. 132 (Spagna 22,
France 283C, Spain 7/10, Portugal 8). They need DECODE images and belong to a later session.
