## The prompt, and what it actually showed

Nate's post ("I went looking for lost cities with Claude", *bakerbench*, 11 Oct 2026,
CLAIMANT) reports ~30 mounds in 2013 NASA G-LiHT lidar on Mexico's Gulf coast and
9 Arizona ruins not in any published source. Its precedent, Valeriana (Auld-Thomas
et al., *Antiquity* 2024, SCHOLARLY), is real. Both of Nate's finds are **reported,
not established**: no INAH reply, and Arizona's site file is closed, so "unmapped"
means "not in anything public". The Reddit "planet" (TIC 4206066, 3.18 d, ~1.4 R⊕)
is also reported, not established. Its finder pre-registered predictions, and a
TESS follow-up is reportedly scheduled for 31 Oct – 26 Nov 2026. That is the
right shape.

## The finding: the verifier is the scarce thing, again

Eight domain surveys, 77 candidates. The pattern is the one `ideas/pooled-compute.md`
found for ciphers:

- **Data is almost never the bottleneck.** In nearly every domain, decades of
  public data sit in S3 buckets. Compute is cheap: the top candidates need CPU, not GPUs.
- **The gap is real but narrow.** Most obvious ideas are `CROWDED`: Amazon lidar
  (2,500+ teams), dark vessels, methane plumes, TESS, Erdős problems. The open ones
  are crossings nobody made: a dataset collected for X joined to a registry kept for Y.
- **What separates a find from a blog post is a cheap, independent check.** The
  strong candidates have one built in: an orbit fit, a satellite before/after
  image, a state well registry, a recomputation. The weak ones end in "an expert
  would have to look," or in a closed registry (US/Mexican/Brazilian archaeological
  site files).

So the ranking weights `verifier` double. The best bets are where **the check is
as cheap as the search**.

## Where the user's own suggestions landed

- **Archaeology lidar**: real, but the verifier is closed (state archaeologists,
  INAH) and the looting risk is real. Rank 43 for US 3DEP, 49 for G-LiHT. The best
  open gap is HEXAGON spy imagery (rank 41): ~670k scenes still being ingested, and
  the cost is georeferencing labour, not compute.
- **ELINT**: the strict sense (radar profiling, decoding links, tracking people) is
  out on ethics and law. The defensible core is geophysics, and it produced rank #1.
- **Consumer data**: complaint databases have precedent (GM ignition switch), but
  only an agency can confirm a signal. FAERS is a warning: one journal stopped
  accepting disproportionality papers after a flood of them in 2024.
- **Fan fiction**: last of 77. AO3 forbids scraping, there is no ground truth for
  trope diffusion, and the only "discoverable" fact (authorship) is excluded,
  because it unmasks pseudonymous people.

## Ethics protocol (applies to every pilot)

1. No coordinates of archaeological finds, wrecks or wells in public files. Send
   them privately to the authority that can act: a state archaeologist/SHPO, INAH,
   a state oil-and-gas regulator, or the Minor Planet Center.
2. No candidates whose payoff is identifying private individuals (authorship
   deanonymization, lottery winners, census enumerators, private aircraft).
3. No defeating security (redaction recovery, ELINT, classified satellites).
4. Signals about companies go to the regulator, not to a blog.
