# Compute modes — what kind of computation each live entry actually needs

The `compute` axis in `rubric/RUBRIC.md` is double-weighted in the **attackable**
ranking, but it lumps together jobs that have nothing in common operationally: a
key search, a corpus sweep, a synchrotron scan, and a stylometric test all score
4 or 5. This file splits that axis. The question it answers is narrow and
specific: **which entries are brute-forceable the way the recent AI math results
were** — generate candidates massively, verify each one cheaply and
mechanically, steer by a partial-credit signal — and which entries mean
something else entirely by "compute."

Classification is from each entry's *What is unsolved*, *What survives*, *What a
solution would have to do* and *Why the scores* sections, in the attackable order
of `RANKINGS.md`. 89 live entries (solved / likely-hoax / likely-noise excluded).

## The seven modes

Each entry gets the one mode that best describes the *primary* undone work.

| mode | meaning |
|---|---|
| **ENUMERATE** | Finite or structured hypothesis space — keys, cipher systems, grid rules, segmentations, sign-value assignments, alignments — with a mechanical check. Classic brute force. |
| **SAMPLE-VERIFY** | Hypothesis space is open, but candidates can be generated by a model and checked cheaply against hard constraints: decrypts continuously, matches a source verbatim, regenerates a table, produces well-formed text under a stated mapping. The Navier–Stokes / AlphaProof pattern. |
| **SWEEP** | Mass source-parallel search over digitized corpora — embedding or n-gram search for the passages, quotations, formulae or parallels a text is built from. |
| **IMAGE** | The bottleneck is physical: imaging, virtual unwrapping, RTI, multispectral capture, HTR or transcription of an unread or untranscribed witness. The first deliverable is a readable text, not a reading. |
| **ATTRIBUTE** | Authorship, stylometry, hand identification or dating against comparison corpora. |
| **DESCRIBE** | Statistical characterization that narrows hypothesis families without decoding anything — entropy, morphology, rhyme profile, fingerprinting, null models. |
| **JUDGE** | Humanistic interpretation. Computation is a minor aid at best. |

## The other fields

- **verifier** — what would recognize a correct answer. `MECHANICAL` (a program
  says yes/no: a key decrypts, a rule regenerates a table, a source matches word
  for word), `STRONG` (predicts unread portions, or explains several independent
  anomalies at once), `CONSENSUS` (experts would have to agree), `NONE` (any
  reading is as good as another). This tracks the entry's own `verifiable` score,
  raised to MECHANICAL where the entry names a program-checkable full test.
- **space** — `ENUMERABLE` (can be exhausted: a finite key family, a fixed corpus,
  a finite set of witnesses or grids), `SAMPLABLE` (infinite, but a model can
  propose plausible candidates and a check exists), `OPEN` (no way to know when
  you are done).
- **signal** — does a partial solution give a gradient to steer search?
  `YES` / `WEAK` / `NO`.
- **fit** — `brute_force_fit`. `HIGH`: pooled LLM tokens in a generate-and-verify
  loop could plausibly move it. `MEDIUM`: one leg is weak — the object text needs
  OCR, or the source corpus is dirty, or the check is strong but not mechanical.
  `LOW`: tokens alone do nothing until a coordinator does imaging, transcription,
  archival or laboratory work first, or no verifier exists at all.

One note on `fit` and data: OCR of a public-domain *printed* edition is treated as
a job the token loop can do for itself, so it does not by itself force a LOW.
Imaging an unphotographed manuscript, RTI on a worn stone, or transcribing an
undeciphered sign sequence by hand does force a LOW.

## Where the attackable ranking misleads

### High in the ranking, but tokens alone do nothing

Seven of the top 25 by attackable score are LOW fit. In every case the `compute`
score is honest — real computation is the next move — but the computation is
imaging, HTR or fieldwork, and it has to be commissioned by somebody with access
to an object before any amount of language-model output is worth anything.

- **#1 [Herculaneum papyri](../catalog/entries/herculaneum-papyri.md)** — the calibration anchor for `compute = 5`, and the work is synchrotron beamtime plus segmentation and ink detection on volumetric data. No text to reason over until the scan exists.
- **#5 [Egyptian enigmatic writing](../catalog/entries/egyptian-enigmatic-writing.md)** — the entry says it plainly: the enigmatic spellings exist only as print transliterations, so the first year of any project is encoding, not searching.
- **#8 [Khunrath, Amphitheatrum](../catalog/entries/khunrath-amphitheatrum.md)** — the plates have never been imaged at a resolution that resolves the lettering; the deliverable is a transcription that does not exist.
- **#14 [Clavis Artis](../catalog/entries/clavis-artis.md)** — the German text has never been transcribed and most copies are not photographed; HTR on early-modern cursive is the whole job.
- **#17 [Rohonc Codex](../catalog/entries/rohonc-codex.md)** — the sharpest case: the method is settled and the held-out test is clean, but there is no confirmed machine-readable sign sequence and no complete high-resolution facsimile. Gated on transcription, not on cryptanalysis.
- **#19 [Toynbee tiles](../catalog/entries/toynbee-tiles.md)** — separating hands is letterform and linoleum metrics on photographs; naming the tiler requires physical evidence or an acknowledgement.
- **#24 [Derveni Papyrus](../catalog/entries/derveni-papyrus.md)** — the same problem the Vesuvius Challenge solved, on an object that has not been volumetrically scanned and whose images are not released.

### Low in the ranking, but genuinely brute-forceable

Three entries ranked below 40 are HIGH fit. Each is depressed on the composite by
`mystery` or `solvable` — that is, by how much anyone would care, or by the odds
that there is anything there — not by how attackable it is.

- **#44 [Virgilius Maro Grammaticus](../catalog/entries/virgilius-maro-grammaticus.md)** — the author states the scinderatio rules; implement them, run them backwards over the whole corpus, and measure whether they discriminate marked from unmarked passages against a null model. A mechanical test with a publishable negative, and the entry says a positive would make `compute` a 5.
- **#55 [Nostradamus, Les Propheties](../catalog/entries/nostradamus-propheties.md)** — `mystery 2` because the prophetic question is closed, which buries a textbook source-alignment sweep: 942 short quatrains against digitized sixteenth-century corpora, with coverage as a running partial-credit score and forgery detection as a by-product.
- **#72 [D'Agapeyeff cipher](../catalog/entries/dagapeyeff-cipher.md)** — `mystery 1` sinks it to the bottom third, but the entry calls it the best pure-search target in the catalog relative to its fame: an exactly transcribed ciphertext, a small enumerable system family, and n-gram scoring for a gradient.

Two near misses worth naming: **#39 [Beale ciphers](../catalog/entries/beale-ciphers.md)** is HIGH fit at `solvable 2` — the search is cheap and the verifier perfect even though the likeliest answer is "hoax" — and **#9 [Abramelin squares](../catalog/entries/book-of-abramelin-squares.md)** sits at rank 9 only because `compute 5` carries it there against `material 3`.
